    Chapter 26 — The Complementarity Tax

    Chapter 7 priced two observers served in sequence, a coarse one and then a fine one. Chapter 25 priced the reset of one stored description against a context $S$ that the eraser holds and the consumer never sees. This chapter joins the two: one description serves two read operators at once, and the question is what the second consumer adds, in bits stored and in ideal reset work. Two ledger rows carry it. Row GO-10 is the complementarity tax. Row GO-11 is the exact conditional region behind the tax. Both rows are class [replicated] today, and every result below carries that class. No theorem of this chapter has a [proved] row.

    The setting: two rank-one reads and the read plane

    The source is $X\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$ on $\mathbb R^d$ with $\Sigma\succ0$ (the papers write $\Sigma_x$). Consumers $A$ and $B$ have linearly independent read directions $u,v$, read operators $P_A=uu^{\mathsf T}$ and $P_B=vv^{\mathsf T}$, and distortions $d_A(x,\hat x)=(u^{\mathsf T}(x-\hat x))^2$, $d_B(x,\hat x)=(v^{\mathsf T}(x-\hat x))^2$. Write $B=[u\ \ v]$ and call $Y=B^{\mathsf T}X$ the read plane: it is all that the two consumers can be judged on. Its covariance is $\Sigma_Y=B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma B$, and

    $$ \kappa=\det(B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma B)=\sigma_u^2\,\sigma_v^2\,(1-\rho_{AB}^2), $$

    where $\sigma_u^2=u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma u$, $\sigma_v^2=v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v$ and $\rho_{AB}$ is the correlation of the two reads under the source. $\kappa$ tends to zero as the reads align.

    The costs are the two coordinates of Chapter 25: a description $(\hat X_A,\hat X_B)$ has rate $R\ge I(X;\hat X_A,\hat X_B)$ and reset content $L\ge I(X;\hat X_A,\hat X_B\mid S)$, with ideal isothermal reset work $W^\star=k_{\mathrm B}T\ln 2\cdot L$ ($L$ in bits), for $S$ jointly Gaussian with $X$ and the chain $(\hat X_A,\hat X_B) - X - S$. The conditional coordinate is not new: conditional leakage comes from secure source coding (Villard and Piantanida 2013, Ekrem and Ulukus 2013; Chapter 24). The multi-consumer region that supplies these coordinates is proved in its source paper for finite alphabets and bounded distortion; its Gaussian use here is an extension that the note flags and does not re-prove (VI-10).

    With $R_{AB}$, $L_{AB}$ the joint optima and $R_A,R_B,L_A,L_B$ the single-consumer ones, the tax is the excess of joint service over the more expensive consumer alone:

    $$ \mathrm{CT}_R=R_{AB}-\max(R_A,R_B),\qquad \mathrm{CT}_W=L_{AB}-\max(L_A,L_B). $$

    The rate floor and when it is exact

    For any description meeting $\mathbb E\,d_A\le D_A$ and $\mathbb E\,d_B\le D_B$,

    $$ I(X;\hat X_A,\hat X_B)\ \ge\ \tfrac12\log_2\frac{\kappa}{D_AD_B}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad D_A\,D_B\ \ge\ \kappa\,2^{-2R}. $$

    The floor is exactly the joint rate-distortion function if and only if $\mathrm{diag}(D_A,D_B)\preceq B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma B$ (for $0[replicated], ledger row GO-10; the information-level statements are netted by the sealed harness 055: all 7 gates passed, the floors never violated on 8000 channels, exactness switching exactly at the regime, the discount identity below to $4.3\times10^{-11}$.]

    The first draft stated the regime wrongly for the isotropic case ($\Sigma=\sigma^2I$, unit reads at angle $\theta$, $D_A=D_B=D$). It read $D\le\sigma^2(1-\cos\theta)$, which is false for obtuse angles. A fresh-context derivation pass (VI-10, 2026-08-03) ran the counterexample $\theta=120^\circ$, $D=0.7$: the printed condition holds, yet the max-determinant program exceeds the floor by $0.061$ bits. The correct condition is $D\le\sigma^2(1-\lvert\cos\theta\rvert)$. The error was caught before the note asserted it, the matrix statement survived a 135-instance net with no mismatch, and the obtuse probe is a gate of 055. The same pass recorded five sharpenings. The recorded content hash of registration 055 matches no stored version of its file; the file is unchanged since its seal commit and the cause cannot be shown (VI-25, disclosed in prereg/SEAL-DISCLOSURES.json).

    The work floor and the discount

    Put $\Sigma_{X\mid S}=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Cov}(X\mid S)]$ and $\kappa_S=\det(B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{X\mid S}B)$. For $(X,S)$ jointly Gaussian and any description meeting the two distortion constraints,

    $$ I(X;\hat X_A,\hat X_B\mid S)\ \ge\ \tfrac12\log_2\frac{\kappa_S}{D_AD_B}, \qquad W^\star\ \ge\ k_{\mathrm B}T\ln 2\cdot\tfrac12\log_2^{+}\frac{\kappa_S}{D_AD_B}. $$

    The inequality needs no Markov hypothesis (a strengthening found by VI-10); the chain scopes the model in which the left side is the reset coordinate. The two floors differ by exactly

    $$ \tfrac12\log_2\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_S}=I(u^{\mathsf T}X,\,v^{\mathsf T}X;\,S), $$

    the information the reset context holds about the read plane. So $\kappa_S\le\kappa$ always, and a context independent of the read plane discounts nothing. [[replicated], ledger row GO-10, registration 055.] Unlike the rate floor, the work floor is generally not attained at $D>0$. Row GO-11 says exactly when it is.

    A worked instance sets the operational targets: $\Sigma=I_2$, orthogonal reads, equal distortion $D$, and $S=u^{\mathsf T}X+N$ with $s^2=\operatorname{Var}(X_1\mid S)$. Then $\mathrm{CT}_W=\tfrac12\log_2\bigl((s^2+(1-s^2)D)/D\bigr)$ and

    $$ \mathrm{CT}_R-\mathrm{CT}_W=\tfrac12\log_2\frac{1}{s^2+(1-s^2)D}, $$

    which is zero for a useless context ($s^2=1$) and rises to $I(X_1;S)$ as $D\to0$.

    What is prior work and what the record found no prior for

    The novelty record (paper/complementarity-tax-NOVELTY.md, four independent searches, 2026-08-03) rescoped the note. After the reduction to the read plane, the rate floor is the Gaussian evaluation of Gray’s 1973 conditional rate-distortion lower bound, and the exactness regime is the low-distortion branch of the bivariate individual-distortion problem: Xiao and Luo 2005, Lapidoth and Tinguely 2010, and the semidefinite-condition literature (Stylianou and coauthors 2021, Chen and coauthors 2026). The book attributes both and keeps the proofs for completeness. The Xiao and Luo paper was checked through two restatements, not at the source. The record found no prior, under its recorded queries, for the tax quantity, for the packaging of two rank-one reads through $\kappa$, and for the whole work side (the conditional floor, the discount identity, the tax gap). That is absence under those queries, not a proof of novelty. One literature chore is open: the full text of Kastner and Schlatter 2024, which could not be retrieved.

    GO-11: the exact conditional function for one rank-one read

    Take one consumer with read $Y=w^{\mathsf T}X$, normalized to unit variance, and a context $S=V+U$, where $V$ is a unit-variance linear function of $X$ with $\rho=\mathbb E[YV]$, $\lvert\rho\rvert<1$, and $U\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau^2)$ is independent noise. $V$ is the part of the context the encoder can compute. Theorem 1 (pair sufficiency): the channel may depend on $X$ only through $(Y,V)$, so in any dimension the problem depends on $(\rho,\tau^2)$ alone. Theorem 2: with $s=1+\tau^2$ (not the $s^2$ of the instance above), for $0

    $$ L(D)=\tfrac12\log_2 g^\star,\qquad g^\star=\text{the larger root of }\ D\,s\,g^2-(D+s-\rho^2)\,g+(1-\rho^2)=0 . $$

    Since the quadratic equals $(D-1)\tau^2<0$ at $g=1$, exactly one root exceeds $1$. The value reduces to the classical $1/D$ at $\rho=0$, to Gray’s conditional function as $\tau^2\to0$, and to Steinberg’s scalar common-reconstruction corner as $\rho^2\to1$. [[replicated], ledger row GO-11; harness 060 passed 6 of 6, the quadratic against direct optimization to $2.0\times10^{-13}$.] The row calls this the first vector-Gaussian function of common-reconstruction type; the targeted search found none in print. The attribution is recorded with it: the chain-rule identity $I(X;\hat X)-I(X;\hat X\mid S)=I(\hat X;S)$ and the one-constraint region are Paper V’s or folklore, and the scalar single-distortion instance of the frontier is prior work (Wang and coauthors; Günlü and coauthors for the conditional coordinate under degradedness).

    The drafting record carries two catches (verification addenda 1 to 8 of paper/go11-conditional-region-NOVELTY.md). The draft stated a cubic; the fresh-context pass showed one factor spurious, reduced it to the quadratic, proved uniqueness and closed the converse analytically. The draft also claimed that Theorem 2 settled the whole frontier. That was an overclaim, and correcting it produced the next result.

    The frontier: two water levels and two corners

    Theorem 3 gives the whole rate-work region for $\rho^2\in(0,1)$, $\tau^2>0$, $0

    $$ a=1-\alpha\gamma_0-(1-\alpha)\gamma_1,\qquad m=\frac{a\rho}{s-(1-\alpha)\gamma_1},\qquad b=(1-\alpha)\gamma_1 m, $$

    $$ n=D-h(a,b),\qquad \gamma_0=\frac{n}{Q_0+n},\qquad \gamma_1=\frac{n}{Q_1+n}, $$

    with $h=(1-a)^2-2(1-a)b\rho+b^2$, $Q_0=a^2+b^2+2ab\rho$ and $Q_1=Q_0-(a\rho+b)^2/s$. The frontier point is $(R,L)=\bigl(\tfrac12\log_2(1/\gamma_0),\ \tfrac12\log_2(1/\gamma_1)\bigr)$: two water levels, one per coordinate. The rate-optimal channel has $b=0$ and the work-optimal one has $b\ne0$, so whenever the context is misaligned, $\rho^2\in(0,1)$, no channel attains both corners. At $(\rho^2,\tau^2,D)=(0.75,0.5,0.3)$ the two excesses are $0.0400$ and $0.0349$ bits. The single-corner region of Paper V’s scalar case (Chapter 25) is exactly the alignment degeneracy ($\rho^2\to1$, or $\rho=0$). [[replicated], ledger row GO-11, harness 060: corner excesses exact.]

    Two consumers, and the correction of Conjecture 3

    For two rank-one consumers and a scalar context, Theorems 4 and 5 reduce the problem to the triple $T=(Y_A,Y_B,V)$ and show that Gaussian channels $\hat{\mathbf Y}=AT+N$ exhaust the region, a nine-parameter matrix program. The conjectured scalar water-filling for two consumers was withdrawn: none emerged. From the program the GO-10 gap formula above is derived, not assumed (060: anchors to 4 decimals).

    Conjecture 3 guessed that the work floor is attained when $\Delta=\mathrm{diag}(D_A,D_B)\preceq\Sigma_{Y\mid S}$. Theorem 6 corrects it. With $\beta=\operatorname{Cov}(Y,V)$ and $\Sigma_{Y\mid V}=\Sigma_Y-\beta\beta^{\mathsf T}$, the floor $\tfrac12\log_2(\det\Sigma_{Y\mid S}/\det\Delta)$ is attained if and only if

    $$ \text{(a)}\ \ \tau^2=0\ \text{ and }\ \Delta\preceq\Sigma_{Y\mid V}, \qquad\text{or}\qquad \text{(b)}\ \ \beta=0\ \text{ and }\ \Delta\preceq\Sigma_Y . $$

    The context must be computable from $X$ and satisfy the semidefinite condition, or be blind to the read plane. The matrix that matters is the encoder-accessible $\Sigma_{Y\mid V}$, not $\Sigma_{Y\mid S}$. In every other case the floor is strict, with a deficit that tends to zero as $\Delta\to0$. [[replicated], ledger row GO-11.]

    Theorems 7 to 10

    • Theorem 7 (vector context, one rank-one read). After whitening ($\Sigma_T\to I$, $\Sigma_{T\mid S}\to\Lambda$, read direction $y_0$), the frontier is the generalized two-water-level system $u=\bigl(1-\alpha\gamma_0-(1-\alpha)\gamma_1\bigr)\bigl[(1-(1-\alpha)\gamma_1)I+(1-\alpha)\gamma_1\Lambda\bigr]^{-1}y_0$, $n=D-\lvert y_0-u\rvert^2$.
    • Theorem 8 (higher-rank reads). The region is a matrix program. It decomposes exactly into per-mode Theorem 7 systems when $\Sigma_T$ and $\Sigma_{T\mid S}$ are simultaneously block-diagonal. A companion proposition proves the minimizer unique at every interior $\alpha$ by matrix convexity, which retires a hedge in Theorem 3.
    • Theorem 9 (the frontier system for two consumers). The water levels become $2\times2$ matrices $M_0,M_1$. With $w\in[0,1]$ the weight on rate, $\Sigma_N$ the channel noise covariance and $\mu$ the distortion multipliers, every Pareto-optimal channel satisfies $\Sigma_N^{-1}=wM_0^{-1}+(1-w)M_1^{-1}+\mathrm{diag}(\mu)$, off-diagonal entry included, with $\mu_i=1/D_i$ at $w=1$ and no symmetry assumption. The frontier is contained in the solution set; the theorem does not claim the converse.
    • Theorem 10 (binary pair). For a doubly symmetric binary pair with $\Pr[Y\ne V]=p$ and $S=V\oplus\mathrm{Bern}(q)$, the conditional function is $L(D)=h_2(u)-(1-p)h_2(d_0)-p\,h_2(d_1)$, $u=a\ast q$, $a=(1-p)d_0+p(1-d_1)$, where $(d_0,d_1)$ solves $(1-p)d_0+p\,d_1=D$ and the tilt equation $\ell(d_0)-\ell(d_1)=2(1-2q)\,\ell(u)$, $\ell(x)=\ln\frac{1-x}{x}$. The discount is exactly $1-h_2(u)$.

    All four are [replicated] under ledger row GO-11. Harness 064 passed 6 of 6 on Theorems 9 and 10 (the off-diagonal identity to $1.3\times10^{-7}$, the binary certificates to $3.4\times10^{-14}$). Harness 063 covers Theorems 7, 8 and uniqueness; its pass (6 of 6) is recorded in claims/REGISTRY-ACCOUNTING.md, while the ledger row’s result cell lists no outcome for it.

Theorem 10 later derived the noisy-context discount that 062 had reported without a formula: $1-h_2(1/6)=0.3500$ against $0.3432$ measured.
