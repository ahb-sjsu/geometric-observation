    Appendix B — The COST Ledger: Theorems, Proofs, and Falsification Harnesses

    Staging tier [B]. This appendix carries the statements and proof status of the COST-face theorems (Paper III).

    What [proved] requires. Under the charter’s theorem-custody rule (T-CUST-1), a theorem reaches [proved] only when it ships four things: 1. a written proof; 2. an R-IND-5 fresh-context adversarial pass; 3. a committed C3 numerical falsification harness, built to fail if the theorem is false; 4. a constants/slack and known-gaps table.

    A green harness is a net, not a proof (T-CUST-2). Full proofs live in Paper III and its appendix. This ledger gives each statement, its status, its custody, and the verification incidents it survived.

    Conventions

    • Source $x$ with covariance $\Sigma_x$.
    • Observer read metric $P$ (dropping the subscript $C$).
    • Coding error $\delta$, with covariance $\Sigma_\delta$.
    • Read distortion $D = \operatorname{tr}(P\Sigma_\delta)$.
    • The whitened basis is $\Sigma_x^{1/2} P \Sigma_x^{1/2}$. Several constants are correct only in this basis, the recurring lesson of VI-4 and VI-6.

    Thm B1 — Consumer rate–distortion achievability & converse

    Statement. For a Gaussian source and read metric $P$, the minimum rate to achieve read distortion $D$ is $R_C(D)$. It is achieved by a test channel that does reverse water-filling in the whitened basis, on the tilted source $Y = P^{1/2}X$ (Paper III prop:rd, lem:tilt, thm:coding, cor:coding).

    (Paper III’s own thm:B1 is a different result: the coding theorem for the true divergence. An earlier sentence here, “the reduction in true divergence … is the tilt”, matched no statement and is withdrawn.)

    Status. - R-IND-5: VI-3. - The single-letter Gaussian identity behind it, lem:maxdet’s rate identity, is [proved] (ledger row PAPER-III.a) since 2026-11-21. thm:coding, the operational theorem, still has no net. - thm:coding has no C3 net of its own; verify_appendixB.py nets thm:B2/cor:B2a and part of thm:B3. - [exploratory] pending a net and custody.

    Custody / gaps. - Verbatim Kostina–Verdú transfer holds for the fixed-$P$ Gaussian case. - The true nonlinear consumer keeps its “should port” hedge. - Constants hold in the whitened basis only.

    Thm B2 — Output ≤ surrogate ≤ reconstruction at matched rate

    Statement. At matched rate, coding for the consumer’s output metric is no worse than coding for a surrogate read metric, which is no worse than coding for reconstruction, on the consumer metric. This is Paper III’s registered prediction GO-6, not its thm:B2, which is the output-reduction theorem.

    Status. [demonstrated] by the ledger, and disputed (VI-32, VI-34): the findings below mean the harness does not test the claim’s matched-rate premise. A re-run at matched rate is owed, and the class is the author’s to set. What GO-6 (GO-P-2026-028) measured: - the ordering holds at every rate tested; - reconstruction is up to about 500× worse at high rate; - the median gap is $+2.45$; - the isotropic control collapses the three coders.

    What VI-32 found: - No theorem gives the ordering, the “kernel entropy share” (which is undefined) or the rate dependence, so the earlier “the analytic statement is proved” is withdrawn. - The harness charges (b) and (c) a backward-channel rate while measuring their distortion with forward noise, so the rates are not exactly matched. - The harness’s isotropic control assigns all three curves the same array.

    Thm B3 — The surrogate–output gap vanishes with rate

    Statement. The relative gap $(b - a)/(c - a)$ between a surrogate-optimal and an output-optimal code shrinks to zero as the rate grows (GO-6 (iii)). Paper III’s thm:B3 is the high-resolution formula, a different statement.

    Status. [demonstrated] by the ledger, and disputed with B2, since it uses the same harness: GO-6 measures the gap falling from $0.41$ to $0.005$. There is no analytic theorem; VI-3 graded the high-resolution argument PLAUSIBLE-with-conditions. Its Linder–Zamir conditions are cited, not checked, and the formula also needs $\mathbb E\log\det G<\infty$.

    Consequence. At high resolution a surrogate for $P$ suffices; at low resolution it does not. This is why the blind probe (Chapter 10) is load-bearing precisely in the bit-starved regime.

    Thm B4 — Two-observer region & refinability

    Statement. The achievable rate region for two observers $(P_1, P_2)$ is contained in the max-determinant region of Paper III (thm:region), with $0\prec\Sigma_f\preceq\Sigma_b\preceq\Sigma_x$, and equals it under Assumption SR.

    If a single scalable code serves both observers at their single-stage optimal rates $R_1(D_1)$ and $R_2(D_2)$ (the corner), the coarse observer with a prefix of the fine observer’s bits, then the observers’ optimal error covariances nest, $\Sigma_2^\star\preceq\Sigma_1^\star$ (thm:multiobs); under Assumption SR nesting also suffices. (Without the corner rates the statement is false: Paper III’s sec:multiobs, consequence (iii), is served by two independent codes at a higher total rate; VI-39, VI-40.) This condition depends on $D_1$ and $D_2$.

    Non-nested optima incur at least the rate loss of thm:multiobs-loss, and exactly that loss under Assumption SR. (Stated unconditionally until 2026-11-21; VI-38.)

    Correction (VI-32). This appendix used to state the criterion as “iff $P_1\preceq P_2$”. That is false in both directions: - not refinable although $P_1\preceq P_2$: $\Sigma_x=I_2$, $P_1=\mathrm{diag}(1,0)$, $P_2=I$, $D_1=0.5$, $D_2=1.2$; - refinable although $P_1\not\preceq P_2$: $P_1=2I$, $P_2=I$, $D_1=1$, $D_2=0.2$.

    The paper always had it right.

    Status. - Proof plus R-IND-5 (VI-4; later VI-32, VI-36 to VI-40, VI-46 and VI-47). - Sealed net 022, but that net is weak. VI-32 re-inserted VI-4’s original bug into verify_two_observer.py, and checks [1]–[3] still pass. Only verify_rate_region.py [3] catches it. - Split (VI-36, corrected by VI-37). verify_rate_region.py [3] computes the closed-form Gaussian corner and takes the region as given, so what it nets is the max-det corner identity (the closed forms of $\Sigma^\star$, $\Sigma^\circ$ and $L$): [proved] since 2026-11-21 (ledger row PAPER-III.a, netted by verify_paper3_nets.py m1–m4 in CI; it was [predicted]-grade pending custody). The coding statements take the region’s class: the necessity of nesting and the rate loss as a lower bound rest on the region converse, which has no net that can fail, and the sufficiency of nesting and the attained minimum rest on Assumption SR (achievability, attributed to Effros, unchecked): [exploratory]. verify_paper3_nets.py nets the closed forms and the corner restriction; it too takes the region as given.

    Verification incident (VI-4, standing). - The bug. An in-context sigma_star formula used $(\Sigma^\star)^{-1} = \Sigma_x^{-1} + \lambda P$, with the inline claim “the cap never binds”. - The catch. A fresh-context adversarial pass found that the max-det error covariance is reverse water-filling in the whitened basis, and that the cap does bind on $\ker P$. - The fix. The formula was corrected and the harness re-run. The analytic theorem statement was unaffected; the harness was wrong. - Later (VI-32). The “decisive” check added then is an algebraic identity that holds for any nested pair, so it cannot detect the bug.

    Thm B5 — Rate region & the $k$-observer chain

    Statement. - Converse and the necessity of nesting: for $k$ observers in a chain, the achievable region is contained in the chain of nested max-det covariances (cor:kobs, with $0\prec\Sigma_k$), and refinability implies that the single-stage optima nest. Paper III proves both, with the $n$-block step added in 2026-11. - Under Assumption SR, for every $k$ (for $k=2$: thm:region, thm:multiobs), the region equals that set and refinability is equivalent to nesting. - Achievability, for every $k$, and with it the sufficiency of nesting: the independent-Fisher-increment Gaussian test channel realizes every feasible chain, but turning it into codes is not proved in Paper III. That step is Paper III’s Assumption SR, attributed to Effros (IEEE T-IT 1999), which Kostina–Tuncel cite for the two-stage region on continuous alphabets; that it covers $k\ge3$ stages is a reading of its title (“multiresolution”), and its hypotheses for this Gaussian case are still to be checked at the source. Every achievability claim here, for $k=2$ too, rests on it. (Checked 2026-11-21, VI-36: Kostina–Tuncel, which Paper III cited as the coding theorem, is a two-stage parametric characterization of the region’s boundary, and itself cites Effros for the continuous-alphabet extension.)

    Status. - $k=2$, the max-det corner identity: proof, R-IND-5 (VI-5), and the sealed net 023, whose check [3] is a real net for it. [proved] since 2026-11-21 (ledger row PAPER-III.a, netted in CI by verify_paper3_nets.py). Refinability as a coding statement is [exploratory], as in B4. - The region converse: proof and R-IND-5, but no net that can fail. 023’s [1] is an identity, and [2] cannot fail on the region’s coupling. [exploratory] until a net exists. - Achievability, for $k=2$ as for $k\ge3$: Assumption SR, unchecked (FRONTIER G12b).

    Verification incident (VI-5). One error was caught before publication, (b) below. A citation was also flagged, but VI-5’s replacement readings proved unchecked or wrong (VI-36 to VI-40), so (a) is not counted as a catch: - (a) the citation: Rimoldi’s is the finite-alphabet $L$-stage result (VI-5’s reading; Kostina–Tuncel quote it for two stages, and the $L$-stage form is not checked at the source; VI-39); - (b) the two-stage K-map does not iterate: an accumulating rotation ambiguity leaks Fisher information by $O(0.1)$ per stage.

    Achievability was restated with independent increments. VI-32 found that the follow-on claim, that the two-stage arguments “extend verbatim”, was itself unproved. It is now withdrawn in Paper III.

    Statement (floor).     Thm B7 — Dispersion counts at most the read dimensions

    Statement. The finite-blocklength consumer coding problem is the $r$-dimensional tilted problem at every blocklength (prop:dispersion, by a covering argument). For the Gaussian quadratic case its dispersion is

    $$V_Y(D)=\tfrac12\Bigl[r_D+\sum_{k:\,0<\gamma_k\le\theta}(\gamma_k/\theta)^2\Bigr]\ \text{nats}^2,\qquad r_D\le 2V_Y\le r,$$

    with $\gamma_k$ the tilted mode variances, $\theta$ the water level, and $r_D$ the active modes (cor:dispersion). Equality holds on the right iff $\gamma_k\ge\theta$ for every $k$, which covers the whole low-distortion regime. It counts at most the $r$ read dimensions, never the ambient $d$. A Monte Carlo at $\gamma=(4,2,0.6,0.3)$, $\theta=1$ gives a variance of 1.226 against the claimed 1.0. The bound survives.
