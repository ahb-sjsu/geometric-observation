    Chapter 7 — COST: What Observation Charges

    This chapter and the two that follow are the thesis chapters of the book (staging tier [B]). Their theorems are stated here with their status; the proofs and the committed falsification harnesses live in Appendix B, and every numbered result resolves to a ledger row.

    If a consumer reads only $P_C$, what does it cost — in bits — to serve it? The answer is a rate–distortion theory in which the fidelity measure is the read distortion, and its entire content is that the optimal bit-allocation tilts toward the read subspace. This is the COST shadow, and it is where Observation Theory reconnects with Shannon: classical rate–distortion is the slice $P_C = I$, and everything below is what happens when the slice is allowed to tilt.

    The consumer rate–distortion function

    Fix a source $x$ with covariance $\Sigma_x$, an observer with read metric $P_C$, and a target read distortion $D$. Define

    $$ R_C(D) = \min_{\,p(\hat x \mid x)\,:\; \operatorname{tr}(P_C\Sigma_\delta) \le D\;} I(x; \hat x), $$

    the fewest bits per symbol needed so that the consumer’s distortion — not the reconstruction distortion — stays within $D$. For a Gaussian source this has a closed form, and the form is the point: the optimal test channel does reverse water-filling in the joint basis that diagonalizes $P_C$ against $\Sigma_x$ (the whitened operator $\Sigma_x^{1/2} P_C \Sigma_x^{1/2}$), pouring bits into directions that are both high-source-variance and high-consumer-sensitivity, and abandoning any direction that either the source does not populate or the consumer does not read. The tilt away from Shannon’s source-only allocation is precisely the factor $P_C$. (An earlier sentence here, that the “true-divergence reduction” relative to reconstruction coding is that tilt, matched no statement in Paper III and is withdrawn; VI-32, VI-39.) [[exploratory] pending a net and custody — Appendix B, B1; tagged [proved] until 2026-11-21, VI-38]

    Two operational consequences frame the rest of the chapter.

    Reconstruction is wasteful by a computable amount. A code that reconstructs $x$ (optimizes $P_C = I$) spends bits on $\ker P_C$ that buy the consumer nothing. GO-6 measures this end to end: at matched rate, output coding $\le$ surrogate $\le$ reconstruction on the consumer metric, the ordering holding at every rate, with reconstruction up to $\sim 500\times$ worse downstream at high rate because its bits went to the unread directions [demonstrated — GO-P-2026-028, all bars pass, nonlinear consumer $d=8, r=4$; disputed: the harness is off the matched-rate premise, re-run owed (VI-32, VI-34)]. The isotropic control $P_C = I$ collapses the three coders, as it must — when the consumer reads everything, there is nothing to save.

    The surrogate–output gap vanishes with rate. GO-6 also supports Thm B3: the relative gap between an output-optimal code and a good surrogate shrinks (measured $0.41 \to 0.005$) as the budget grows [demonstrated, disputed (VI-32, VI-34)]. At high resolution you do not need to know the consumer exactly; a surrogate for $P_C$ suffices. At low resolution you do. This is the rate-dependence that makes the blind probe (Chapter 10) load-bearing precisely in the bit-starved regime where compression matters.

    Two observers: the rate region and refinability

    Serving two consumers from one code is a network-information-theory problem with an exact outer structure. For observers with read metrics $P_1, P_2$, Paper III proves that every two-stage code lies in a max-determinant region, and that the region is tight under a successive-refinement achievability assumption (Assumption SR, attributed to Effros and not yet checked at the source). Its corner decides refinability — whether a single scalable code can serve both observers at their single-stage optimal rates $R_1(D_1)$ and $R_2(D_2)$, the coarse one with a prefix of the bits it serves the fine one. (Without the optimal rates the question is empty: two independent codes always serve both, at a higher total rate; VI-40.) Refinability requires the observers’ optimal error covariances to nest, $\Sigma_2^\star \preceq \Sigma_1^\star$, and under the assumption nesting suffices. Nesting depends on the targets $D_1, D_2$, not only on the read metrics: $P_1 \preceq P_2$ neither implies it nor follows from it (VI-32; Appendix B, B4). Nesting is the precise sense in which one observer is “a refinement of” another: it can be served by adding bits at no rate loss (under Assumption SR), not resending them. When the optima do not nest, serving both costs a rate loss, which the theory bounds below (exactly, under the assumption). [The max-det corner identity [proved], ledger row PAPER-III.a; the region and its coding statements [exploratory] — Appendix B.] (This paragraph said until 2026-11-21 that refinability holds exactly when $P_1 \preceq P_2$, tagged [proved]; that criterion is false both ways, VI-32.)

    This region carries one of the program’s standing verification incidents, and it is worth stating because it is exactly the kind of error the discipline exists to catch. The in-context derivation of the two-observer error covariance used a water-filling formula with an inline claim that a certain cap “never binds”; a fresh-context adversarial pass found the max-determinant error covariance is reverse water-filling in the whitened basis and the cap does bind on $\ker P$ (VI-4). The formula was corrected, the prereg amended and re-sealed, and the false pass logged with the same prominence as a positive. The theorem statements — the abstract max-det program — were unaffected; the harness was wrong and the harness was fixed. This is what checking both the analytic claim and its numerical net buys you: they disagreed, and the disagreement was the alarm. (A later custody pass, VI-32, found that the check added then cannot fail either: re-inserting the original bug still passes it. The corner is now netted by prereg 023’s check [3] and by verify_paper3_nets.py.)

    The $k$-chain and what counts a dimension

    For $k \ge 3$ observers in a chain, Paper III proves the outer bound and that refinability requires the optima to nest; equality, and the sufficiency of nesting, rest on the same achievability assumption. The chain is not a naive iteration of the two-stage map — another caught error (VI-5): the two-stage construction does not iterate, because an accumulating rotation ambiguity leaks Fisher information by $O(0.1)$ per stage; the correct single-letter construction uses independent Fisher increments from physically degraded channels, exact to $10^{-14}$ [corrected; prereg GO-P-2026-023; the coding step is assumed, not proved]. The lesson survives the technicality: a chain of observers is not a stack of two-observer problems, and the geometry that makes it work is the nesting of the observers’ optimal error covariances, not of their read metrics.

    Dispersion — the second-order term governing finite-blocklength performance — counts at most the read dimensions, never the source dimensions: $r_D \le 2V \le r$ in nats$^2$, with $r_D$ the modes above the water level and equality on the right exactly when no read mode lies below it (a mode at the level, $\gamma_k=\theta$, counts fully). Modes below the water level still contribute, $(\gamma_k/\theta)^2/2$ each, because fluctuations of their energy are absorbed by the active ones. As the target distortion falls, more modes become active and $2V$ rises to $r$. [The variance formula [proved], ledger row PAPER-III.b. Reading it as this dispersion also needs the finite-$n$ reduction, [exploratory], and kernel uniqueness, and the expansion is Kostina–Verdú’s, imported — Appendix B.] (This paragraph said until 2026-11-21, following VI-7’s “sharpening”, that the dispersion counts only the active modes, an “effective dimension $r_D$”; that is refuted, NEG-18, Chapter 16.)

    Mismatch: the tax and the floor

    The two results with the most operational bite are about using the wrong read metric — which is what every reconstruction-optimal code does.

    The commission tax. Coding for a read metric $\hat P$ when the consumer’s is $P$, with $m\hat P\preceq P\preceq M\hat P$ on their shared range (Loewner constants $0thm:commission states, and the $r/2$ is sharp — you pay for weighting directions as the consumer does not [[predicted]-grade — Appendix B, B6; prereg GO-P-2026-024]. (Until 2026-11-21 this paragraph gave the metrics ranks $M$ and $m$, confusing the Loewner constants with ranks, and tagged it [proved]; VI-32, VI-38.) This is the rate-domain shadow of “reconstruction is wasteful”: the waste has a closed-form price.

    The omission floor. The dangerous mismatch is the other direction — a code blind to a direction the consumer reads. Then downstream distortion does not merely cost extra bits; it hits a floor it cannot descend below at any rate:

    $$ D \;\ge\; D_{\text{floor}} = \operatorname{tr}(\tilde P \, \Pi), $$

    where $\tilde P$ is the read metric in the whitened basis $\Sigma_x^{-1/2}$ and $\Pi$ projects onto the omitted direction. Two subtleties the discipline forced: the omission gives a floor, a compact distortion band $[D_{\text{floor}}, \operatorname{tr}(P\Sigma_x)]$, not the “unbounded distortion” a first draft claimed; and the floor uses the whitened kernel, which a naive kernel projector overstates by $\sim 30\%$ when $\Sigma_x \ne I$ (VI-6, corrected; [predicted]-grade, prereg GO-P-2026-024, and the floor’s measurement [demonstrated] — Appendix B, B6; tagged [proved] until 2026-11-21, VI-38).

    The omission floor is not only a theorem. It was measured biting a real language model. On trained Llama-3.2-3B read operators, the read-metric floor is reached on 16/16 heads — $\operatorname{tr}(P\Sigma_\delta)$ saturates at the measured whitened-kernel value, while a naive $\Sigma_x = I$ assumption is off by 20–60% — and, crucially, a misidentified coder plateaus at a softmax divergence $\sim 10^5\times$ the matched operator’s (median $0.10$ nats versus $\sim 6\times10^{-7}$) [demonstrated — GO-P-2026-027, sealed before the run on held-out layers {4,20}]. This row has a history worth carrying: the first sealed finite-rate gate for it missed (0/16), because softmax-KL was still improving at the top swept rate — a shallow-sweep artifact that conflated floor-reached with floor-exists (NEG-13). The gate flaw was logged, and the claim was resolved not by relaxing the sealed bar but by a fresh, held-out test that passed. The floor is real; the first instrument for seeing it was too shallow, and the ledger says both.

    A second party: the rate–leakage–distortion series

    The theorems above price one consumer and no one else. A companion series of four papers for the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory (the RLD series: papers I and II submitted, paper II on 2026-11-18, III and IV in preparation; Chapter 24 treats the series in full) adds a second party, a holder that observes the source through its own noisy map and learns from the description without decoding it, and charges a second cost beside the rate: the leakage, the conditional information $I(X;\hat X\mid S)$ the description gives the holder beyond its own observation. For a fixed per-symbol weight $Q$ the series finds the region of pairs of rate and leakage:

    • I — Gaussian vectors: every frontier point is reverse water-filling on the weight tilted by the holder’s information, and the optimal directions turn with the budget.
    • II — stationary Gaussian processes: the same frequency by frequency under one level, and the spectral region is the operational one, for holders whose observation is filtered (with finite or infinite memory), delayed, or the whole observation process.
    • III — slowly varying sources (water-filling over time and frequency), cyclostationary sources, scalar unstable sources, and sources that are not Gaussian (the Gaussian region is achievable; every code is bounded below by the Gaussian frontier less two relative entropies). One inequality joins the branches: averaging a covariance, or a spectrum over time or a cycle, never lowers the cost.
    • IV — unstable vector sources: an unstable pole costs the logarithm of its modulus in rate, and again in leakage only if the holder cannot observe it ($\rho_\star$ against $\rho_{\mathrm{hid}}$), for holders with finite or rational memory; poles on the unit circle (scalar) cost nothing; sources whose statistics vary without a period, including the proof that incommensurate sampling removes the dependence on the sampling phase, a conjecture of Tan, Dabora, and Poor; and ergodically driven vector sources.

    Each result has a written proof, fresh-context model audits, and numerical checks whose criteria were fixed before they ran but were not sealed; in this book’s grammar they are [exploratory] until promoted (ledger section RLD).

    The series takes two things as given, and they are the business of other chapters. The weight. The consumer’s own geometry is the local read metric $P_C(x)$; the series’ $Q$ is a fixed input, and the optimal description — an error covariance whose directions turn with the budget — depends on $Q$, the source, the holder, and $D$ together. That turning is the description’s geometry, not the observer’s: it does not mean the consumer’s metric has changed. Replacing $P_C(x)$ by a constant $Q$ is the bridge of Chapter 5, which has conditions and a price. The knowledge of the weight. The series assumes $Q$ known; Chapter 10 recovers the read operator from the consumer, but its recovery result identifies the read subspace, under a specified model, probe distribution, and output metric, and not the weights within it, which an allocation also needs. Composing recovery with the series’ coding theorems, and charging for the probing, is the keystone T1 (Chapter 17).

    And one thing the series establishes is read differently elsewhere. Its leakage is the conditional information $I(X;\hat X\mid S)$; that this quantity is the ideal work to reset a record against the holder’s knowledge is a further claim, which uses the physical memory and reset model of the consumer-relative Landauer paper, in which the downstream consumer and the physical reset agent are different parties. That paper, its coding theorems and its measured results are Chapter 25. Chapter 26 prices one description that serves two read operators, Chapter 27 lets the reset agent’s access to its context vary in time, Chapter 28 makes the second party an adversary, and Chapter 29 carries the read operator into estimation and control.

    COST, in one line

    Observation charges a rate, the rate is $R_C(D)$, and its whole difference from Shannon is a tilt of the bit-allocation toward the read subspace — with a commission tax for protecting too much and an omission floor for protecting too little. The next chapter shows what you buy by paying the tilted price instead of the reconstruction price: the flip.
