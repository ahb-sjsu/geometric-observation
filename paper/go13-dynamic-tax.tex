\begin{abstract}
GO-10 measured the complementarity tax two consumers pay on one static
source; GO-11 solved the exact region behind it, through the $m=2$
frontier system with its $2\times2$ matrix water levels; GO-12 showed
the staleness of the eraser's context is an access-width phenomenon,
reduced entirely to one conditional variance $q_{\mathcal G}$. GO-13
asks the merged question: \emph{what do two consumers pay when the
eraser's context ages?} Nothing here is a claim; the conjectures are
gates for future registrations.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}

Take GO-11's $m=2$ setup verbatim (whitened latent $Z\in\mathbb
R^{2+r}$, reads $y_A,y_B$, context block $V$ now a stationary AR(1)
\emph{process} with observations $S_s=V_s+U_s$, $\mathrm{Var}\,U=
\tau^2$), single-letter two-dimensional records
$\hat{\mathbf Y}=AZ+N$ with per-consumer distortions $D_A,D_B$. The
eraser holds a context $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal G$ from the
observation process (slice, causal prefix, full path --- GO-12's access
family). Coordinates: the joint conditional coordinate
$L_{AB}(\mathcal G)$ (weighted-program value of GO-11 Theorem~9 with
the conditional covariance induced by $\mathcal G$), the single-consumer
coordinates $L_A(\mathcal G),L_B(\mathcal G)$, and the taxes
\[
\mathrm{CT}_R \;=\; R_{AB}-\max(R_A,R_B),\qquad
\mathrm{CT}_W(\mathcal G)\;=\;L_{AB}(\mathcal G)-
\max\bigl(L_A(\mathcal G),L_B(\mathcal G)\bigr).
\]
$\mathrm{CT}_W$ is now a \emph{function of the eraser's access}: the
dynamic complementarity tax.

\section{Conjectures}

\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}
\begin{theorem}[The matrix-$q$ reduction; settled and netted,
GO-P-2026-067]\label{thm:matq}
Hypotheses (all load-bearing, per the R-IND-5 pass): joint
Gaussianity; record noise $N\perp(T,\mathcal G)$; the
conditional-independence embedding $\mathrm{Cov}(T,V_s)=
\mathrm{Cov}(T,V_t)\,a^{|t-s|}$; $\mathcal G$ generated by a nonempty
subset of the observations $\{S_s\}$; weight $w\in[0,1]$. Then for
$m=2$ single-letter records, $\mathcal G$ enters all coordinates only
through $\Sigma_{T\mid\mathcal G}=\Sigma_T-(1-q_{\mathcal G})\,
cc^{\mathsf T}$ ($c=\mathrm{Cov}(T,V_t)$, $q_{\mathcal G}=
\mathrm{Var}(V_t\mid\mathcal G)$): the coordinates are GO-11
Theorem~9's program at $(\Sigma_T,\Sigma_{T\mid\mathcal G})$ ---
GO-12's channel-agnostic sufficiency argument transfers verbatim to
2-D records. Consequently, for \emph{scalar} context every coordinate
and both taxes are functions of $q_{\mathcal G}$ alone
(\textbf{access-class universality}: equal-$q$ classes give identical
taxes --- and only classes at \emph{attainable} common $q$ compare,
each class having its own $q_{\min}$); the coordinates are
nondecreasing in $q$ (Loewner: $\partial_q\Sigma_{T\mid\mathcal G}
=cc^{\mathsf T}\succeq0$, pointwise in every channel, feasible set
$q$-free); and at $q\to1$ they converge to the marginal program
(continuity of the min from concavity of the value in $q$), so
$\mathrm{CT}_W\to\mathrm{CT}_R$. Scalar universality \emph{fails} for
vector context $r\ge2$ (verifier counterexample: equal
$\mathrm{tr}\,\mathrm{Cov}(V\mid\mathcal G)$ with coordinates
differing by $0.12$); the reduction survives only to the matrix
$\mathrm{Cov}(V\mid\mathcal G)$. [R-IND-5: PASS, reduction to
$3.6\times10^{-15}$, universality triples to $7\times10^{-12}$;
governed harness 067 ALL PASS 4/4, seed 20260921.]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The tax-curve characterization; settled and netted,
GO-P-2026-068]\label{thm:taxcurve}
Convention: $q$ increasing means staler access. All of $J$, $L_A$,
$L_B$ are individually nondecreasing in $q$ unconditionally (the
$q$-sensitivity $f_q=(Ac)^{\mathsf T}M_1^{-1}(Ac)\ge0$ pointwise), so
only their difference can decrease. At any $q$ where the optimum is
attained, the solution set is locally bounded, and the
context-coupling sensitivity is constant on the solution set
(uniqueness of the optimal channel suffices, but is not necessary ---
a slack-constraint instance with a non-singleton argmin and constant
sensitivity is on record), Danskin gives
\[
\frac{dJ}{dq}=\frac{1-w}{2\ln2}\,(Ac)^{\mathsf T}M_1^{-1}(Ac),\qquad
\frac{dL_i}{dq}=\frac{1-w}{2\ln2}\,\frac{(u^{\mathsf T}c)^2}{Q_1+n},
\]
at the optimal channels (otherwise only the one-sided derivatives,
$J'(q\pm)=\min/\max$ of the sensitivity over the argmin). Away from
the max-switch locus, with the binding consumer any argmax of
$(L_A,L_B)$ (their sensitivities agreeing at ties),
\[
\frac{d\,\mathrm{CT}_W}{dq}=\frac{1-w}{2\ln2}\Bigl[(Ac)^{\mathsf T}
M_1^{-1}(Ac)-s_{\mathrm{binding}}\Bigr]:
\]
\textbf{staleness raises the tax iff the joint record's context
coupling, in the $M_1^{-1}$ metric, exceeds the binding consumer's.}
At an interior max-switch crossing the curve is continuous with a
kink that is \emph{always downward}: the right derivative subtracts
the \emph{larger} sensitivity (that consumer binds beyond $q^\star$),
the left the smaller, magnitude $\tfrac{1-w}{2\ln2}|s_A-s_B|$ (zero
allowed at sensitivity ties; interior crossings require asymmetric
distortions or variances). At $w=1$ the curve is identically flat,
and $\mathrm{CT}_W\ge0$ everywhere. [R-IND-5: PASS --- 21 derivative
checks to $1.5\times10^{-9}$, both signs realized, the kink's
one-sided slopes to $2.6\times10^{-9}$, the $067$ instance's
reference-dependence confirmed ($+0.177$ max-based vs $-0.0007$
$A$-referenced); governed harness 068.]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:taxcurve} closes the question the 067 pass opened
and explains its instances: I2's increasing tax is a
joint-coupling-dominant regime; the near-invariant curves arise when
the binding consumer carries almost all the context coupling
($s_{\mathrm{binding}}\approx s_J$). Open residue: the global regime
map over $(\rho_{AB},\rho_{AV},\rho_{BV},D_A,D_B,w)$ --- for which the
sign law is now the instrument --- and the operational face
(Question~3).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The regime map --- exploratory, unregistered,
disclosed]
An 18-point envelope sweep (\texttt{go13\_regime\_sweep.py}, seed
20260925; $[$exploratory$]$-grade only): the dynamic tax \emph{rises}
with staleness at 16/18 grid points; decreases are shallow slivers
($|d\mathrm{CT}/dq|<10^{-3}$) occurring only when the binding consumer
is the context-\emph{rich} one; when the binding consumer is
context-poor the rise is strong at 10/10 points, up to $+0.286$
bits/unit-$q$. The candidate iff-law (``rises iff binding is
context-poor'') \emph{fails} at 6/18 points and is recorded as
refuted-as-iff; the surviving asymmetry is the map. The operational
face (draft registration 069) fixes its instances from this sweep:
the grid-maximum rising instance, an equal-$q$ universality control,
and a paired flat contrast.
\end{remark}

\section{Questions}

\begin{theorem}[The spectral $m=2$ theorem; settled and netted,
GO-P-2026-073]\label{thm:specm2}
Circulant embedding (load-bearing: the verifier's Toeplitz control
breaks the mode mapping at $O(0.1)$; $\lambda_k$ are the periodic
AR(1) eigenvalues, mean $1$); CI embedding with temporally
\emph{white} residual coupling (colored residuals change the mapping);
jointly Gaussian linear records with two rows per symbol (hypothesis);
noise $Q=I$ WLOG by scale invariance; bits/symbol, per-symbol mean
distortions; $w\in[0,1]$. Then the DFT modes are independent triples
($S_{A,k}=\rho_{AV}^2\lambda_k+(1-\rho_{AV}^2)$, cross-read covariance
$(\rho_{AB}-\rho_{AV}\rho_{BV})+\rho_{AV}\rho_{BV}\lambda_k$, context
column $\propto\lambda_k$, conditional by the rank-one $S$-mode
correction), cross-mode records gain nothing, and
\[
J_{w,n}(D_A,D_B)=\min_{\substack{\frac1n\sum_kD_{A,k}=D_A\\
\frac1n\sum_kD_{B,k}=D_B}}\ \frac1n\sum_kV^{(9)}_k(D_{A,k},D_{B,k}),
\]
$V^{(9)}_k$ the static weighted matrix-water-level value of GO-11
Theorem~9 at mode $k$ --- \textbf{the static kernel is not ours}: the
rate-face endpoint is Xiao--Luo (Allerton 2005), and the static
tuple's reduction to pairwise subproblems with an implicit
two-water-level system is Stylianou--Charalambous--Charalambous (ISIT
2021/2024); what this theorem adds is the \emph{spectral lift}, the
conditional/weighted face, and the third-party conditioner. At the
allocation optimum the interior-mode partial slopes are equal per
consumer: \textbf{two common prices} $(\mu_A,\mu_B)$, one per
distortion constraint, generically distinct (a measure-zero
coincidence set is possible) --- this is where a ``common pair of
Lagrange prices'' genuinely lives, vindicating GO-12 Conjecture~1's
phrasing at $m=2$ after its $m=1$ refutation. \emph{Scoping}: equal
slopes is KKT \emph{necessity} (interior modes, complementary
slackness for slack modes --- a slack $D_B$ collapses to the $m=1$
one-price structure exactly, verifier-checked to $1.4\times10^{-14}$);
\emph{sufficiency} of the two-price rule needs joint convexity of
$V^{(9)}_k$, unproven while $m=2$ uniqueness is open --- numerically
audited only (minimum FD-Hessian eigenvalue $+0.33$ over three
covariance families, zero Jensen violations). Anchors: $n{=}1$ $=$
static Theorem~9; $w{=}1$ $=$ the two-constraint rate face;
$\tau^2\to\infty$ $=$ the rate face; Toeplitz transfer inherits
Theorem~\ref{thm:matq}-lineage lemma debts. [R-IND-5: PASS --- mode
mapping $2.7\times10^{-15}$ for $n\le8$; full-vs-allocation
$7.4\times10^{-14}$ over five settings and 100+ starts; prices
$(-2.80690,-2.88527)$ with spreads $\le3.4\times10^{-7}$; five
sharpenings folded. Novelty flank swept (record in the GO-13 novelty
file): the spectral lift with two prices and the third-party
conditioner remain claimable; language gated per the sweep.]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The binary twin; settled and netted, GO-P-2026-072]
\label{thm:bintwin}
DSBS($p$) pair at epoch $t$; context a stationary symmetric binary
Markov chain (per-step flip $f$, lag-$k$ crossover $\delta_k$);
observations $S_s=V_s\oplus\mathrm{Bern}(q)$; records
$P(\hat Y\mid Y,V_t)$; access class $\mathcal G$ a \emph{finite}
subset of observations. Then: (i) \textbf{slice collapse is exact} ---
$\{S_{t\pm\Delta}\}$ reduces to Theorem~10 at $q_{\mathrm{eff}}
=q*\delta_\Delta$ (chain reversibility + BSC composition, both time
directions); (ii) the $\sigma$-symmetric family remains optimal for
every finite $\mathcal G$ (the joint flip fixes noise realizations
pointwise, so the involution consumes no condition on $q$ and covers
asymmetric classes; convexity of the shared-channel conditional MI +
symmetrization verbatim), with closed form
\[
L_{\mathcal G}(d_0,d_1)=\mathbb E_r\bigl[h_2(a*r)\bigr]
-(1-p)h_2(d_0)-p\,h_2(d_1),
\]
$r$ the \emph{posterior reliability distribution} of $V_t$ given
$\mathcal G$ --- the per-atom step holds because
$J=\hat Y\oplus V_t$ is $\mathrm{Bern}(a)$ \emph{independent of}
$(V_t,\mathcal G)$ on the family (not by atom pairing; pairing
belongs to the symmetrization lemma alone); (iii) the
\textbf{generalized tilt equation}
$\ell(d_0)-\ell(d_1)=2\,\mathbb E_r[(1-2r)\,\ell(a*r)]$ is sufficient
--- the family is affine in $(d_0,d_1)$ and MI is convex in the
channel, so slice convexity is a \emph{proof}, not an assertion;
interior solutions may have $d_j\in(0,1)$ (at $q=0$, $d_1\to1$ as
$D\to p$). (iv) \textbf{Universality is a Gaussian privilege}: no
single $q_{\mathrm{eff}}$ reproduces a general class's curve --- at
$p{=}0.25$, $f{=}0.15$, $q{=}0.1$, class $\{S_{t-1},S_{t+1}\}$, the
one-point-fit gaps are $+7.1\times10^{-5}$ ($D{=}0.05$) and
$-8.9\times10^{-5}$ ($D{=}0.15$), real at 50-digit precision --- yet
the family is \emph{numerically near-universal}: over
$D\in[0.02,0.40]$ the probed envelope is $\le2.1\times10^{-4}$
(one-point fit) and $\le1.7\times10^{-4}$ (min-sup fit) --- a
numerical observation, not a theorem. Anchors: $\Delta{=}0$ slice
$=$ Theorem~10; $r\equiv\tfrac12$ $=$ marginal $1-h_2(D)$; $q=0$
regime $D\le p$ confirmed. [R-IND-5: PASS --- joints to
$1.4\times10^{-17}$, closed form as an identity to
$2.8\times10^{-16}$, direct optima on-family to $9\times10^{-15}$,
non-collapse at 50 digits; six sharpenings folded, including the two
numeric corrections now in this statement.]
\end{theorem}
