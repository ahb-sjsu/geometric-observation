\begin{abstract}
This plan sets out a thesis built on two completed pillars and one open question.
The first pillar is information-theoretic.
For a jointly Gaussian source with encoder-observed context, the least conditional content of a description given what a third party retains has the closed form $L(D)=\tfrac12\log_2 g^\star$, with $g^\star$ the larger root of an explicit quadratic, and the rate and the conditional content trade off strictly exactly when the context is neither useless nor perfect.
The second pillar is behavioral.
A diagonal Mahalanobis cost on a nine-coordinate decision space, with three variances fitted on nine bargaining and public-goods aggregates, reproduces six classic prospect-theory frequencies and one historical replication within stated tolerances, and its sharpest corollary, invariance of offers to the stake, fails on high-stakes data from three countries.
The two pillars share a method, closed forms where they exist and machine-checked or preregistered falsification where they do not, but they are not yet one theory.
The plan therefore keeps them separate, states the one conjecture that would connect them as a named test rather than a definition, and treats the nine coordinates as a basis to be earned rather than a fact.
Five papers follow: a reproducibility lock, a held-out companion, a boundary paper on the bundled high-stakes data, an encoding paper that tests whether the metric result survives a change of basis, and a reduction paper that derives prospect theory and inequality aversion as limits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Two Pillars, Not One Theory}

A stored description has two prices.
Shannon priced the first: the rate $R(D)$ needed to reproduce $Y$ within distortion $D$.
Landauer priced the second: the average work to erase the stored index given what a third party still holds, which in the limit is the conditional entropy rate $H(M\mid S^n)/n$.
Gray priced the conditional problem when both terminals share the side information \cite{gray1972tr,gray1973}, Wyner and Ziv when only the decoder holds it \cite{wynerziv1976}, and Steinberg priced common reconstruction \cite{steinberg2009}.
In behavioral economics, Kahneman and Tversky priced risky choice and Fehr and Schmidt priced inequality.

An earlier synthesis manuscript argued that these were all one price.
It was declined without review, on scope and presentation, and the argument here does not repeat it.
The thesis rests on two results that stand on their own, and it treats their connection as an open conjecture with a stated test.
Nothing in Papers~1 to 4 below depends on that conjecture.

\section{Pillar I: The Information-Theoretic Price}

The encoder observes a jointly Gaussian pair $(Y,V)$ with correlation $\rho$ after normalization.
The decoder sees only the description $M$.
A third party holds $S=V+U$ with $U\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau^2)$ independent of $(Y,V)$.
With the augmented source $X=(Y,V)$ and a distortion that ignores $V$, define
\begin{equation}
L(D)=\min\bigl\{I(X;\hX\mid S):\ \E(Y-\hY)^2\le D,\ \hX - X - S\bigr\},
\end{equation}
Steinberg's common-reconstruction functional on the augmented alphabet, as Lapidoth, Mal\"ar, and Wigger observed \cite{lapidoth2014}.

\textbf{Result I.1 (closed form).}
$L(D)=\tfrac12\log_2 g^\star$, where $g^\star$ is the larger root of an explicit quadratic in $(D,\rho^2,\tau^2)$, with an achieving Gaussian channel.
The formula recovers Shannon at $\rho=0$, Gray as $\tau^2\to0$, and Steinberg's scalar Gaussian example as $\rho^2\to1$.
The pair $(Y,V)$ suffices for arbitrary-dimensional Gaussian sources through a pair-sufficiency lemma.

\textbf{Result I.2 (region).}
The set of jointly achievable rate and conditional-content pairs $(R,L)$ is exact.
Its Pareto frontier is traced by a two-water-level stationarity system, every Pareto point is attained by a single test channel, and the two prices trade off strictly if and only if $0<\rho^2<1$.
The conditional content is not determined by the joint law of $(Y,S)$: two problems with the same reduced law can have different $L(D)$, so access to the clean context enters the function itself.
The problem is the unpenalized-context limit of the Lu--Xu vector Gaussian Heegard--Berger problem with common reconstruction \cite{luxu2021}, whose admissibility condition leaves that limit outside their closed form.

\textbf{Operational and thermodynamic readings.}
$L(D)$ is the least asymptotically achievable $H(M\mid S^n)/n$ over codes meeting the distortion.
Under the average-work reading of stochastic thermodynamics \cite{landauer1961,esposito2011,sagawa2012,parrondo2015}, it is the infimum of the average erasure work per symbol for an ideal degenerate logical memory reset against retained side information.
Single-shot and quantum refinements \cite{sagawa2009} are deliberately not invoked; the manuscript's referee rounds settled on the average-work reading and the thesis keeps to it.

\textbf{A useful identity.}
For $T=(Y,V)$ and any $\hY$ with $\hY - T - S$,
\begin{equation}
\alpha\,I(T;\hY)+(1-\alpha)\,I(T;\hY\mid S)=I(T;\hY)-(1-\alpha)\,I(S;\hY),
\end{equation}
so the weighted objective of the region is an information-bottleneck Lagrangian with a hard $Y$-distortion.
This is an identity, not a result, and it is recorded here because Paper~5 (Section~\ref{sec:conjecture}) needs it.

\section{Pillar II: The Behavioral Price}

An agent at a reference point $\mathbf r\in[0,1]^9$ chooses an alternative $\mathbf s(x)$, where $x$ ranges over an integer percentage grid of offers or over a finite menu of lotteries.
The cost of the move is
\begin{equation}
c(\Delta\mathbf a)=\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{9}(\Delta a_k)^2/\sigma_k^2\Bigr)^{1/2},\qquad \Delta\mathbf a=\mathbf s(x)-\mathbf r,
\end{equation}
with diagonal $\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_k^2)$.
The nine coordinates are consequences ($d_1$), rights ($d_2$), fairness ($d_3$), autonomy ($d_4$), privacy and trust ($d_5$), social impact ($d_6$), virtue and identity ($d_7$), legitimacy ($d_8$), and epistemic status ($d_9$).
Game predictions are deterministic cost minima on the grid.
Lottery choices use a softmax with a cost-dependent temperature $T(\Delta c)=\max(0.5,\,0.24\,\Delta c^{2.13})$, which is non-monotone with a turnover at $\Delta c=1.41$.
The encodings (reference points, alternatives, the rejection logistic with threshold $0.18$ and steepness $15$ per unit share, the public-goods drift, the certainty measure, and the domain-dependent coding on $d_5$, $d_7$, $d_9$) are listed in full in Part~I and reproduced by eris-econ.

\textbf{Result II.1 (Part~I, IEEE TCSS, accepted 6 September 2026).}
On sixteen aggregates, nine game targets and six Ruggeri et al.\ lottery items ($N=4{,}098$ per item) and one historical ultimatum replication, the active set $\{d_6,d_7,d_9\}$ with variances $\{78.26,\,32.28,\,0.01262\}$ fitted on the nine game targets passes all sixteen within $\pm5$~pp (games) and $\pm10$~pp (lotteries), with unweighted MAE $2.70\%$ and Ruggeri-subset MAE $3.95\%$.
Four of the six lottery predictions fall outside the Ruggeri 95\% intervals, which the paper states as the principal place where the tolerance band overstates precision.
The temperature constants were set from P1 and P3, the historical reference coordinate from the G\"uth offer, and the active set was ranked on all sixteen targets, so the lottery results are cross-domain parameter reuse within a jointly selected architecture, not out-of-sample prediction.
The Andersen high-stakes test is the only result independent of every fitting step.
As of 10 September 2026 the whole table is recomputed by one command, \texttt{eris-econ-table7}, in eris-econ 0.1.1, with a test that fails if the package ever stops reproducing the paper.

\textbf{Result II.2 (falsification, and the data already in hand).}
Share-normalized money makes the model predict offers invariant to the stake.
The openICPSR package of Andersen et al.\ bundles three individual-level high-stakes studies with the pie expressed in days of wages, which is the ratio $\Lambda/Y$ of stake to income:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l r r l l@{}}
\toprule
Study & $N$ & Max days of wages & Offers, low to high stake & Acceptance \\
\midrule
Slonim and Roth, Slovakia \cite{slonim1998} & 820 & 2.7 & 44.5\% to 42.7\% & 0.83 to 0.91 \\
Cameron, Indonesia \cite{cameron1999} & 178 & 30.9 & 42.5\% to 41.2\% & 0.78 to 0.90 \\
Andersen et al., Meghalaya \cite{andersen2011} & 458 & 24.2 & 24.2\% to 12.1\% & 0.64 to 0.96 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Offers do not depend on $\Lambda/Y$ alone: Meghalaya at 2.9 days of wages offers 14\%, Slovakia at 2.7 days offers 43\%, and Cameron's offers are flat at 31 days.
Acceptance rises with days of wages in all three.
The boundary is therefore located on the responder side.
A finite monetary variance belongs on the rejection function, and proposer levels are population reference points $\mathbf r$.

\section{The Basis Is Arbitrary, and That Is Testable}
\label{sec:basis}

The nine coordinates are a chosen basis, not a discovery.
The cost $c$ is coordinate-free for a full covariance: under any invertible linear map $A$ of the coordinates, $\Delta\mathbf a\mapsto A\Delta\mathbf a$ and $\Sigma\mapsto A\Sigma A^{\top}$ leave $c$ unchanged.
The basis enters the model in exactly two places.
First, the diagonal restriction is not invariant under $A$, so the statement ``three active dimensions'' is a statement about a subspace expressed in a particular basis.
Second, the encodings $\mathbf s(x)$ were written by hand in that basis.
Part~II's preregistered result that a rank-one or rank-two precision matrix matches the full metric on three held-out domains is consistent with this reading: what is not arbitrary is the rank and the active subspace, not the axes.

This suggests treating the coordinates as dynamic.
A scenario can be embedded by a fixed, published encoder, for example a multilingual sentence embedding such as LaBSE \cite{feng2022labse} with 768 coordinates, and the metric can be fitted as a rank-constrained precision matrix on that space, with the nine named directions recovered afterwards by projecting the active subspace onto the codebook.
Two cautions govern this.
With sixteen aggregates nothing of that size is identifiable, so the learned-basis arm must be fitted on individual-level data (CPC18, the Fraser--Nettle panels, the Ruggeri country cells) under a preregistered rank bound.
And a learned basis that reproduces the benchmark says nothing until its active subspace is compared with the hand-coded one across encoders.
The significance the owner suspects in the number nine is therefore a hypothesis with a measurement: the dimension of the active subspace that survives a change of encoder.
If it is three under the hand basis, three under raters, and three under a learned embedding, the number is earned.
If it moves, the thesis reports which dimensions were encoding artifacts.

\textbf{Months 9 to 18, Paper 4: reductions.}
Two theorems and a discussion, under Part~II's fixed information-price choice rule, since Part~I's temperature is non-monotone and four of six lottery targets sit on its non-monotone branch.
\begin{conjecture}[Degenerate projections]
Under the fixed information-price rule:
(i) as $\sigma_6,\sigma_7\to\infty$ the ordering over lotteries induced by $c$ is ordinally equivalent to cumulative prospect theory with a weighting function derived from the certainty measure and a value function from the first-order expansion of $c$ near $\mathbf r$;
(ii) as $\sigma_9\to0$ with rejection risk encoded, the cost minimizer over offers is ordinally equivalent to Fehr--Schmidt with $\alpha,\beta$ functions of $\sigma_6/\sigma_9$;
(iii) heuristic search on $\mathcal E$ with $d_9$ truncation is a tractable approximation where the Nash point exists but is not computed.
\end{conjecture}
Title: ``Geometric Reductions of Prospect Theory and Inequality Aversion.''

\section{Paper 5, Conditional: The Bridge as a Test}
\label{sec:conjecture}

The two pillars touch at one coordinate.
In the games, epistemic status carries what the responder knows, through the rejection probability, and that is an information structure.
Information structures have conditional entropies.
The only honest form of the thesis's unifying claim is therefore a measurement:

\begin{conjecture}[Bridge]
For each game target, let $\ell$ be the conditional content of the proposer's decision given the responder's information, computed from the target's information structure under the identity of Section~II.
Then the fitted epistemic displacements $\Delta a_9$ across the sixteen targets are monotone in $\ell$.
\end{conjecture}

If the conjecture holds, the epistemic variance $0.01262$ has an information-theoretic reading and Paper~5 can say so with a number.
If it fails, the thesis reports that the two pillars share a method and a vocabulary, and no more.
Either outcome is publishable; only the assertion without the test is not.
No definition in this plan presupposes the outcome, and the phrase ``unified price theory'' does not appear in any paper title.

\section{Conclusion}

The thesis does not unify economics, and it does not unify information theory with behavior.
It prices two things exactly, a description against retained context and a choice against a reference point, locates the boundary of the second on data already in hand, tests whether its coordinates are a basis or a fact, and states the one connection between the two as a conjecture with a named measurement.
