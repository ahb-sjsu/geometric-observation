\begin{abstract}
We outline a unified price theory that connects two independently developed results: (i) a closed-form rate--content tradeoff for jointly Gaussian sources with encoder-observed context, $L(D)=\tfrac12\log_2 g^\star$ with $g^\star$ the larger root of an explicit quadratic in distortion $D$, source-context correlation $\rho$, and third-party noise $\tau^2$, whose Pareto frontier is traced by two coupled water levels and trades off strictly iff $0<\rho^2<1$; and (ii) a geometric model of economic behavior in which choice cost is a diagonal Mahalanobis distance in a 9-dimensional decision space, with active dimensions $\{d_6\text{: social impact}, d_7\text{: identity}, d_9\text{: epistemic status}\}$ and variances $\{78.26,32.28,0.01262\}$ organizing 16 aggregate frequencies from game theory and prospect theory within $\pm5$pp / $\pm10$pp, MAE $2.70\%$ [Table VII, TCSS Part I]. We argue these are two implementations of one principle: every stored description has two prices — rate $R$ to keep it decodable and conditional content $L$ to erase it given what a third party retains. Access to clean context $V$ strictly lowers $L$ even when the reduced $(Y,S)$ laws are equivalent up to scaling. When refactored, prospect theory and inequality aversion emerge as degenerate projections of the rate--content frontier at $\alpha\to1$ and $\alpha\to0$ via the information-bottleneck identity $J_\alpha = I(T;\hat Y)-(1-\alpha)I(S;\hat Y)$. We present a five-paper research program that moves from reproducibility to boundary location to basis invariance to formal reduction, using bundled high-stakes data (Slonim--Roth Slovakia 820 obs, 2.7 days max; Cameron Indonesia 178 obs, 30.9 days; Andersen Meghalaya 458 obs, 24.2 days) with pie in days-of-wages as $\Lambda/Y$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: Two Prices}

A stored description has two prices. Shannon priced the first: the rate $R(D)$ needed to reproduce $Y$ within distortion $D$. Landauer priced the second: the average work to erase the stored index $M$ given side information $S^n$, $H(M\mid S^n)/n$.

In source coding, Gray priced $R_{Y\mid S}(D)$ when both terminals share $S$, Wyner-Ziv when decoder alone holds $S$, Steinberg \cite{steinberg2009} priced $\min I(X;\hat X\mid S)$ under common reconstruction. In behavioral economics, Kahneman and Tversky priced risky choice via $v(o)w(p)$, Fehr-Schmidt via inequality aversion.

This paper proposes these are not separate prices. They are projections of one frontier.

\section{Pillar I: Information-Theoretic Price}

Consider encoder observes jointly Gaussian pair $(Y,V)$, $\rho=\E[YV]$ after normalization. Decoder sees only description $M$. Third party holds $S=V+U$, $U\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau^2)$ independent.

Define
\begin{equation}
L(D) = \min I(X;\hat X\mid S) \quad \text{s.t. } \E(Y-\hat Y)^2\le D,\; \hat X-X-S,
\end{equation}
with augmented source $X=(Y,V)$ and distortion ignoring $V$. This is Steinberg's functional on augmented alphabet [Lapidoth et al. Remark 8].

\textbf{Result I.1 [Closed form, T-IT CR Thm.~\ref{thm:function}]:} For $Y,V$ scalar linear functionals of arbitrary-dimensional Gaussian source,
\[
L(D)=\tfrac12\log_2 g^\star,
\]
$g^\star$ larger root of explicit quadratic $P(g)=0$ in $(D,\rho,\tau^2)$, with achieving channel. Recovers Shannon at $\rho=0$, Gray at $\tau^2\to0$, Steinberg scalar Gaussian at $\rho^2\to1$.

\textbf{Result I.2 [Region, Thm. region]:} Jointly achievable $(R,L)$ region exact, Pareto frontier traced by coupled two-water-level stationarity system. Strict misalignment — $R$ and $L$ strictly trade off — iff $0<\rho^2<1$ in noisy model $\tau^2>0$, persisting at clean boundary. Structural corollary: $L(D)$ not determined by joint law of $(Y,S)$; encoder access to clean $V$ enters function itself [Cor. notmarginal].

Operational: $L(D)$ is least asymptotically achievable $H(M\mid S^n)/n$. Thermodynamic reading [Remark thermo]: infimum average erasure work per symbol under ideal conditional reset, following Landauer \cite{landauer1961}, Sagawa-Ueda \cite{sagawa2009}, del Rio et al. \cite{delrio2011}, Faist et al. \cite{faist2015}.

IB identity:
\begin{equation}
J_\alpha := \alpha I(T;\hat Y)+(1-\alpha)I(T;\hat Y\mid S) = I(T;\hat Y)-(1-\alpha)I(S;\hat Y),
\end{equation}
for $T=(Y,V)$, $\hat Y-(Y,V)-S$. Weighted objective of region is IB Lagrangian with hard $Y$-distortion.

\section{Pillar II: Behavioral Price}

Consider agent at reference $r\in\R^9$ choosing $s(x)$ on integer grid $0\le s_k\le10$. Cost
\begin{equation}
c(\Delta a)=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^9 (\Delta a_k)^2/\sigma_k^2},\quad \Delta a=s(x)-r,
\end{equation}
with diagonal $\Sigma=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_k^2)$. Dimensions $d_1$..$d_9$: money, effort, risk, delay, valence, social impact, identity, scarcity, epistemic status. Encoding $s_k(x)$: e.g., $m(x)=\min(2x,1)$, $q(x)=1-P_{rej}$, $P_{rej}$ logistic $x_{th}=0.18,\kappa=15$, public-goods drift $-0.02(j-1)$, certainty $\mathrm{cert}=(\max p_i)^2$, loss-domain reversal on $d_5,d_7,d_9$.

\textbf{Result II.1 [TCSS Part I, Table VII]:} On 16 aggregates — 9 game targets (ultimatum, dictator, public goods) + 7 lottery targets (Ruggeri et al. $N=4098$ per item) — active set $\{d_6,d_7,d_9\}$ with variances fitted on 9 game aggregates $\{78.26,32.28,0.01262\}$, temperature constants $T_{base}=0.24,T_\alpha=2.13$ set from P1,P3, $r_9=0.78$ from G\"uth 37\%, passes $\pm5$pp games / $\pm10$pp lottery, 16/16, MAE $2.70\%$, Ruggeri MAE $3.95\%$ with 4 of 6 outside 95\% CI. MRI 1.39pp, perturbation $\epsilon=0.20$ mean 14.8/16, uniform rescaling 16/16. Active set selected from 381 candidates using complete benchmark — cross-domain parameter reuse within jointly selected architecture, not fully out-of-sample.

\textbf{Result II.2 [Falsification]:} Corollary: stake-scaling invariance under share-normalized $s_1=q(x)(1-x)$. Tested on bundled openICPSR high-stakes data with column \texttt{days\_of\_wages} $=\Lambda/Y$:

\section{Unification: Refactoring OT into Geometry}

The T-IT functional is the limit of Lu-Xu vector Gaussian Heegard-Berger with covariance constraint $\mathrm{diag}(D,D_V)$ as $D_V\to\infty$. Their hypothesis $D\preceq\Sigma_X$ requires $(1-D)(1-D_V)\ge\rho^2$, leaving no unpenalized $D_V$. Theorem function(a) evaluates that limit.

Refactored thesis:

\begin{definition}[Unified Price]
Let description $M$ of $(Y,V)$ have two prices $(R,L)$. Let agent decision $x$ map to $s(x)$. Then $c(\Delta a)$ is operationalization of $L(D)$ with $V=(d_6,d_7,d_9)$ and $S$ third-party belief.
\end{definition}

Consequence: $d_9$ variance $0.01262$ is smallest because epistemic status is most informative about $S$. Money $d_1$ inactive at low stakes corresponds to $D_V\to\infty$ limit; finite $\sigma_1^2$ corresponds to $D_V<\infty$ where $\Lambda/Y$ matters for $L$ but not $R$.

Non-monotone temperature $T(\Delta c)=\max(0.5,0.24\Delta c^{2.13})$ with turnover $\Delta c^\dagger=1.41$ — four of six lottery targets above turnover — is artifact of Part I. Information-price fixed $T$ [Part II] restores monotonicity needed for limit theorems.

\textbf{Conjecture [Degenerate Projections]:} Under fixed information-price choice rule,
\begin{itemize}
\item As $\sigma_6,\sigma_7\to\infty$, ordering over lotteries induced by $c$ is ordinally equivalent to CPT with $w(p)$ derived from $\mathrm{cert}$ and $v$ from first-order expansion of $c$ near $r$.
\item As $\sigma_9\to0$, cost minimizer over offers is ordinally equivalent to Fehr-Schmidt with $\alpha,\beta=f(\sigma_6/\sigma_9)$.
\item Nash equilibrium exists but $A^\ast$ on $\mathcal E$ with heuristic $d_9$ truncation is tractable approximation.
\end{itemize}

These are Papers 4 theorems, not assumed in Papers 1-3.

\section{Economic Consequences When Mature}

If refactor holds, every stored economic commitment has two ledger entries: $R$ to keep it decodable, $L$ to erase it given counterparty retention. Mechanisms optimizing $R$ alone underprice deletion. Data centers, platform contracts, demand-response incentives, credit scoring — all pay $L$.

Policy simulation with EV maximizers fails because it sets $\sigma_6,\sigma_7,\sigma_9\to\infty$. Policy simulation with geometric agents at 0.1.1 reproduces Table VII within 2.7pp but must switch to finite $\sigma_1^2$ on rejection at $>2$ days wages.

This is why the concept applies to all domains: rate-content tradeoff is domain-agnostic. Economics is projection at $\alpha$ weighting rate vs leakage $I(\hat X;S)$.

\section{Conclusion}

We have not unified economics. We have priced its two cheapest errors: forgetting context $V$ matters for $L$, and assuming money coordinate is inactive universally. The five-paper arc makes that price checkable, then locates its boundary, then proves its limits.
