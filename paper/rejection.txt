Dear Mr. Bond:

I am writing in regard to manuscript # IT-26-0996 titled "Observation Theory: Geometry, Distortion, and Reliability as Properties of Observation" which you submitted to the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory.

Unfortunately the scope and nature of the work described in your submission do not fit the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, which publishes papers that normally have a significant analytical or conceptual component and aim to have implications for engineering problems related to the storage, transmission, processing and utilization of information, or an associated direction. As stated in the "Information for Authors" of the Transactions at https://www.itsoc.org/it-trans/author-info the suitability of a paper and its usefulness to the readership of the Transactions are important criteria.

The paper is not well written. Already in the abstract, there are terms that are imprecise or not familiar to the IT audience such as "consumer", "resolution budget", "pullback of the output metric", and "isotropic corner". Definition 1 is also not mathematically precise. There is a disconnect between X and x and the underlying probability space/measure is not defined before the use of the expectation. 

Regretfully, I shall not be able to give further consideration to your submission. The EIC and [ArE/AE] were consulted in coming to this decision and they are copied on this email.
