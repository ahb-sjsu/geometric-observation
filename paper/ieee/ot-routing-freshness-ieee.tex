\begin{abstract}
Routing systems monitor \emph{reachability freshness}: whether the control plane
has converged, whether a route is stable, whether a prefix is still validly
originated. Each such monitor is a \emph{certificate}. We show, and measure, that
quiescence-style monitors---``no update seen for $T$ seconds, therefore
converged''---are \emph{consumer-relative vacuous}: the false-clear rate depends
on the observer's vantage and the protocol's opacity, and a monitor that infers
quiescence from update silence clears while the network is still churning. We
formalize the false-clear rate against an independent \emph{witness}---a
BGP Monitoring Protocol (BMP) feed, a receiver-side adjacency-in change-point, or
a dataplane probe---and measure it under a sealed-preregistration protocol.
Across control-plane substrates the $60$\,s quiescence certificate false-clears at
$0.351$ (BGP), $0.184$ (IS-IS), and $0.083$ (OSPF), an ordering set by protocol
opacity; a posterior detector holds a dominance band at $30$--$120$\,s.

\section{Certificates, Consumers, Witnesses}
\begin{definition}[Quiescence certificate]
An assertion that the routing state is converged/stable, inferred from the
absence of updates over a window $T$ at an observer's vantage.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Witness]
An independent signal of actual churn: a BMP route-monitoring feed
(RFC~7854)~\cite{rfc7854}, the receiver-side adjacency-in change-points at the
consumer router, or a dataplane reachability probe. The witness reports what the
quiescence certificate can only infer.
\end{definition}
The false-clear rate is consumer-relative: it depends on vantage (a distant
observer sees fewer updates) and on protocol opacity (link-state floods differ
from path-vector churn). Route-flap damping~\cite{rfc2439} and origin
validation~\cite{rfc6811} are the deployed witnessed corrections for two adjacent
certificates.
