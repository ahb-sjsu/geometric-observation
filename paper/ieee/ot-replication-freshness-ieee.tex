\begin{abstract}
Replicated data stores expose \emph{staleness monitors}: a replica-lag
threshold that certifies a replica ``fresh enough'' to read. We show, and
measure, that such monitors are \emph{consumer-relative vacuous}: no single lag
threshold serves heterogeneous consumers, because staleness is a property of
\emph{what a given consumer reads}, not of the replica in aggregate. We
formalize a \emph{footprint certificate}---the divergence the actual consumer
feels, $\tr(\PC\,\Sigma)$ for a consumer read-operator $\PC$ and a replica drift
$\Sigma$---graded against an independent \emph{witness}: the write-ahead-log
sequence number, the oplog timestamp, or the replica's own replay position. Under
a sealed-preregistration protocol (bars committed before graded runs, a coded
cooling-off, manipulation checks, disjoint seeds), we measure the false-clear
rate of the naive lag monitor and the footprint certificate across four
substrates: a PostgreSQL lab, a MongoDB replica set, a production PostgreSQL
instance with footprints derived from live query statistics, and a
geo-distributed PostgreSQL fleet spanning three real datacenters. In every cell
the naive monitor false-clears near $0.5$ (two-sided: it both admits stale reads
and rejects fresh ones), while the witnessed footprint certificate holds at
$0.01$--$0.06$; on the geo-fleet a \emph{nearest-certified} router is both
fresher than nearest-replica routing and more local than least-lag routing. The
result is the database instance of a program thesis: reliability is a property of
the consumer relation, and a monitor that does not read the consumer's footprint,
graded against a witness, is measurably vacuous.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{} read served from a replica may be stale. Systems therefore
ship \emph{staleness monitors}: bounded-staleness reads, maximum replica lag
thresholds, ``read-your-writes'' flags. Each is a \emph{certificate}---an
assertion that a replica is fresh enough to serve a read. This paper asks what
such certificates actually certify, and measures how often they are wrong.

The central finding is that a replica-lag threshold is the wrong object.
Staleness is not a scalar property of a replica; it is a property of the
relation between a replica's divergence and \emph{what a particular consumer
reads}. A consumer that reads only slowly-changing objects tolerates a lag that
would corrupt a consumer reading a hot object. Consequently no single lag
threshold serves a heterogeneous workload: set it low and the monitor
false-alarms on consumers who do not care; set it high and it admits stale reads
to consumers who do. We measure this two-sided failure directly: a hot consumer
sees a truth-staleness rate near $0.5$ above the threshold, while a cold consumer
pays a false-alarm rate near $0.9$ below it.

The repair is to certify the quantity the consumer feels. Let $\PC$ be a
consumer's \emph{footprint}---a positive operator encoding which objects (or
query results) it reads and how heavily---and let $\Sigma$ be the replica's drift
(the divergence of replicated state from the primary). The consumer's felt
staleness is $\tr(\PC\,\Sigma)$; a \emph{footprint certificate} clears a read iff
this quantity is within tolerance. Crucially, $\Sigma$ is witnessed
natively: the write-ahead log (WAL) sequence number in PostgreSQL, the oplog
timestamp in MongoDB, and the replica's replay position are exactly the
independent ground truth of how far behind the replica is, per object. The
certificate is thus \emph{consumer-relative} (it reads $\PC$) and \emph{witnessed}
(it grades against the log), the same $\tr(\PC\,\Sigma)$ form we develop generally
in the Observation-Theory program~\cite{bond_ot} and ship publicly for
compression~\cite{bond_turboquant}.

\section{Footprint Certificates and Their Witnesses}\label{sec:framework}
\begin{definition}[Footprint certificate]
For a consumer with read-operator $\PC\succeq0$ (which objects/queries it reads,
weighted) and a replica with per-object drift $\Sigma$, the felt staleness is
$d_{\mathcal{C}}=\tr(\PC\,\Sigma)$; the certificate clears a read from that
replica iff $d_{\mathcal{C}}\le\epsilon$ for a tolerance $\epsilon$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Witness]
An independent measurement of $\Sigma$ from the replication substrate itself:
the WAL log-sequence number (LSN) reached by the replica versus the primary in
PostgreSQL, the oplog applied-timestamp in MongoDB, or the replica's replay
position. The witness is not an inference; it is the ground truth the substrate
already maintains.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[False-clear rate]
$\fc$ is the rate at which a monitor clears a read whose witnessed felt staleness
exceeds tolerance (admitting stale) or rejects a read whose felt staleness is
within tolerance (false alarm). A scalar lag monitor incurs both; a footprint
certificate reads $\PC$ and grades against the witness, and incurs neither beyond
sampling.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Two-sided vacuity of scalar lag, informal]
For a workload with heterogeneous footprints there is no replica-lag threshold
that simultaneously bounds admitted-stale rate for hot consumers and false-alarm
rate for cold consumers; the two error rates trade off along the threshold and
meet near $\tfrac12$ at the crossover, independent of the threshold.
\end{proposition}
This is the object we measure. The consumer-relativity is not a modeling choice;
it is forced by the workload, and the witness makes the felt staleness
observable per read.

\section{Sealed-Preregistration Measurement}\label{sec:protocol}
Each cell commits, before any graded run, three bars---B1 (the naive lag monitor
is vacuous, false-clear $\ge$ a threshold near $\tfrac12$), B2 (the footprint
certificate holds, false-clear $\le$ a small tolerance), B3 (dominance)---and
manipulation checks that are also bars: that the induced staleness is real, that
a fresh replica is certified sane, that the certificate is non-degenerate, and,
for the production cell, that the footprint derivation from live statistics is
exact. A verdict runner refuses to grade unless the pre-registration was sealed
at least one day after construction (enforced in code), and grades on seeds
disjoint from the shakedown used to calibrate the bars. Failing runs are kept as
executed. This is the same protocol used for the radio-access instance and the
routing instance of the program.

\subsection{PostgreSQL lab and the MongoDB twin}
The base cell runs a PostgreSQL primary and a replica with a bounded apply delay;
a seeded write schedule drives heterogeneous consumer footprints. The naive
monitor---a single replica-lag threshold---is two-sidedly vacuous: below the
apply delay a cold consumer pays a $0.90$ false-alarm rate (an idle
replay-timestamp pathology inflates apparent lag), while above it a hot consumer's
admitted-stale rate is $0.50$; the min--max false-clear across graded seeds sits
at $0.50$. The WAL-witnessed footprint certificate---$\tr(\PC\,\Sigma)$ against
the replica's replay LSN---holds at $0.05$--$0.06$. The MongoDB cell reproduces
the result on a second substrate and a \emph{second witness}: a replica set with a
delayed secondary, the oplog applied-timestamp as $\Sigma$. Graded, the naive
monitor is $0.45$--$0.48$ and the oplog-witnessed certificate $0.03$--$0.05$. Two
substrate-specific pitfalls, found and fixed during shakedown, are reported in the
cell: a majority write-concern that erases the modeled staleness, and reading a
secondary's applied time through the primary's heartbeat (which itself adds
staleness); the certificate must read the secondary's own member position.

\subsection{Production PostgreSQL with derived footprints}
The production cell earns two graduations over the lab. First, the consumer
footprints $\PC$ are not seeded but \emph{derived} from live query statistics
(\texttt{pg\_stat\_statements}); a manipulation check verifies the derivation is
exact on every seed. Second, the replica lag is emergent (network-emulated,
not dialed). Graded, the naive monitor is $0.46$--$0.47$ and the WAL-witnessed
certificate over the derived footprint $0.01$--$0.03$. The guard rule that makes
the certificate robust---a witness horizon matched to the truth oracle's
guard---generalizes directly to the geo setting: each replica's own minimum
round-trip time sets its guard.

\subsection{Geo-distributed nearest-certified routing}\label{sec:geo}
The geo cell is the read-routing endgame and the bridge to the routing instance
of the program. A PostgreSQL primary and three streaming replicas are placed in
three real, geographically separated datacenters on the National Research
Platform. Three routers compete on two axes: \emph{nearest} minimizes
round-trip time, \emph{least-lag} minimizes replica lag, and \emph{certified}
routes to the nearest replica whose footprint certificate clears against its own
witnessed replay LSN. Graded, the certified router attains a false-clear of $0$
at round-trip $\approx11$\,ms, while nearest pays a false-clear of $\approx0.30$
(it routes local but stale) and least-lag pays $16$--$18$\,ms (it routes fresh
but far). The certificate is thus \emph{fresher than nearest and more local than
least-lag}: it is the only router that reads both the consumer's footprint and
the witness. A guard-consistency lesson recurs from the production cell---the
witness horizon must match the truth oracle's guard, or every replica is
disqualified by in-flight writes and the router falls back to the distant
primary.

\section{The Consistency Design Space}\label{sec:space}
A survey of the replication and consistency design space through the certificate
lens (archived with the program) organizes the space by one claim: the
\emph{form} of the certificate follows the order structure of the consistency
model. A total order (consensus, strongly consistent stores) admits a scalar
log-position certificate---the LSN or oplog timestamp used above. A partial order
(leaderless, causal, CRDT stores~\cite{shapiro2011crdt}) requires a version-set
dominance certificate. A probabilistic model~\cite{bailis2012pbs} requires a
calibrated depth. A physical-time model~\cite{corbett2012spanner} requires a
lease. The witness leg is near-universal, and prior art already ships pieces:
Nakamoto confirmations and Spanner commit-wait are \emph{already} calibrated;
session guarantees~\cite{terry1994session} and vendor bounded-staleness reads are
consumer-relative by construction but \emph{uncalibrated}. The program's delta is
to ship all four grammar words together---consumer-relative, witnessed,
calibrated, derivable---and to \emph{measure the rate}. ZooKeeper's local-read
staleness under \texttt{sync}~\cite{hunt2010zookeeper,junqueira2011zab} is a
natural next cell.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\emph{CAP and PACELC.} The footprint certificate is the constructive form of the
staleness face of consistency. Where CAP~\cite{gilbert2002cap} and
PACELC~\cite{abadi2012pacelc} name a tradeoff between consistency and latency
(or availability), the certificate \emph{quantifies the interior}: it prices the
consistency each consumer actually needs, per read, and routes to the nearest
replica that supplies it. Partition is the limiting case where the witnessed
staleness diverges; the else-latency regime is exactly where nearest-certified
routing wins. \emph{Age of information.} As in the radio-access
instance~\cite{kaul2012aoi}, age bounds staleness on the log; the footprint
certificate measures its consequence for the specific consumer, which is what a
consistency SLA must bound. \emph{Benchmarking.} The cells are amenable to
YCSB-lineage workloads~\cite{cooper2010ycsb}; the footprint is exactly the
per-record read distribution such benchmarks parameterize.

\section{Limitations and External Validity}
The lab and production cells are single-primary, single- or few-replica; the geo
cell is three replicas across three datacenters but still PostgreSQL streaming
replication. Footprints are diagonal read-operators (per-object weights); a full
$\PC$ over query-result functionals is a natural extension already used in the
compression instance~\cite{bond_turboquant}. Induced staleness in the geo cell is
partly disclosed (a rotating apply-pause) because the research backbone is faster
than the guard; the locality axis is real inter-datacenter round-trip. The
consistency-model taxonomy of Section~\ref{sec:space} is argued from the survey,
not measured cell-by-cell; leaderless and CRDT certificates are future cells.

\section{Conclusion}
Replication freshness is not a scalar property of a replica but a relation between
its drift and what a consumer reads. A replica-lag threshold is two-sidedly
vacuous on a heterogeneous workload; a footprint certificate $\tr(\PC\,\Sigma)$,
graded against the replication log the substrate already maintains, holds at a
percent-level false-clear across PostgreSQL, MongoDB, a production instance with
derived footprints, and a three-datacenter fleet. Nearest-certified read routing
is simultaneously fresher than nearest and more local than least-lag. We
recommend that bounded-staleness reads be specified, measured, and
\emph{certified} against the consumer's footprint and the replication witness,
rather than a global lag bound.
