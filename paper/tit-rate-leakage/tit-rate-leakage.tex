\begin{abstract}
An encoder observes a jointly Gaussian vector and describes part of it
to a decoder under a weighted quadratic distortion. A second party
holds a noisy linear observation of the source and never decodes. The
description has two costs, its rate and its conditional leakage to the
second party. We determine the structure of the single-letter region
known from secure source coding. At every budget and in every
geometry, each frontier point is the fixed point of reverse
water-filling on the decoder's weight tilted by the second party's
information. For a scalar second-party observation this reduces to two
scalar equations, and for a scalar described variable to one scalar
root. When the optimal description is one-dimensional, the minimum
leakage is a closed-form function
of the largest eigenvalue of a matrix pencil in which the budget enters.
The direction of the best one-dimensional description turns with the
budget unless the two parties' geometries share it, while a description
built for a single party keeps fixed directions, and a fixed direction
pays an explicit excess leakage. The tradeoff exists because the
distortion is a trace-type constraint. Under a matrix distortion on the
whole source, one description minimizes both costs. With side
information at the decoder, we prove the region for the continuous
source. If the decoder knows more than the second party there is no
tradeoff, and if it knows less the problem reduces exactly to the one
without decoder side information.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

One description of data is often held by more than one party. The
decoder that reconstructs from it pays its rate. A second party that
holds the description together with its own observation of the source
learns from it the conditional mutual information between the source and
the description given that observation. In a secrecy reading the second
party is an eavesdropper and this quantity is the information leakage
beyond its side information. Minimizing it maximizes the eavesdropper's
equivocation about the source. In a storage reading the second party
keeps the description compressed against what it already knows, and the
same quantity is the least rate at which it can re-encode the
description. Either way one description carries two costs, set by two
different parties, and the encoder, which may observe more of the
source than either party, decides how they trade off.

The single-letter region of jointly achievable rate, distortion, and
equivocation is due to Villard and Piantanida
\cite[Theorem~3]{villard2013}, and for vector Gaussian sources Ekrem and
Ulukus \cite{ekrem2013} obtained the minimum leakage when the rate is
unconstrained, as an optimization over covariance matrices. The joint
rate--leakage region was left open. They report that they could not
solve the weighted problem that traces it \cite[eqs.~(30)--(31)]{ekrem2013}, and Xu,
Chen, Chen, and Jin \cite{xu2024} have since characterized the
rate--distortion--equivocation function by a refined
enhancement-and-perturbation argument, leaving the region as a matrix
program. Without decoder side
information the region itself is elementary, because a single Gaussian
exhaustion lemma bounds both costs at once (Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior}).
What this paper contributes is the structure of that region for an
informed encoder. The
encoder observes a jointly Gaussian vector $T$, the decoder must
reproduce a linear part $Y=FT$ under a weighted quadratic distortion,
and the second party observes $S=HT+U$ for an arbitrary matrix $H$.
The two costs disagree because the distortion is a single trace-type
constraint, which leaves the encoder a choice among error covariances.
Under a matrix distortion on the whole source, the formulation of the
earlier work, one description minimizes both costs, as the bounds of
Ekrem and Ulukus already imply (Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix}). Which
directions the optimal description favors is set by how the decoder's
weight and the second party's information are aligned, whether or not
the encoder observes more than it describes. When $H$ also reaches
parts of $T$ outside $Y$, the encoder holds information about the second
party's observation that the decoder does not need, and then the
leakage is not determined by the joint law of the described variable
and the second party's observation.

The results fall into three groups.

\emph{General characterization and computation.} The coding theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:op}) identifies the operational region with the
single-letter one, and Gaussian descriptions, indexed by their error
covariance, exhaust it (Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior}). In that
coordinate both costs are convex (Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}), so every
frontier point is the unique minimizer of a convex program and the
minimum leakage is a determinant maximization under linear matrix
inequalities. An optimal description has no more components than the
described variable, even though it uses the context
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rank}). At every budget and in every geometry the
frontier error covariance is the fixed point of reverse water-filling on
the decoder's weight tilted by the information the second party's
observation adds to the description (Theorem~\ref{thm:tilted}).
Equivalently, it is the rate--distortion optimum for a decoder that must
also describe the holder's view (Corollary~\ref{cor:augmented}). An
iteration of classical water-filling steps computes it for holders and
described variables of any rank (Proposition~\ref{prop:mm}). With side
information at the decoder we prove the region for the continuous source
and settle both degraded cases (Section~\ref{sec:decsi}).

\emph{Explicit solutions.} When the optimal description is
one-dimensional, which an explicit certificate decides, the minimum
leakage is
\[
L(D)=\tfrac12\log\bigl(1+\Delta/\mu_{\max}\bigr),
\]
with $\Delta$ the reduction in distortion the description must buy and
$\mu_{\max}$ the largest generalized eigenvalue of a pencil formed from
the source covariance, the decoder's weight, the conditional covariance
left after the second party's observation, and the budget
(Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}). For a scalar described variable and a scalar
context this is an explicit closed form (Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar}). For
a scalar holder the whole frontier reduces to two scalar equations
(Corollary~\ref{cor:scalarholder}). For a scalar described variable, with
any context and any holder, every optimal description reads a direction
on the curve $((1-\varrho)\Sigma_T+\varrho\Kc)^{-1}\Sigma_Tq$,
$\varrho\in[0,1]$, selected by one scalar root
(Theorem~\ref{thm:scalarpath}). The frontier is also explicit at every
budget when the two geometries commute, and it splits along every
subspace decomposition they share (Theorems~\ref{thm:commuting}
and~\ref{thm:decouple}). It is explicit at small budgets when the decoder
reproduces the whole source (Theorem~\ref{thm:lowdist}), and for
independent components, whose leakage-optimal split activates components
in an order that is not the order of their variances
(Section~\ref{sec:parallel}).

\emph{Budget-dependent directions and the price of fixed ones.} The
rate-optimal description reads fixed directions at every budget, as do
principal components, reverse water-filling \cite{berger1971}, and the
Gaussian information bottleneck \cite{chechik2005}. The best
one-dimensional description, which is the leakage-optimal one wherever
the certificate of Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(b) holds, reads a direction
that turns with the budget, unless that direction is a common generalized
eigenvector of the two parties' geometries (Theorem~\ref{thm:turning}).
No description optimized for a single party under a weighted quadratic
distortion does this (Proposition~\ref{prop:single}). A one-dimensional
description with a fixed projection direction pays an explicit excess
leakage at one of any two budgets whose optimal directions differ
(Theorem~\ref{thm:forced}), and the same condition decides whether the
rate-optimal and leakage-optimal descriptions coincide
(Theorem~\ref{thm:misalign}). The effect survives quantization. At equal
rate and equal budget, a Lloyd--Max quantizer on a leakage-aware
projection leaks 13\% to 72\% less than the same quantizer on the
principal axis, and its direction turns with the budget
(Appendix~\ref{sec:quant}).

\subsection*{Related work}

The conditional leakage studied here is the secrecy literature's
quantity. For a deterministic encoder,
$h(T^n\mid M,S^n)=h(T^n\mid S^n)-H(M\mid S^n)$, so minimizing
$H(M\mid S^n)/n$, which equals $I(T^n;M\mid S^n)/n$, is the same as
maximizing the equivocation $h(T^n\mid M,S^n)/n$. Secure lossy source
coding with side information at the eavesdropper was characterized by
Villard and Piantanida \cite{villard2013}. With no decoder side
information their Theorem~3 gives the discrete region of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dmc} below. Ekrem and Ulukus \cite{ekrem2013} treat
vector Gaussian sources with side information at both receivers, with
arbitrary observation maps \cite[Sec.~5]{ekrem2013}, under a matrix
distortion on the whole source. They give an outer bound that is tight
without a rate constraint and leave the joint region open
\cite[eqs.~(30)--(31)]{ekrem2013}. Their leakage is the total
information at the eavesdropper, which differs from the conditional
leakage used here by the constant $I(T;S)$. Without decoder side
information, the region of Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:op} is the union of their matrix-distortion regions
over $\Lambda\preceq\Sigma_T$ with $\tr(Q\Lambda)\le D$, because a code
meeting the trace constraint may output $\E[T\mid M]$, whose error
matrix is at most $\Sigma_T$. Appendix~\ref{app:coding} proves the
continuous-alphabet coding theorem directly. Xu, Chen, Chen, and Jin
\cite{xu2024} characterize the rate--distortion--equivocation function of
vector Gaussian secure source coding, which the conventional
enhancement-and-perturbation approach had not reached, by refining that
approach with a monotone path argument. The published version describes
its proofs as simplified and corrected relative to earlier ones. The
detailed form below is from the preprint \cite{xuchen2021}, which is the
version we checked against, and its assumptions and equation numbers
should be read as the preprint's. There the characterization is a matrix
program over two precision matrices \cite[eq.~(189), Thm.~4]{xuchen2021}
for full-rank side informations at both receivers. Absent decoder side information, a formal limit
outside their theorem, the program reduces to the one of
Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior} in the precision coordinate
$\Sez^{-1}-\Sigma_T^{-1}$ with the matrix constraint, which has no
tradeoff (Proposition~\ref{prop:matrix}). These results fix the form of
the optimizers. The structure of the solution under a trace-type
distortion, which is the subject here, is not addressed there.
G\"unl\"u, Schaefer, Boche, and
Poor \cite{gunlu2022} evaluate the lossy rate region for scalar Gaussian
sources with a remote source and separate secrecy and privacy leakage.
None of these works treats an encoder that observes components it need
not describe, a trace-type distortion on part of the source, or the
structure results below. Related problems price the leakage of a
description about side information
\cite{yamamoto1983,tandon2013,makhdoumi2014} or the equivocation of the
source in a cipher system \cite{yamamoto1997,schieler2014}. The leakage
endpoint is Steinberg's common-reconstruction functional
\cite{steinberg2009} on the source as the encoder observes it, an
augmentation noted for finite alphabets by Lapidoth, Mal\"ar, and Wigger
\cite{lapidoth2014}. A vector Gaussian common-reconstruction function
with every component penalized was given by Lu and Xu \cite{luxu2021}.
Weighted quadratic distortion models a decoder that cares about some
directions more than others, as in weighted rate--distortion
\cite{sakrison1968}, indirect source coding \cite{wolfziv1970}, and
task-based quantization \cite{shlezinger2019}. Parameterizing a
Gaussian description by its error covariance is a standard device in
vector Gaussian source coding \cite{rahman2012}, and the resulting
endpoint program is a determinant maximization \cite{vandenberghe1998}.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:model} sets up the
model and the coding theorem, Section~\ref{sec:convex} proves the convex
structure, Section~\ref{sec:tilted} gives the water-filling
characterization of the whole frontier, Section~\ref{sec:commuting} its
explicit cases, Section~\ref{sec:onedim} the one-dimensional case,
Section~\ref{sec:directions} the results on directions and misalignment,
Section~\ref{sec:parallel} independent components, and
Section~\ref{sec:decsi} decoder side information.
Section~\ref{sec:examples} gives instances and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} open problems. The appendices prove the
coding theorem and a Gaussian exhaustion lemma, evaluate practical
scalar quantizers (Appendix~\ref{sec:quant}), and describe the numerical
checks.

\section{Model and the Coding Theorem}
\label{sec:model}

Logarithms are base two unless written $\ln$. The encoder observes
i.i.d.\ copies of $T\in\R^p$, jointly Gaussian and centered with
$\Sigma_T\succ0$. The described variable is $Y=FT\in\R^m$ for a matrix
$F$ of full row rank, and the decoder's distortion is
$\E[(Y-\hY)^{\mathsf T}W(Y-\hY)]$ for a weight $W\succ0$. Write
\[
Q:=F^{\mathsf T}WF\succeq0,
\qquad
D_{\max}:=\tr(Q\Sigma_T).
\]
The second party, which we call the holder, observes $S=HT+U\in\R^r$
with $U\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_U)$, $\Sigma_U\succ0$, independent of $T$
and of all coding randomness. Write
\[
J:=H^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_U^{-1}H\succeq0,
\qquad
\Kc=(\Sigma_T^{-1}+J)^{-1},
\]
the information that $S$ carries about $T$ and the conditional
covariance it leaves.

A code consists of an encoder $f_n:\R^{p\times n}\to\mathcal M_n$, a finite
set, and a decoder $g_n:\mathcal M_n\to\R^{m\times n}$. With $M=f_n(T^n)$
and $\hY^n=g_n(M)$, its rate, distortion, and leakage are
\[
R_n=\tfrac1n\log|\mathcal M_n|,\quad
D_n=\tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\E\bigl[(Y_i-\hY_i)^{\mathsf T}W(Y_i-\hY_i)\bigr],\quad
L_n=\tfrac1nH(M\mid S^n)=\tfrac1nI(T^n;M\mid S^n).
\]
A pair $(R,L)$ is achievable at distortion $D$ when a sequence of codes
has $\limsup R_n\le R$, $\limsup L_n\le L$, and $\limsup D_n\le D$. The
closure of the achievable set is $\RW(D)$. Throughout $0<D<D_{\max}$. At
$D\ge D_{\max}$ the zero reproduction gives $R=L=0$. Write $R_{\min}(D)$
and $L(D)$ for the least rate and the least leakage over $\RW(D)$, and
call a description rate-optimal or leakage-optimal when it attains one
of them.

A test channel $p(\hy\mid t)$ is admissible when its distortion is at
most $D$. The Markov chain $\hY-T-S$ holds because the description is
formed without $S$. For a channel, let $\Sez$ and $\Se$ be the error
covariances of the best linear estimates of $T$ from $\hY$ and from
$(\hY,S)$. Appendix~\ref{app:exhaust} proves the following Gaussian
exhaustion lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gauss}
Let $\hY\in\R^k$, for any $k$, be centered with finite second moments, let $A$ be its
regression matrix on $T$ and $N'=\hY-AT$ its residual, and suppose
$\Cov(N',S)=0$ and $\Cov N'\succ0$. Then
$I(T;\hY)\ge\tfrac12\log(\det\Sigma_T/\det\Sez)$ and
$I(T;\hY\mid S)\ge\tfrac12\log(\det\Kc/\det\Se)$, and the jointly
Gaussian channel $AT+N$, $N\sim\mathcal N(0,\Cov N')$ independent of
$(T,U)$, has the same second moments and meets both bounds with
equality.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:posterior}
The union of the quadrants above
$\bigl(I(T;\hY),I(T;\hY\mid S)\bigr)$ over admissible channels equals the
union, over $\Sez\in\mathcal P(D)$, of the quadrants above
\begin{equation}
\bigl(r(\Sez),\ \ell(\Sez)\bigr)
=\Bigl(\tfrac12\log\frac{\det\Sigma_T}{\det\Sez},\
\tfrac12\log\frac{\det\Kc}{\det\Se}\Bigr),
\qquad
\Se^{-1}=\Sez^{-1}+J,
\label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal P(D)=\{\Sez:\ 0\prec\Sez\preceq\Sigma_T,\ \tr(Q\Sez)\le D\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given an admissible channel, centering $\hY$ changes neither coordinate
and does not raise the distortion. If $\Cov N'$ is singular, either a
noiseless linear functional of $T$ is revealed and both coordinates are
infinite, or a nonzero combination of the components of $\hY$ vanishes
and may be dropped after an invertible linear map. So assume
$\Cov N'\succ0$. The residual is uncorrelated with $T$ by construction
and with $U$ by independence, hence with $S$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:gauss}
replaces the channel by $\hY=AT+N$ with the same distortion and no
larger coordinates. For it,
$\Sez^{-1}=\Sigma_T^{-1}+A^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_N^{-1}A$, so
$0\prec\Sez\preceq\Sigma_T$. The pair $(\hY,S)$ is a linear observation
of $T$ with independent noises, so $\Se^{-1}=\Sez^{-1}+J$. The
distortion is at least that of $\E[Y\mid\hY]$, which is $\tr(Q\Sez)$.
Conversely, given $\Sez\in\mathcal P(D)$, factor
$\Sez^{-1}-\Sigma_T^{-1}=G^{\mathsf T}G$, let $Z=GT+N$ with
$N\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ independent of $(T,U)$, and reproduce
$\hY=\E[Y\mid Z]$. Then $\Cov(T\mid Z)=\Sez$, the distortion is
$\tr(Q\Sez)\le D$, $\hY-T-S$ holds, and since $\hY$ is a function of $Z$,
data processing bounds its coordinates by those of $Z$, which are
\eqref{eq:posterior}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:op}
For every $0<D<D_{\max}$, $\RW(D)$ equals the union of
Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior}.
\end{theorem}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:coding}. Its discrete core is the
region of Villard and Piantanida without decoder side information
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dmc}). The Gaussian extension quantizes the side
information, the source, and the reproduction and passes to the limit.

The tradeoff studied below needs the distortion to leave the encoder a
choice of error covariance. The vector Gaussian secrecy literature
\cite{ekrem2013,xu2024} instead asks the decoder to reproduce the
whole source under a matrix constraint, and without decoder side
information that choice disappears. The bounds of Ekrem and Ulukus
imply the following. We state it in the present notation because the
contrast organizes the rest of the paper.

\begin{proposition}[cf.~{\cite[Thms.~6--7]{ekrem2013}}]\label{prop:matrix}
Suppose the decoder reproduces all of $T$, an admissible channel is one
whose reproduction $\hat T$ has
$\E[(T-\hat T)(T-\hat T)^{\mathsf T}]\preceq\Lambda$, and
$0\prec\Lambda\preceq\Sigma_T$. Then the union of the quadrants above
$\bigl(I(T;\hat T),I(T;\hat T\mid S)\bigr)$ over admissible channels is
the single quadrant above $\bigl(r(\Lambda),\ell(\Lambda)\bigr)$ of
\eqref{eq:posterior}, so one description is both rate-optimal and
leakage-optimal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior} applies with the matrix
constraint in place of the trace, because the error matrix of $\hat T$
depends only on second moments and dominates $\Cov(T\mid\hat T)=\Sez$
for the Gaussian channel. The feasible set becomes
$\{0\prec\Sez\preceq\Lambda\}$. Both $r$ and $\ell$ are nonincreasing in
the Loewner order, since $\Se=(\Sez^{-1}+J)^{-1}$ is monotone in $\Sez$,
and $\Lambda$ is the largest feasible element.
\end{proof}

Under the weighted constraint $\tr(Q\Sez)\le D$ the feasible set has no
largest element, and which maximal element the encoder picks is the
whole problem. The same is true of a matrix constraint on the described
part $Y$ alone, $F\Sez F^{\mathsf T}\preceq\Lambda_Y$, which leaves the
error covariance free outside $Y$.

\textbf{Running example.} Two instances recur. The scalar instance has
$T=(Y,V)$ with unit variances and correlation $\rho$, $W=1$, and
$S=V+U$ with $\Var U=\tau^2$. The coupled instance has a
two-dimensional described variable with $\Sigma_Y=\mathrm{diag}(1,0.4)$,
$W=I$, a scalar context with $\Var V=1$ and $\Cov(Y,V)=0.6\,u$ for the
unit vector $u$ at $50^\circ$, and $S=V+U$ with $\Var U=0.05$, so
$D_{\max}=1.4$. In both the holder observes only the context.

\section{Convex Structure}
\label{sec:convex}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:convex}
On $\{0\prec\Sez\preceq\Sigma_T\}$, the rate $r$ and the leakage $\ell$
of \eqref{eq:posterior} are strictly convex functions of $\Sez$, and the
distortion $\tr(Q\Sez)$ is linear. Consequently the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every $\alpha\in[0,1]$ the program
$\min\{\alpha r(\Sez)+(1-\alpha)\ell(\Sez):\Sez\in\mathcal P(D)\}$ has a
unique minimizer, the distortion constraint is active there, and the
minima over $\alpha$ trace the Pareto frontier of $\RW(D)$.
\item[(b)] In the coordinate $\Se$ the minimum leakage is a determinant
maximization,
\begin{equation}
L(D)=\tfrac12\log\det\Kc-\tfrac12\max\bigl\{\log\det\Se:\
0\prec\Se\preceq\Kc,\ \tr\bigl(Qh(\Se)\bigr)\le D\bigr\},
\qquad h(\Se):=(\Se^{-1}-J)^{-1},
\label{eq:maxdet}
\end{equation}
in which $h$ is matrix-convex and the constraint is equivalent to a
linear matrix inequality. With $J=B^{\mathsf T}B$,
$\tr(Qh(\Se))\le D$ if and only if some symmetric $Z$ has
$\tr(QZ)\le D$ and
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
Z-\Se&\Se B^{\mathsf T}\\
B\Se&I-B\Se B^{\mathsf T}
\end{bmatrix}\succeq0 ;
\label{eq:lmi}
\end{equation}
\item[(c)] A feasible $\Se$ is the maximizer in \eqref{eq:maxdet} if and
only if $\tr(Qh(\Se))=D$ and there are $\varpi\ge0$ and $\Theta\succeq0$
with
\begin{equation}
\tfrac12\Se-\varpi\,\Sez Q\Sez=\Se\Theta\Se,
\qquad
\Theta(\Kc-\Se)=0,
\qquad \Sez=h(\Se),
\label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
and then $\varpi>0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Convexity.} $r=\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_T-\tfrac12\log\det\Sez$ is
strictly convex. For the leakage, write $J=B^{\mathsf T}B$ and note that
$\Se=(\Sez^{-1}+B^{\mathsf T}B)^{-1}$ is the error covariance of the best
linear estimate of a vector with covariance $\Sez$ from its noisy image
under $B$ with unit noise. For every matrix $L$, the estimator with gain
$L$ has error covariance
$E_L(\Sez)=(I-LB)\Sez(I-LB)^{\mathsf T}+LL^{\mathsf T}\succeq\Se$, with
equality at the optimal gain, and $E_L$ is affine in $\Sez$. A pointwise
minimum of affine maps in the L\"owner order, attained at each point, is
matrix-concave. At $\Sigma_t=(1-t)\Sigma_0+t\Sigma_1$ with optimal gain
$L_t$,
$\Se(\Sigma_t)=E_{L_t}(\Sigma_t)=(1-t)E_{L_t}(\Sigma_0)+tE_{L_t}(\Sigma_1)
\succeq(1-t)\Se(\Sigma_0)+t\Se(\Sigma_1)$. The map
$\Sigma\mapsto-\log\det\Sigma$ is strictly convex and decreasing in the
L\"owner order, so
$-\log\det\Se(\Sigma_t)\le-\log\det\bigl((1-t)\Se(\Sigma_0)+t\Se(\Sigma_1)\bigr)
\le(1-t)(-\log\det\Se(\Sigma_0))+t(-\log\det\Se(\Sigma_1))$, with strict
inequality in the second step when $\Sigma_0\ne\Sigma_1$, because
$\Sez\mapsto\Se$ is injective. So $\ell$ is strictly convex.

(a) $\mathcal P(D)$ is convex, the objective is strictly convex and
diverges as $\det\Sez\to0$, and the feasible set is bounded, so the
minimizer exists and is unique. Both $r$ and $\ell$ decrease when $\Sez$
increases in the L\"owner order, so at a minimizer with slack distortion
a move toward $\Sigma_T$ would stay feasible and lower the objective,
since $\Sigma_T$ itself has distortion $D_{\max}>D$. The region is
convex and upward closed, so its Pareto points are exactly the minima of
the weighted programs.

(b) The map $\Sez\mapsto\Se$ is a bijection of
$\{0\prec\Sez\preceq\Sigma_T\}$ onto $\{0\prec\Se\preceq\Kc\}$, with
inverse $h$, since $\Sez\preceq\Sigma_T$ if and only if $\Se^{-1}=\Sez^{-1}+J
\succeq\Sigma_T^{-1}+J=\Kc^{-1}$. The matrix $B\Kc B^{\mathsf T}$ has the
same nonzero eigenvalues as $\Kc^{1/2}J\Kc^{1/2}\prec
\Kc^{1/2}(\Sigma_T^{-1}+J)\Kc^{1/2}=I$, so on the feasible set
$B\Se B^{\mathsf T}\prec I$, and the Woodbury identity gives
$h(\Se)=\Se+\Se B^{\mathsf T}(I-B\Se B^{\mathsf T})^{-1}B\Se$. By the
Schur complement criterion \cite[Sec.~A.5.5]{boyd2004}, $h(\Se)\preceq Z$
if and only if \eqref{eq:lmi} holds, and the block matrix there is
affine in $(\Se,Z)$. Averaging two instances with $Z_i=h(\Se^{(i)})$
gives matrix convexity of $h$. Since $\tr(QZ)$ is monotone in $Z$, the
constraint $\tr(Qh(\Se))\le D$ is equivalent to the existence of $Z$ with
$\tr(QZ)\le D$ and \eqref{eq:lmi}.

(c) The program \eqref{eq:maxdet} has a strictly feasible point
($\Sez=\epsilon\Sigma_T$ with $\epsilon D_{\max}<D$), so the
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions are necessary and sufficient. The
differential of $h$ is $dh=h\Se^{-1}d\Se\Se^{-1}h$, so the gradient of
$\tr(Qh)$ is $\Se^{-1}\Sez Q\Sez\Se^{-1}$, and stationarity of
$-\tfrac12\ln\det\Se+\varpi\tr(Qh)+\tr(\Theta(\Se-\Kc))$, multiplied on
both sides by $\Se$, is the first condition of \eqref{eq:kkt}, the factor
$\ln2$ being absorbed in $\varpi$. When the distortion constraint is
active its complementary slackness holds for every $\varpi\ge0$, and the
maximizer has an active constraint by (a). If $\varpi=0$, then
$\Theta=\tfrac12\Se^{-1}\succ0$ and the second condition forces
$\Se=\Kc$, which is infeasible. So $\varpi>0$.
\end{proof}

The optimal description uses the context, but it never needs more
dimensions than the described variable.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rank}
For every $\alpha\in[0,1]$ the minimizer $\Sez$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}(a) has
$\operatorname{rank}(\Sigma_T-\Sez)\le\operatorname{rank}Q$, so it is
attained by a jointly Gaussian description $GT+N$ with at most
$\operatorname{rank}Q$ components.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The weighted program in $\Sez$ is convex with the strictly feasible point
$\epsilon\Sigma_T$, so there are $\nu\ge0$ and $\Theta\succeq0$ with
$\Theta(\Sigma_T-\Sez)=0$ and, from the gradients
$\nabla r=-\tfrac12\Sez^{-1}$ and
$\nabla\ell=-\tfrac12\Sez^{-1}\Se\Sez^{-1}$ (in natural units),
\[
\tfrac12\Sez^{-1}\bigl(\alpha\Sez+(1-\alpha)\Se\bigr)\Sez^{-1}=\nu Q+\Theta .
\]
The left side is positive definite, so
$\operatorname{rank}\Theta\ge p-\operatorname{rank}Q$, and the range of
$\Sigma_T-\Sez$ lies in the null space of $\Theta$. The precision
$\Sez^{-1}-\Sigma_T^{-1}=\Sez^{-1}(\Sigma_T-\Sez)\Sigma_T^{-1}$ has the
same rank, and factoring it as $G^{\mathsf T}G$ gives the description of
Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior}.
\end{proof}

\section{Water-Filling on a Tilted Weight}
\label{sec:tilted}

The frontier has one characterization that holds in every geometry and
at every budget, for partial and full descriptions alike. It rests on
the identity $\ell=r-I(\hY;S)$, which holds because $\hY-T-S$. Whiten by
the source covariance,
\[
X=\Sigma_T^{-1/2}\Sez\Sigma_T^{-1/2},\qquad
\tilde Q=\Sigma_T^{1/2}Q\Sigma_T^{1/2},\qquad
\tilde H=H\Sigma_T^{1/2},
\]
so that $r=-\tfrac12\log\det X$,
$I(\hY;S)=\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_S-\tfrac12\log\det(\tilde HX\tilde H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U)$,
the distortion is $\tr(\tilde QX)$, and the cap is $X\preceq I$. For a
symmetric $A\succeq0$ with eigenpairs $(\lambda_i,e_i)$, let
\[
\Phi(A)=\sum_i\min\{1,1/\lambda_i\}\,e_ie_i^{\mathsf T},
\]
with $\min\{1,1/\lambda_i\}=1$ when $\lambda_i=0$, reverse water-filling at
level one on the spectrum of $A$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:tilted}
For $\alpha\in[0,1]$ and $0<D<D_{\max}$, the frontier point of weight
$\alpha$ is the unique $X$ with $\tr(\tilde QX)=D$ and
\begin{equation}
X=\Phi\bigl(\tilde A(X)\bigr),\qquad
\tilde A(X)=2\nu\tilde Q+(1-\alpha)\,\tilde H^{\mathsf T}\bigl(\tilde HX\tilde H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U\bigr)^{-1}\tilde H
\label{eq:tilted}
\end{equation}
for some $\nu>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In natural units the weighted objective is
$-\tfrac12\ln\det X+\tfrac{1-\alpha}2\ln\det(\tilde HX\tilde H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U)$
plus a constant, convex in $X$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}, with
gradient $-\tfrac12X^{-1}+\tfrac{1-\alpha}2\tilde H^{\mathsf T}(\tilde HX\tilde H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U)^{-1}\tilde H$.
The program has the strictly feasible point $\epsilon I$, so a point is
the minimizer if and only if $\tr(\tilde QX)\le D$ and there are $\nu\ge0$
and $\Theta\succeq0$ with $\Theta(I-X)=0$, $\nu(\tr(\tilde QX)-D)=0$, and
$X^{-1}=\tilde A(X)+2\Theta$. Suppose these hold.
Let $\mathcal N$ be the null space of $I-X$. The range of $\Theta$ lies in
$\mathcal N$, and for $v\in\mathcal N$, $\tilde Av=v-2\Theta v\in\mathcal N$,
so $\mathcal N$ and $\mathcal N^\perp$ are invariant under $\tilde A$. On
$\mathcal N$, $\tilde A=I-2\Theta\preceq I$ and $X=I$. On $\mathcal N^\perp$,
$\Theta=0$, $X^{-1}=\tilde A$, and $X\preceq I$ gives $\tilde A\succeq I$.
So $X=\Phi(\tilde A)$. Conversely, if $X=\Phi(\tilde A)$, the matrix
$\Theta=\tfrac12\sum_{\lambda_i<1}(1-\lambda_i)e_ie_i^{\mathsf T}$ is
positive semidefinite, vanishes off the eigenvectors on which $X=I$, and
satisfies $X^{-1}=\tilde A+2\Theta$. If $\nu=0$, these are the optimality
conditions of the program without the distortion constraint, whose
unique minimizer is $X=I$ because both costs decrease in the L\"owner
order. Its distortion is $D_{\max}>D$, so $\nu>0$. The minimizer is
unique by strict convexity.
\end{proof}

The second term of $\tilde A$ is the information about $T$ that the
holder's observation adds to the description, weighted by $1-\alpha$. It
equals $(1-\alpha)X^{-1}(X-X_{\mathrm e})X^{-1}$ with
$X_{\mathrm e}=\Sigma_T^{-1/2}\Se\Sigma_T^{-1/2}$. At $\alpha=1$ it vanishes,
and the theorem is reverse water-filling on the spectrum of $\tilde Q$,
the weighted rate--distortion solution. For $\alpha<1$ the holder tilts
the weight, and the error covariance is reverse water-filling on the
tilted spectrum. The decoupling, commuting, and low-distortion results
of Section~\ref{sec:commuting} are the cases in which $\tilde Q$ and the
tilt leave common subspaces invariant, in which they are diagonal in a
common basis, and in which $Q\succ0$ and no eigenvalue of $\tilde A$ is
below one. In the last case $\Phi(\tilde A)=\tilde A^{-1}$, so
$X^{-1}=\tilde A(X)$, which by the Woodbury identity is the stationarity
condition in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lowdist}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:augmented}
Let $\Sez$ be the frontier point of weight $\alpha<1$ and $\nu$ its
multiplier in \eqref{eq:tilted}. Then $\Sez$ is the rate--distortion
optimal error covariance, at its own distortion, for the weighted
quadratic distortion with weight
\[
Q'=Q+\varkappa H^{\mathsf T}\bigl(H\Sez H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U\bigr)^{-1}H,
\qquad \varkappa=\frac{1-\alpha}{2\nu},
\]
that is, for a decoder that reproduces $Y$ under the weight $W$ and the
holder's noiseless view $HT$ under the weight
$\varkappa\,\Cov(S\mid\hY)^{-1}$, with $\Cov(S\mid\hY)=H\Sez H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U$
evaluated at the frontier description itself.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In whitened coordinates the weighted rate--distortion solution for $Q'$
at a given distortion is $\Phi(2\nu'\tilde Q')$ for the level $\nu'$ that
meets it, which is unique. With $\tilde Q'=\tilde A(X)/(2\nu)$ and the
distortion $\tr(\tilde Q'X)$, the level $\nu'=\nu$ gives
$\Phi(\tilde A(X))=X$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:tilted}.
\end{proof}

So the least-leaking description at a given rate is the one a
rate--distortion encoder would produce if it also had to describe what
the holder sees. Making the description informative about $S$ is what
lowers $\ell=r-I(\hY;S)$. The weight $Q'$ depends on the frontier
point, so the corollary is an equivalence of single-letter solutions,
not a construction. It says that the optimal test channel is a weighted
rate--distortion test channel, and the operational consequences for
codes designed for $Q'$ are not developed here. The target $(Y,HT)$ need
not have full row rank, and nothing in the corollary uses it.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:scalarholder}
Suppose the holder's observation is scalar, $S=\zeta^{\mathsf T}T+U$ with
$\Var U=\sigma_U^2$, and let $\tilde\zeta=\Sigma_T^{1/2}\zeta$. Then the
frontier point of weight $\alpha$ is
$X=\Phi(2\nu\tilde Q+\omega\tilde\zeta\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T})$, where the two
scalars $\nu>0$ and
$\omega\in[(1-\alpha)/(\|\tilde\zeta\|^2+\sigma_U^2),(1-\alpha)/\sigma_U^2]$
solve
\[
\tr(\tilde QX)=D,\qquad \omega\,\bigl(\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}X\tilde\zeta+\sigma_U^2\bigr)=1-\alpha .
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
With $\tilde H=\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}$ the tilt in \eqref{eq:tilted} is
$(1-\alpha)\tilde\zeta\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}/(\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}X\tilde\zeta+\sigma_U^2)$,
and $0\preceq X\preceq I$ bounds $\omega$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:solver}
The two scalars can be found by nested one-dimensional searches. For
fixed $\nu$, the conditions $X=\Phi(2\nu\tilde Q+\omega\tilde\zeta\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T})$
and $\omega(\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}X\tilde\zeta+\sigma_U^2)=1-\alpha$ are
the optimality conditions of the strictly convex program of minimizing
the weighted objective plus $\nu\tr(\tilde QX)$ over $0\prec X\preceq I$,
so they have exactly one solution. The function
$\omega\mapsto\omega(\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}X\tilde\zeta+\sigma_U^2)-(1-\alpha)$,
with $X=\Phi(2\nu\tilde Q+\omega\tilde\zeta\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T})$, is
continuous because $\Phi$ is, negative at $\omega=0$ when $\alpha<1$, and
nonnegative at $\omega=(1-\alpha)/\sigma_U^2$. Any root gives that unique
solution, so bisection finds it. The distortion of the penalized
minimizer is nonincreasing in $\nu$ by the standard comparison of the
penalized optima at two values of $\nu$, so a second bisection matches
$D$. No convex solver is needed.
\end{remark}

For a holder of rank $r>1$ the tilt is an $r\times r$ matrix that
depends on the solution, and we do not have a closed form. The fixed
point is nevertheless reached by iterating steps that are closed form
up to a monotone one-dimensional search for the level.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mm}
Let $\mathcal P=\{0\prec X\preceq I,\ \tr(\tilde QX)\le D\}$, let $X_0=I$, and
for $t\ge0$ let $X_{t+1}$ be the minimizer over $\mathcal P$ of the
surrogate $g_t$ defined in the proof. Then
\[
X_{t+1}=\Phi\bigl(2\nu_t\tilde Q+T_t\bigr),\qquad
T_t=(1-\alpha)\tilde H^{\mathsf T}\bigl(\tilde HX_t\tilde H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U\bigr)^{-1}\tilde H,
\]
where $\nu_t\ge0$ is the multiplier of the distortion constraint, with
$\nu_t=0$ if $\tr(\tilde Q\Phi(T_t))\le D$, and otherwise $\nu_t$ is a level
with $\tr(\tilde QX_{t+1})=D$. The weighted cost is nonincreasing along
$(X_t)_{t\ge1}$, $X_{t+1}-X_t\to0$, and $X_t$ converges to the frontier
point of weight $\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write the cost as
$f(X)=-\tfrac12\ln\det X+\tfrac{1-\alpha}2\ln\det M(X)$ with
$M(X)=\tilde HX\tilde H^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U\succ0$. The second term is concave
in $X\succeq0$, so it lies below its tangent at $X_t$, and
\[
g_t(X)=-\tfrac12\ln\det X+\tfrac12\tr(T_tX)+c_t,\qquad
c_t=\tfrac{1-\alpha}2\bigl[\ln\det M(X_t)-\tr\bigl(\tilde H^{\mathsf T}M(X_t)^{-1}\tilde HX_t\bigr)\bigr],
\]
satisfies $g_t\ge f$ on $X\succeq0$, $g_t(X_t)=f(X_t)$, and
$\nabla g_t(X_t)=\nabla f(X_t)$. The function $g_t$ is strictly convex, so
its minimizer over $\mathcal P$ exists and is unique ($\epsilon I$ is
strictly feasible and $-\ln\det$ diverges at the boundary). Its optimality
conditions are $X^{-1}=2\nu\tilde Q+T_t+2\Theta$ with $\nu\ge0$,
$\Theta\succeq0$, $\Theta(I-X)=0$, and $\nu(\tr(\tilde QX)-D)=0$. The
invariant-subspace argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:tilted} uses only these
relations, so $X_{t+1}=\Phi(2\nu_t\tilde Q+T_t)$. The same argument shows
that $\Phi(2\nu\tilde Q+T_t)$ minimizes $g_t+\nu\tr(\tilde QX)$ over
$0\prec X\preceq I$, so comparing the minimizers at two levels shows that
its distortion is nonincreasing in $\nu$, and it is continuous because
$\Phi$ is a spectral function of the continuous map
$\lambda\mapsto\min\{1,1/\lambda\}$. Hence $\nu_t$ is as stated. The level
need not be unique, but every admissible level gives the same
$X_{t+1}$, the unique minimizer of $g_t$.

\emph{Monotonicity.} For $t\ge1$, $X_t\in\mathcal P$ by construction, so
$f(X_{t+1})\le g_t(X_{t+1})\le g_t(X_t)=f(X_t)$. The start $X_0=I$ is not
feasible and serves only to define $T_0$.

\emph{Compactness.} Since $\min\{1,1/\lambda\}\le2/(1+\lambda)$ for
$\lambda\ge0$, $\Phi(A)\preceq2(A+I)^{-1}$, and for $A=2\nu\tilde Q+T$ with
$T\succeq0$ this is at most $2(2\nu\tilde Q+I)^{-1}$. So
$\tr(\tilde Q\Phi(2\nu\tilde Q+T))\le\sum_i2q_i/(2\nu q_i+1)$, with $q_i$ the
eigenvalues of $\tilde Q$, uniformly in $T$, and $\nu_t\le\bar\nu$, the
level at which this bound equals $D$. With
$T_t\preceq\bar T=(1-\alpha)\tilde H^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_U^{-1}\tilde H$, every
eigenvalue of $2\nu_t\tilde Q+T_t$ is at most
$\Lambda=\lambda_{\max}(2\bar\nu\tilde Q+\bar T)$, so $X_{t+1}\succeq\delta I$
with $\delta=\min\{1,1/\Lambda\}$. The iterates lie in the compact set
$\mathcal P\cap\{X\succeq\delta I\}$.

\emph{Sufficient decrease.} On $\{0\prec X\preceq I\}$ the second
derivative of $-\tfrac12\ln\det X$ in a direction $V$ is
$\tfrac12\tr(X^{-1}VX^{-1}V)\ge\tfrac12\|V\|_F^2$, so $g_t$ is strongly
convex there with modulus $\tfrac12$, and the first-order optimality of
$X_{t+1}$ over the convex set $\mathcal P$ gives
$g_t(X_t)-g_t(X_{t+1})\ge\tfrac14\|X_t-X_{t+1}\|_F^2$. Hence
$f(X_t)-f(X_{t+1})\ge\tfrac14\|X_t-X_{t+1}\|_F^2$, the series of these
terms is bounded by $4(f(X_1)-\min_{\mathcal P}f)$, and $X_{t+1}-X_t\to0$.

\emph{Limit.} Let $X_{t_k}\to\bar X$. Then $X_{t_k+1}\to\bar X$, and along
a further subsequence $\nu_{t_k}\to\nu^\ast\in[0,\bar\nu]$. The matrices
$\Theta_k=\tfrac12(X_{t_k+1}^{-1}-2\nu_{t_k}\tilde Q-T_{t_k})\succeq0$ satisfy
$\Theta_k(I-X_{t_k+1})=0$, and $T_t$ depends continuously on $X_t$, so
$\Theta_k$ converges to some $\bar\Theta\succeq0$ with
$\bar X^{-1}=2\nu^\ast\tilde Q+T(\bar X)+2\bar\Theta$, $\bar\Theta(I-\bar X)=0$,
and $\nu^\ast(\tr(\tilde Q\bar X)-D)=0$. These are the optimality
conditions of $f$ over $\mathcal P$ from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tilted}, so $\bar X$ is the frontier point, which is
unique, and $\nu^\ast>0$ because $\nu^\ast=0$ would make $\bar X=I$, whose
distortion $D_{\max}$ exceeds $D$. Every limit point of the bounded
sequence is that point, so the sequence converges.
\end{proof}

Each step is a weighted reverse water-filling and a
majorization--minimization step, the convex--concave procedure applied
to the concave log-determinant term
\cite{yuille2003,lipp2016,hunter2004,sun2017}. Like a Blahut--Arimoto
update it is explicit apart from a scalar search and decreases the cost
monotonically. In 40 random instances with described variables and
holders of rank between two and six in non-commuting geometry, the cost
never increased beyond round-off (largest increase
$2.7\times10^{-15}$), the distortion constraint was active at all 1023
steps (no step had $\nu_t=0$), the relative change in the cost fell
below $10^{-9}$ in a median of 6 and at most 21 steps, and the limit
agreed with an independent determinant program to $3.3\times10^{-8}$ in
cost (one reference solve was reported as possibly inaccurate by the
solver).

So for a scalar holder, the case of the scalar and coupled instances
and of Appendix~\ref{sec:quant}, the whole frontier, partial description
included, is the fixed point of reverse water-filling on a rank-one tilt
of the decoder's weight, computed as in Remark~\ref{rem:solver}. As
intuition for why the projection direction turns, note that a single party has
$\omega=0$ and water-fills on the fixed eigenvectors of $\tilde Q$, while
the holder adds $\omega\tilde\zeta\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}$, and as the
budget moves, the ratio $\omega/\nu$ moves and the eigenvectors of
$2\nu\tilde Q+\omega\tilde\zeta\tilde\zeta^{\mathsf T}$ rotate between those
of $\tilde Q$ and the direction $\tilde\zeta$. In the coupled instance of
Section~\ref{sec:model}, the two-scalar solution agrees with the
determinant program at $D=0.3$, $0.6$, $0.9$, and $1.2$, with a
two-dimensional description at the first three budgets and a
one-dimensional one at the last.

\section{Explicit Cases}
\label{sec:commuting}

Use the whitened coordinates $X$ and $\tilde Q$ of Section~\ref{sec:tilted}, and write
$\tilde J=\Sigma_T^{1/2}J\Sigma_T^{1/2}$. Since
$\Kc=\Sigma_T^{1/2}(I+\tilde J)^{-1}\Sigma_T^{1/2}$ and
$\Sez^{-1}+J=\Sigma_T^{-1/2}(X^{-1}+\tilde J)\Sigma_T^{-1/2}$, the costs and
the constraint of Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior} become
\begin{equation}
r=-\tfrac12\log\det X,\qquad
\ell=\tfrac12\log\det(X^{-1}+\tilde J)-\tfrac12\log\det(I+\tilde J),\qquad
\tr(\tilde QX)\le D,\qquad 0\prec X\preceq I .
\label{eq:whitened}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:decouple}
Suppose $\R^p=\bigoplus_k\mathcal V_k$ is an orthogonal decomposition into
subspaces invariant under both $\tilde Q$ and $\tilde J$. Then for every
$\alpha\in[0,1]$ the minimizer of Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}(a), written in
the coordinate $X$, is block-diagonal with respect to the decomposition,
and the weighted program splits into one program per block, coupled only
through an allocation $D=\sum_kD_k$ that equalizes the slopes of the
blocks' optimal values.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $P_k$ be the orthogonal projector onto $\mathcal V_k$, and for signs
$\epsilon_k\in\{\pm1\}$ let $S_\epsilon=\sum_k\epsilon_kP_k$, an orthogonal
symmetric matrix that commutes with $\tilde Q$ and $\tilde J$. The map
$X\mapsto S_\epsilon XS_\epsilon$ preserves $\log\det X$,
$\log\det(X^{-1}+\tilde J)$ because
$(S_\epsilon XS_\epsilon)^{-1}+\tilde J=S_\epsilon(X^{-1}+\tilde J)S_\epsilon$,
the distortion $\tr(\tilde QS_\epsilon XS_\epsilon)=\tr(\tilde QX)$, and the
constraint $X\preceq I$. The minimizer is unique, so it is fixed by every
$S_\epsilon$, and then $P_kXP_l=\epsilon_k\epsilon_lP_kXP_l$ for all signs,
which forces $P_kXP_l=0$ for $k\ne l$. For block-diagonal $X$ both costs
and the distortion are sums over blocks, and the remaining problem is a
separable convex program with one linear constraint, whose optimality
condition is a common subgradient level.
\end{proof}

The independent components of Section~\ref{sec:parallel} are the case of
two-dimensional blocks, each holding one described coordinate and its
context. When the blocks are one-dimensional, which happens exactly when
$\tilde Q$ and $\tilde J$ commute, the whole region is explicit.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:commuting}
Suppose $Q\Sigma_TJ=J\Sigma_TQ$, equivalently $\tilde Q\tilde J=\tilde J\tilde Q$,
and let $u_1,\dots,u_p$ be a common orthonormal eigenbasis with
$\tilde Qu_i=q_iu_i$ and $\tilde Ju_i=j_iu_i$. For $\alpha\in[0,1]$ the
frontier point of weight $\alpha$ is
$X^\star=\sum_ix_iu_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$, where $x_i=1$ if $q_i=0$ and
otherwise $x_i=\min\{1,\xi_i(\theta)\}$, with $\xi_i(\theta)$ the positive
root of
\begin{equation}
\theta q_ij_i\,x^2+(\theta q_i-\alpha j_i)\,x-1=0
\label{eq:commroot}
\end{equation}
($\xi_i=1/(\theta q_i)$ when $j_i=0$), and $\theta>0$ the unique level with
$\sum_iq_ix_i=D$. Its rate and leakage are
\[
R=\sum_i\tfrac12\log\frac1{x_i},
\qquad
L=\sum_i\tfrac12\log\frac{1+j_ix_i}{x_i(1+j_i)} .
\]
In particular the minimum leakage takes
$x_i=\min\bigl\{1,(\sqrt{1+4j_i/(\theta q_i)}-1)/(2j_i)\bigr\}$, read as
$x_i=\min\{1,1/(\theta q_i)\}$ when $j_i=0$ (the limit as $j_i\to0$), and direction
$i$ is described exactly when $\theta>(1+\alpha j_i)/(q_i(1+j_i))$, so the
leakage-optimal description begins with the directions of largest
$q_i(1+j_i)$, the rate-optimal one with those of largest $q_i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:decouple} with the one-dimensional blocks spanned by
the $u_i$, the minimizer is $\sum_ix_iu_iu_i^{\mathsf T}$ and the program is
$\min\sum_i\phi_i(x_i)$ subject to $\sum_iq_ix_i\le D$ and $0<x_i\le1$, with
$\phi_i(x)=\tfrac\alpha2\ln\frac1x+\tfrac{1-\alpha}2\ln\frac{1+j_ix}{x(1+j_i)}$
in natural units. Each $\phi_i$ is convex and decreasing, with
$-\phi_i'(x)=(1+\alpha j_ix)/(2x(1+j_ix))$. A direction with $q_i=0$ does
not enter the constraint, so $x_i=1$. For the others the optimality
condition is $-\phi_i'(x_i)=\tfrac12\theta q_i$ when $x_i<1$ and
$-\phi_i'(1)\ge\tfrac12\theta q_i$ when $x_i=1$, for a common $\theta>0$.
The first is \eqref{eq:commroot}, whose roots have product
$-1/(\theta q_ij_i)<0$, so exactly one is positive, and the second
reads $\theta\le(1+\alpha j_i)/(q_i(1+j_i))$. Each $x_i(\theta)$ is
continuous and nonincreasing, strictly decreasing where it is below one,
so $\sum_iq_ix_i(\theta)$ decreases continuously and strictly from
$\sum_iq_i>D$ and $\theta$ is unique. The formulas for $R$ and $L$ are
\eqref{eq:whitened} at a diagonal $X$.
\end{proof}

In each direction the leakage
$\tfrac12\log[(1+j_ix_i)/(x_i(1+j_i))]$ is Steinberg's scalar Gaussian
common-reconstruction function \cite{steinberg2009} at unit variance,
distortion $x_i$, and squared correlation $j_i/(1+j_i)$ between the
direction and the holder's observation. The theorem is the allocation
of the distortion budget among such problems, and the joint frontier
that goes with it.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:commmis}
In commuting geometry every optimal description, at every budget and
weight, reads the fixed directions $u_i$, consistent with
Theorem~\ref{thm:turning}. The rate-optimal and leakage-optimal
descriptions nonetheless differ whenever two directions described by the
rate-optimal one have different ratios $j_i/q_i$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is Theorem~\ref{thm:commuting}. If the two optimal
descriptions coincided, they would describe the same directions with
the same $x_i$. For those directions $x_i=1/(\theta_Rq_i)$ and
$x_i(1+j_ix_i)=1/(\theta_Lq_i)$, so $1+j_ix_i=\theta_R/\theta_L$ is the same
for all of them, and $j_ix_i=j_i/(\theta_Rq_i)$ forces $j_i/q_i$ to be the
same.
\end{proof}

When the decoder reproduces the whole source, the same per-direction
formula holds without commuting geometry at every small budget, in the
basis of the pencil $(J,Q)$ instead of the common eigenbasis.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lowdist}
Suppose $Q\succ0$, and let
$\Pi=Q^{-1/2}JQ^{-1/2}=\sum_i\pi_iw_iw_i^{\mathsf T}$ with orthonormal
$w_i$. For $\alpha\in[0,1]$ and $\theta>0$ let $\xi_i(\theta)$ be the
positive root of
\begin{equation}
\theta\pi_i\,\xi^2+(\theta-\alpha\pi_i)\,\xi-1=0
\label{eq:lowroot}
\end{equation}
($\xi_i=1/\theta$ when $\pi_i=0$), let $\theta(D)$ be the unique level
with $\sum_i\xi_i=D$, and let
$\Sez(D)=Q^{-1/2}\bigl(\sum_i\xi_iw_iw_i^{\mathsf T}\bigr)Q^{-1/2}$. Then
$\Sez(D)$ is increasing in $D$ in the L\"owner order, the set of $D$ with
$\Sez(D)\preceq\Sigma_T$ is an interval $(0,D_{\mathrm{int}}(\alpha)]$
with $D_{\mathrm{int}}(\alpha)>0$, and on it $\Sez(D)$ is the frontier
point of weight $\alpha$, with
\[
R=\tfrac12\log\det(Q\Sigma_T)-\sum_i\tfrac12\log\xi_i,
\qquad
L=\tfrac12\log\det(Q\Kc)+\sum_i\tfrac12\log\frac{1+\pi_i\xi_i}{\xi_i} .
\]
No commuting assumption is made, and whether a budget lies in the
interval is a single test of the L\"owner order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the coordinate $\Xi=Q^{1/2}\Sez Q^{1/2}$ the distortion is $\tr\Xi$,
and $\Se^{-1}=Q^{1/2}(\Xi^{-1}+\Pi)Q^{1/2}$, which gives the formulas for
$R$ and $L$ at $\Xi=\sum_i\xi_iw_iw_i^{\mathsf T}$. When the constraint
$\Sez\preceq\Sigma_T$ is slack, its multiplier vanishes, and the
stationarity condition in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:rank},
multiplied on the right by $\Sez$, reads
$\alpha I+(1-\alpha)(I+J\Sez)^{-1}=2\nu Q\Sez$, which in the coordinate
$\Xi$ is
\[
\alpha I+(1-\alpha)(I+\Pi\Xi)^{-1}=\theta\Xi,\qquad \theta=2\nu .
\]
For $\alpha<1$ the right side is symmetric, so $I+\Pi\Xi$ is symmetric
and $\Pi\Xi=\Xi\Pi$. For $\alpha=1$ the condition is $\Xi=I/\theta$.
Either way $\Xi$ leaves each eigenspace of $\Pi$ invariant, and on the
eigenspace of $\pi_i$ every eigenvalue $\xi$ of $\Xi$ satisfies
$\alpha+(1-\alpha)/(1+\pi_i\xi)=\theta\xi$, which is
\eqref{eq:lowroot}. Its left side decreases and its right side increases
in $\xi>0$, so the positive root is unique and decreases in $\theta$.
Hence $\Xi$ is a multiple of the identity on each eigenspace of $\Pi$
and diagonal in any eigenbasis $w_i$, $\theta(D)$ is unique, each
$\xi_i$ increases in $D$, and so does $\Sez(D)$, by congruence. As
$D\to0$, $\Sez(D)\to0\prec\Sigma_T$. When $\Sez(D)\preceq\Sigma_T$, the
point satisfies the Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions with zero multiplier
for the cap, which suffice by convexity, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}(a) makes it the unique minimizer.
\end{proof}

When $Q\Sigma_TJ=J\Sigma_TQ$ and no cap of Theorem~\ref{thm:commuting} is
active, the two results agree, with $\xi_i=q_ix_i$ and $\pi_i=j_i/q_i$.
When $Q$ is singular, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank} shows that the cap is
always active, and the error covariance equals $\Sigma_T$ on a subspace
of dimension at least $p-\operatorname{rank}Q$.

\section{The One-Dimensional Case}
\label{sec:onedim}

Write $\Delta=D_{\max}-D$ for the reduction in distortion the description
must buy, and
\[
A(\Delta)=\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_T-\Delta\,\Sigma_T .
\]
A one-dimensional description is $Z=b^{\mathsf T}T+N$ with $b\ne0$ and
$N\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ independent, reproduced by $\E[Y\mid Z]$.
Its projection direction is $b$, and the direction in the space of the
described variable along which it reduces the error is $F\Sigma_Tb$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:onedim}
(a) A one-dimensional description meets the distortion constraint with
finite leakage if and only if
$\Delta<\lambda_{\max}(W^{1/2}\Sigma_YW^{1/2})$, where
$\Sigma_Y=F\Sigma_TF^{\mathsf T}$, and the least leakage among such
descriptions is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)=\tfrac12\log\Bigl(1+\frac{\Delta}{\mu_{\max}(\Delta)}\Bigr),
\label{eq:onedim}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{\max}(\Delta)>0$ is the largest root of
$\det\bigl(A(\Delta)-\mu\Kc\bigr)=0$. It is attained with $b$ a
corresponding generalized eigenvector and
$\sigma^2=b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_Tb/\Delta-b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb$, at
which the distortion constraint is active.

(b) Suppose $\Delta<\lambda_{\max}(W^{1/2}\Sigma_YW^{1/2})$ and that
$\mu_{\max}(\Delta)$ is simple (if it is not, two distinct one-dimensional
optima exist, their midpoint in the coordinate $\Sez$ is strictly better by
Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}, and $L(D)<L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)$). Let $\Sez$ and
$\Se$ be the error covariances of the description of (a), and let
$\Gamma(\varpi)=\tfrac12\Se-\varpi\,\Sez Q\Sez$. Then $L(D)=L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)$
if and only if there is $\varpi\ge0$ with $\Gamma(\varpi)\succeq0$ and
$\Gamma(\varpi)b=0$, and then
$\varpi=\tfrac12b^{\mathsf T}\Se b/(b^{\mathsf T}\Sez Q\Sez b)$.

(c) If the reproduction is scalar ($m=1$), then $L(D)=L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)$
for every $0<D<D_{\max}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) For the description $Z$,
$\Cov(T\mid Z)=\Sigma_T-\Sigma_Tbb^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_T/(b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb+\sigma^2)$,
so the distortion of $\E[Y\mid Z]$ is
$D_{\max}-b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_Tb/(b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb+\sigma^2)$,
and the constraint reads
$\sigma^2\le b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_Tb/\Delta-b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb$.
The leakage
$I(T;Z\mid S)=\tfrac12\log\bigl(1+b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b/\sigma^2\bigr)$
decreases in $\sigma^2$, so at the largest admissible $\sigma^2$, where
the constraint is active, it equals
$\tfrac12\log\bigl(1+\Delta\,b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b/b^{\mathsf T}A(\Delta)b\bigr)$,
finite exactly when $b^{\mathsf T}A(\Delta)b>0$. Minimizing over $b$ is
maximizing the Rayleigh quotient $b^{\mathsf T}A(\Delta)b/b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b$
of a symmetric pencil with $\Kc\succ0$, whose maximum is $\mu_{\max}$.
Some $b$ has $b^{\mathsf T}A(\Delta)b>0$ if and only if
$\Sigma_T^{1/2}Q\Sigma_T^{1/2}\not\preceq\Delta I$, and the nonzero
eigenvalues of that matrix are those of $W^{1/2}\Sigma_YW^{1/2}$.

(b) The description of (a) has an active constraint and
$\Se^{-1}=\Kc^{-1}+bb^{\mathsf T}/\sigma^2$, so $\Kc-\Se$ is a positive
multiple of $\Kc bb^{\mathsf T}\Kc$ and $\Se^{-1}\Kc b$ is a positive
multiple of $b$. With $\Theta=\Se^{-1}\Gamma(\varpi)\Se^{-1}$, the
conditions \eqref{eq:kkt} at this point read $\Gamma(\varpi)\succeq0$ and
$\Gamma(\varpi)b=0$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}(c) makes them necessary
and sufficient. Taking $b^{\mathsf T}\Gamma(\varpi)b=0$ gives $\varpi$.

(c) By Lemma~\ref{lem:gauss} the optimum is attained by a jointly
Gaussian channel $\hY=a^{\mathsf T}T+N$. If $\Cov(Y,\hY)=0$ its
distortion is at least $D_{\max}>D$. Otherwise $\E[Y\mid\hY]$ is a
nonzero multiple of $\hY$, which leaves the leakage unchanged and does
not raise the distortion, so the optimum is a one-dimensional
description.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:scalar}
Let $T=(Y,V)$ be scalar with unit variances and correlation $\rho$,
$|\rho|<1$, let $W=1$, and let $S=V+U$ with $\Var U=\tau^2>0$, and write
$s=1+\tau^2$. For $0<D<1$,
\[
L(D)=\tfrac12\log g^\star,\qquad
g^\star=\frac{(D+s-\rho^2)+\sqrt{(D+s-\rho^2)^2-4Ds(1-\rho^2)}}{2Ds},
\]
the larger root of $P(g)=Dsg^2-(D+s-\rho^2)g+(1-\rho^2)$, attained by
$\hat Y=aY+bV+N$ with $a=(g^\star-1)/g^\star$,
$b=(g^\star-1)\rho/(g^\star(g^\star s-1))$, and the noise variance that makes
the distortion equal to $D$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(c), $L(D)=\tfrac12\log(1+(1-D)/\mu_{\max})$
with $\Delta=1-D$. Substituting $\mu=(1-D)/(g-1)$ into
$\det(A(1-D)-\mu\Kc)$, with $\Sigma_T=\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\rho\\ \rho&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$,
$Q=e_1e_1^{\mathsf T}$, and $J=e_2e_2^{\mathsf T}/\tau^2$, gives
$-(1-D)(1-\rho^2)P(g)/(s(g-1)^2)$, a direct computation confirmed by
computer algebra (Appendix~\ref{app:numver}). Since $P(1)=(D-1)\tau^2<0$
and $P(0)=1-\rho^2>0$, $P$ has one root in $(0,1)$, which corresponds to
a negative $\mu$, and one root $g^\star>1$, which corresponds to the
unique positive root $\mu_{\max}$. The projection direction is the
corresponding generalized eigenvector. Normalizing the reproduction
gives the stated coefficients.
\end{proof}

For a scalar described variable the whole frontier is explicit up to
one scalar root, with any context and any holder.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:scalarpath}
Let $m=1$, write $Q=qq^{\mathsf T}$, and for $\varrho\in[0,1]$ let
\[
\Sigma_\varrho=(1-\varrho)\Sigma_T+\varrho\Kc,\qquad
b(\varrho)=\frac{\Sigma_\varrho^{-1}\Sigma_Tq}{q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_T\Sigma_\varrho^{-1}\Sigma_Tq},
\]
$\mathsf u(\varrho)=b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb$, and $\mathsf v(\varrho)=b^{\mathsf T}(\Sigma_T-\Kc)b$.
For $\alpha\in[0,1]$ and $0<D<D_{\max}$, the frontier point of weight
$\alpha$, the minimizer of $\alpha R+(1-\alpha)L$, is the one-dimensional
description $b(\varrho)^{\mathsf T}T+N$ with $\Var N=(1-\Delta\mathsf u)/\Delta$,
where $\varrho$ is a root of
\begin{equation}
\varrho\bigl(1-\Delta\mathsf v(\varrho)\bigr)=(1-\alpha)\bigl(1-\Delta\mathsf u(\varrho)\bigr)
\label{eq:scalarpath}
\end{equation}
in $[0,1-\alpha]$, the one of least weighted cost. Its rate and leakage
are
\[
R=-\tfrac12\log\bigl(1-\Delta\mathsf u\bigr),\qquad
L=R+\tfrac12\log\bigl(1-\Delta\mathsf v\bigr)=\tfrac12\log\frac{1-\alpha}{\varrho}\quad(\alpha<1).
\]
The roots are finitely many, every root is a feasible description, and
at $\alpha=0$ the root is unique. At $\alpha=1$ the root is $\varrho=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:rank} the minimizer is a one-dimensional
description $b^{\mathsf T}T+N$ (rank zero cannot reduce the distortion),
at the active noise level by Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}(a). A direction with
$q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb=0$ reduces no distortion, so normalize
$q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb=1$. From the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(a)
the active noise variance is $\sigma^2=b^{\mathsf T}A(\Delta)b/\Delta=(1-\Delta\mathsf u)/\Delta$,
so the rate is $\tfrac12\log(1+\mathsf u/\sigma^2)=-\tfrac12\log(1-\Delta\mathsf u)$,
and the leakage is
$\tfrac12\log\bigl(1+b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b/\sigma^2\bigr)=R+\tfrac12\log(1-\Delta\mathsf v)$,
since $b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b=\mathsf u-\mathsf v$. The weighted cost
$-\tfrac12\ln(1-\Delta\mathsf u)+\tfrac{1-\alpha}2\ln(1-\Delta\mathsf v)$ is smooth
on the open set where $1-\Delta\mathsf u>0$, the minimizer lies in it, and
stationarity on the hyperplane $q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb=1$ gives
$\Sigma_Tb-\varrho(\Sigma_T-\Kc)b\propto\Sigma_Tq$ with
$\varrho=(1-\alpha)(1-\Delta\mathsf u)/(1-\Delta\mathsf v)$, which is
\eqref{eq:scalarpath} and $b=b(\varrho)$, with $\Sigma_\varrho\succ0$. Since
$\Kc\succ0$, $\mathsf u-\mathsf v=b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b>0$. So for $\alpha<1$ a
root has $1-\Delta\mathsf v>1-\Delta\mathsf u$ and both factors positive,
hence $0<\varrho<1-\alpha$, and every root is a feasible description,
while $\alpha=1$ forces $\varrho=0$. The equation is rational in $\varrho$
and not identically zero, since at $\varrho=0$ its right side is
$(1-\alpha)D/D_{\max}>0$. So the roots are finitely many, and distinct
roots give distinct descriptions, so the root of least cost is the
unique minimizer. At a root, $(1-\Delta\mathsf v)/(1-\Delta\mathsf u)=(1-\alpha)/\varrho$,
which gives the last form of $L$.

For $\alpha=0$, put $\mu=\Delta\varrho/(1-\varrho)$, so
$\Sigma_\varrho\propto\Delta\Sigma_T+\mu\Kc$ and
$(\Delta\Sigma_T+\mu\Kc)b=c\,\Sigma_Tq$ for a scalar $c$. Multiplying by
$b^{\mathsf T}$ gives $c=\Delta\mathsf u+\mu(\mathsf u-\mathsf v)$, and
\eqref{eq:scalarpath} with $\alpha=0$ reduces to
$\Delta\mathsf u+\mu(\mathsf u-\mathsf v)=1$, that is, $c=1$. Multiplying
$b=c(\Delta\Sigma_T+\mu\Kc)^{-1}\Sigma_Tq$ by $q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_T$ shows
that $c=1$ is the secular equation \eqref{eq:secular} below, whose
positive root is unique.
\end{proof}

So every optimal description of a scalar variable, at every budget and
weight, reads a direction on one explicit curve, from $b(0)\propto q$,
which describes $Y$ itself, toward
$b(1)\propto\Kc^{-1}\Sigma_Tq=q+J\Sigma_Tq$. The rate-optimal description
has $\varrho=0$ at every budget. The leakage-optimal one moves along the
curve with the budget, with $\varrho\to1$ as $D\to D_{\max}$, where it reads
$q+J\Sigma_Tq$, and $\varrho\to0$ as $D\to0$, where it must reveal $Y$.
At $\alpha=0$ the stationarity condition is the pencil of
Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}. With $Q=qq^{\mathsf T}$ the eigenvector equation
$A(\Delta)b=\mu\Kc b$ reads $(\Delta\Sigma_T+\mu\Kc)b\propto\Sigma_Tq$, and
with $q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb=1$ it gives $1-\Delta\mathsf u=\mu(\mathsf u-\mathsf v)$
and $1-\Delta\mathsf v=(\mu+\Delta)(\mathsf u-\mathsf v)$, so
$\varrho=\mu_{\max}/(\Delta+\mu_{\max})$ and $L=\tfrac12\log(1+\Delta/\mu_{\max})$.
The root is the unique positive solution of the secular equation
\begin{equation}
q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_T\bigl(\Delta\Sigma_T+\mu\Kc\bigr)^{-1}\Sigma_Tq=1 ,
\label{eq:secular}
\end{equation}
whose left side decreases from $D_{\max}/\Delta>1$ to $0$ on $\mu>0$. In
the basis of the pencil $(\Sigma_T,\Kc)$ it is a rational equation with
$p$ poles, and for $p=2$ it is the quadratic of
Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar}.


\section{Directions and Misalignment}
\label{sec:directions}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:single}
With no holder ($J=0$), the best one-dimensional description reads the
top generalized eigenvector of the pencil $(\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_T,\Sigma_T)$
at every budget, and the rate-optimal description reverse water-fills on
the eigenvalues of $W^{1/2}\Sigma_YW^{1/2}$ along fixed directions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $J=0$, $\Kc=\Sigma_T$ and $A(\Delta)b=\mu\Sigma_Tb$ reads
$\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_Tb=(\mu+\Delta)\Sigma_Tb$, whose eigenvectors do not
depend on $\Delta$. The rate-optimal description is the weighted
quadratic rate--distortion solution \cite{sakrison1968}. In the
coordinates $W^{1/2}Y$ the distortion is squared error, and reverse
water-filling describes fixed principal components \cite{berger1971}.
\end{proof}

The Gaussian information bottleneck has the same property. Its optimal
projections are fixed eigenvectors, and its tradeoff parameter sets only
their number and scale \cite{chechik2005}. The leakage-optimal
description behaves differently.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:turning}
Let $\mu_{\max}(\Delta)$ be simple and positive on an open interval
$\mathcal I\subseteq(0,\lambda_{\max}(W^{1/2}\Sigma_YW^{1/2}))$, with
generalized eigenvector $b(\Delta)$. Then $b(\Delta)$ spans the same line
for every $\Delta\in\mathcal I$ if and only if, for some
$\Delta_0\in\mathcal I$, $b_0=b(\Delta_0)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
Q\Sigma_Tb_0=\lambda b_0
\quad\text{and}\quad
J\Sigma_Tb_0=\varsigma b_0
\label{eq:common}
\end{equation}
for scalars $\lambda>0$ and $\varsigma\ge0$, that is, if and only if $b_0$
is a common generalized eigenvector of the pencils
$(\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_T,\Sigma_T)$ and $(\Sigma_TJ\Sigma_T,\Sigma_T)$.
Otherwise $b(\Delta)$ is constant on no open subinterval of $\mathcal I$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A simple eigenvalue of a symmetric pencil with positive definite second
matrix, and its eigenvector, are analytic in the parameter
\cite{kato1995}. Write $A(\Delta)=A_0-\Delta\Sigma_T$.

Suppose $b$ is constant on an open subinterval. Differentiating
$(A_0-\Delta\Sigma_T)b=\mu_{\max}(\Delta)\Kc b$ gives
$\Sigma_Tb=c\Kc b$ for a scalar $c$, and
$c=b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb/b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b\ge1$ because
$\Kc\preceq\Sigma_T$. Then $A_0b=(\Delta+\mu_{\max}/c)\Sigma_Tb$, that is,
$Q\Sigma_Tb=\lambda b$ with $\lambda=\Delta+\mu_{\max}/c>0$.
Multiplying $\Sigma_Tb=c\Kc b$ by $\Kc^{-1}=\Sigma_T^{-1}+J$ gives
$J\Sigma_Tb=(c-1)b$, so $\varsigma=c-1\ge0$. This proves the last sentence
and the forward direction.

Conversely, if $b_0$ satisfies \eqref{eq:common}, then
$\Kc^{-1}\Sigma_Tb_0=(1+\varsigma)b_0$, so $\Sigma_Tb_0=(1+\varsigma)\Kc b_0$ and
$A(\Delta)b_0=(\lambda-\Delta)\Sigma_Tb_0=(\lambda-\Delta)(1+\varsigma)\Kc b_0$
for every $\Delta$, so $b_0$ is a generalized eigenvector with eigenvalue
$(\lambda-\Delta)(1+\varsigma)$ throughout. Let
$\mathcal E=\{\Delta\in\mathcal I:(\lambda-\Delta)(1+\varsigma)=\mu_{\max}(\Delta)\}$.
It contains $\Delta_0$ and is closed in $\mathcal I$ by continuity. It is
open, because near a point of $\mathcal E$ the simple eigenvalue
$\mu_{\max}$ is the only eigenvalue of the pencil close to it, and
$(\lambda-\Delta)(1+\varsigma)$ is an eigenvalue close to it. Since
$\mathcal I$ is connected, $\mathcal E=\mathcal I$, and by simplicity
$b(\Delta)$ spans the line of $b_0$ throughout.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:context}
Suppose the holder observes only the context, that is, in coordinates $T=(Y,V)$,
$F=[\,I\ 0\,]$ and $H=[\,0\ H_V\,]$. Then \eqref{eq:common} holds if
and only if $b_0=F^{\mathsf T}\beta$ with $W\Sigma_Y\beta=\lambda\beta$ and
$\Cov(S,\beta^{\mathsf T}Y)=0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$Q\Sigma_Tb_0=\lambda b_0$ with $\lambda>0$ puts $b_0$ in the range of
$Q$, which is the range of $F^{\mathsf T}$, so $b_0=F^{\mathsf T}\beta$
and $F^{\mathsf T}W\Sigma_Y\beta=\lambda F^{\mathsf T}\beta$. The vector
$J\Sigma_Tb_0$ lies in the range of $H^{\mathsf T}$, which meets the
range of $F^{\mathsf T}$ only at zero, so $J\Sigma_Tb_0=\varsigma b_0$
forces $J\Sigma_Tb_0=0$. With $w=\Sigma_Tb_0$, $w^{\mathsf T}Jw=0$ gives
$\Sigma_U^{-1/2}Hw=0$, that is,
$\Sigma_U^{-1}H\Sigma_TF^{\mathsf T}\beta=0$, which is
$\Cov(S,\beta^{\mathsf T}Y)=0$. The converse is direct.
\end{proof}

The same condition decides whether the two optimal descriptions coincide.
Let $b_R$ be the top generalized eigenvector of
$(\Sigma_TQ\Sigma_T,\Sigma_T)$, the projection direction of the best
one-dimensional rate description.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:misalign}
Let $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2$ be the two largest eigenvalues of
$W^{1/2}\Sigma_YW^{1/2}$, with $\lambda_2=0$ when $m=1$, and suppose $\Delta\le\lambda_1-\lambda_2$, the
range in which the rate-optimal description is one-dimensional,
$b_R^{\mathsf T}T+N$. If $J\Sigma_Tb_R$ is not parallel to $b_R$, the
rate-optimal description is not leakage-optimal and the leakage-optimal
description is not rate-optimal, so each pays a strictly positive excess
in the other cost. If $J\Sigma_Tb_R$ is parallel to $b_R$, then $b_R$ is a
generalized eigenvector of the leakage pencil at every $\Delta$, and
inside the one-dimensional range of Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim} the two
descriptions coincide exactly when it is the top one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the stated range reverse water-filling describes only the principal
component, so the rate-optimal description is the one-dimensional
description reading $b_R$, where $Q\Sigma_Tb_R=\lambda_1b_R$. If it were
leakage-optimal, then $L(D)=L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)$ and $b_R$ would maximize
the Rayleigh quotient of Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(a), hence be a
generalized eigenvector of $(A(\Delta),\Kc)$, that is,
$(\lambda_1-\Delta)\Sigma_Tb_R\propto\Kc b_R$. Multiplying by
$\Kc^{-1}=\Sigma_T^{-1}+J$ gives $J\Sigma_Tb_R\propto b_R$. So when
$J\Sigma_Tb_R$ is not parallel to $b_R$ the rate-optimal description is not
leakage-optimal, and since both minimizers of Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}(a)
are unique, the leakage-optimal description is not rate-optimal either.
The same computation read backward shows that $J\Sigma_Tb_R\propto b_R$
makes $b_R$ a generalized eigenvector at every $\Delta$, and inside the
one-dimensional range the leakage-optimal description reads the top
one.
\end{proof}

In the scalar case of Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar}, $b_R=e_1$ and
$J\Sigma_Te_1=(\rho/\tau^2)e_2$, which is parallel to $e_1$ only when
$\rho=0$. So the two descriptions differ exactly when the context is
correlated with the described variable. The rate-optimal channel is the
classical reverse channel, with leakage
$\tfrac12\log\bigl[((1-D)(1-\rho^2/s)+D)/D\bigr]$, strictly above $L(D)$,
and the leakage-optimal channel of Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar} has rate
strictly above $\tfrac12\log(1/D)$. The rate excess is unbounded toward the
clean-context boundary $\tau^2\to0$ at $D=1-\rho^2$, where the
leakage-optimal channel pins the reproduction to the context.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nonred}
There are sources for which the leakage-optimal one-dimensional projection
takes different directions at two budgets. For such a source no
description problem for a single party with a fixed weighted quadratic
distortion has the leakage-optimal descriptions as its optimal
descriptions at both budgets.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take the scalar source of Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar} with $\rho\ne0$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(c) the leakage-optimal description is the
one-dimensional description at every $D$, and its projection direction is
proportional to $(g^\star s-1,\rho)$, the ratio of the coefficients in
Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar}. Since $L(D)$ is strictly decreasing in $D$
(if $L(D_1)=L(D_2)$ with $D_1<D_2$, the unique minimizer at $D_2$ would be
the one at $D_1$, whose constraint is slack at $D_2$, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:convex}(a)), $g^\star$ is strictly decreasing in $D$, so the
projection line differs at any two budgets. By Proposition~\ref{prop:single},
applied to any fixed weight on $T$, the best one-dimensional description
for a single party reads the same line at both budgets. The coupled
instance of Section~\ref{sec:model}, whose leakage-optimal
descriptions reduce the error along $47.48^\circ$ and $41.15^\circ$ at
two budgets, illustrates the same effect in the space of the described
variable.
\end{proof}

A description whose projection direction is fixed, such as one designed
for a single party with a weighted quadratic distortion, therefore loses
something at all but isolated budgets. The loss has an explicit lower
bound. Let $\mu_1=\mu_{\max}\ge\mu_2\ge\dots$ be the roots of
$\det(A(\Delta)-\mu\Kc)=0$, and measure angles between directions in the
inner product of $\Kc$, which does not depend on the budget.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fixedread}
Let $0<\Delta<\lambda_{\max}(W^{1/2}\Sigma_YW^{1/2})$, so that $\mu_1>0$,
and let $b(\Delta)$ be a generalized eigenvector for $\mu_1$. For $b$
with $\mu_b=b^{\mathsf T}A(\Delta)b/b^{\mathsf T}\Kc b>0$, the
one-dimensional description with projection direction $b$ at the active
noise level has leakage $\ell_b(\Delta)=\tfrac12\log(1+\Delta/\mu_b)$, and
with $a=(\mu_1-\mu_2)\sin^2\psi$, where $\psi$ is the angle between $b$ and
$b(\Delta)$, we have $a<\mu_1$ and
\begin{equation}
\ell_b(\Delta)-L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)\ \ge\ \tfrac12\log\Bigl(1+\frac{\Delta\,a}{(\mu_1-a)(\mu_1+\Delta)}\Bigr),
\label{eq:fixedread}
\end{equation}
with equality when $p=2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The leakage is the one computed in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(a), where $\mu_1>0$ is also shown. In a
$\Kc$-orthonormal basis of generalized eigenvectors $v_i$, with
$v_1=b(\Delta)/\|b(\Delta)\|_{\Kc}$, write $b=\sum_ic_iv_i$. Then
$\mu_b=\sum_i\mu_ic_i^2/\sum_ic_i^2$, so
$\mu_1-\mu_b\ge(\mu_1-\mu_2)\sum_{i\ge2}c_i^2/\sum_ic_i^2=a$, with equality
when $p=2$, and $a\le\mu_1-\mu_b<\mu_1$. Finally
$(1+\Delta/\mu_b)/(1+\Delta/\mu_1)=1+\Delta x/((\mu_1-x)(\mu_1+\Delta))$ with
$x=\mu_1-\mu_b$, which increases in $x\in[a,\mu_1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:forced}
Let $\Delta_1$ and $\Delta_2$ satisfy the hypotheses of
Proposition~\ref{prop:fixedread}, and let $\psi_{12}$ be the angle
between $b(\Delta_1)$ and $b(\Delta_2)$. Every one-dimensional
description with the same projection direction at both budgets has, at
one of them, excess leakage over $L_{\mathrm{1d}}$ at least
\[
\min_{i=1,2}\ \tfrac12\log\Bigl(1+\frac{\Delta_i\,a_i}{(\mu_1-a_i)(\mu_1+\Delta_i)}\Bigr),
\qquad a_i=(\mu_1-\mu_2)\sin^2(\psi_{12}/2),
\]
with $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$ evaluated at $\Delta_i$, and a term read as
$+\infty$ when $a_i\ge\mu_1$. If $\mu_{\max}$ is simple on an open
interval containing $\Delta_1$, then unless $b(\Delta)$ is constant there
(Theorem~\ref{thm:turning}), $\psi_{12}>0$ for all but isolated $\Delta_2$
in that interval, by analyticity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The angle between lines is a metric on the projective space of the
$\Kc$ inner product, so the angles from $b$ to $b(\Delta_1)$ and to
$b(\Delta_2)$ sum to at least $\psi_{12}$, and one of them is at least
$\psi_{12}/2$. At that budget, a direction whose angle gives $a\ge\mu_1$
has $\mu_b\le0$ and infinite leakage, and otherwise the bound
\eqref{eq:fixedread} increases with $\sin^2\psi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$.
\end{proof}

Since $L(D)\le L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)$, the bounds are also lower bounds on
the excess over the least leakage of any description, and they bound
that excess exactly where $L(D)=L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D)$, at every budget for a
scalar described variable and inside the certificate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(b) in general. The bounds need no search. The
two-budget values below were found by search over directions. In the
coupled instance the rate-optimal description, whose projection
direction is fixed, pays $0.139$ bits of excess leakage at $D=1.1$
(bound $0.098$) and $0.011$ bits at $D=1.38$ (bound $0.010$). For the
two budgets $D=1.38$ and $1.1$, whose optimal projection directions are
$24.8^\circ$ apart in the $\Kc$ metric, the best single fixed direction
pays only $0.0003$ bits at the worse of them. In this instance the
excess comes from the choice of direction, and little from holding it
fixed. A less noisy context changes this. For the scalar source with
$(\rho^2,\tau^2)=(0.75,0.05)$ and budgets $D=0.1$ and $0.7$, the optimal
directions are $49.8^\circ$ apart in the $\Kc$ metric, and every fixed
direction pays at least $0.0245$ bits at one budget (bound $0.0048$),
more than the least leakage $0.0216$ bits at $D=0.7$. With
$(\rho^2,\tau^2)=(0.5,10^{-3})$ and $D=0.2$ and $0.8$, the directions are
$84.0^\circ$ apart and every fixed direction pays at least $0.0386$
bits (bound $0.0004$), against a least leakage of $0.0005$ bits at
$D=0.8$.

\section{Independent Components}
\label{sec:parallel}

Suppose $Y=(Y_1,\dots,Y_m)$, $W$ is diagonal with entries $w_j$, and each
$Y_j$ has its own context $V_j$ and holder observation $S_j=V_j+U_j$, the
triples $(Y_j,V_j,S_j)$ being independent, with $\Var Y_j=\sigma_j^2$,
$\Var V_j=1$, $\E[Y_jV_j]=\rho_j\sigma_j$, $|\rho_j|<1$, and
$\Var U_j=\tau_j^2>0$. Absorb $w_j$ into $\sigma_j^2$. Write $\ell_j$ for
the scalar function $\delta\mapsto\tfrac12\log g^\star(\delta)$ of
Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar} at $(\rho_j,\tau_j^2)$, extended by zero for
$\delta\ge1$, and $L_j(D_j)=\ell_j(D_j/\sigma_j^2)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:slope}
For $0<\delta<1$, with $g=g^\star(\delta)$, $s=1+\tau^2$, $k=gs-1$, and
$\Delta_\delta=\sqrt{(\delta+s-\rho^2)^2-4\delta s(1-\rho^2)}>0$,
\[
\ell'(\delta)=-\frac{k}{2\ln2\,\Delta_\delta},
\qquad
\ell''(\delta)=\frac{(1-\rho^2)k^3
+\rho^2\tau^2g^2\bigl(\tau^4g+\tau^2(2g+1)+g+1\bigr)}{2\ln2\;g\,\Delta_\delta^3}>0,
\]
so $\ell$ is strictly convex and decreasing, $\ell'\to-\infty$ as
$\delta\to0$, and $\ell'\to-\tau^2/(2\ln2(\rho^2+\tau^2))$ as $\delta\to1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The larger root is simple, with $\partial_gP=\Delta_\delta$ there, so
$g^\star$ is smooth. Differentiating $P(g^\star(\delta))=0$ with
$\partial_\delta P=gk$ gives $g'=-gk/\Delta_\delta$ and the first formula.
Differentiating once more and eliminating $\delta$ through
$\delta gk=(s-\rho^2)g-(1-\rho^2)$ gives the second, an identity of
polynomials confirmed by computer algebra (Appendix~\ref{app:numver}).
Its numerator is positive. The limits follow from $g^\star\to\infty$,
$\Delta_\delta\to s-\rho^2$ as $\delta\to0$ and $g^\star\to1$, $k\to\tau^2$,
$\Delta_\delta\to\rho^2+\tau^2$ as $\delta\to1$.
\end{proof}

Define the activation level
$\kappa_j=-L_j'(\sigma_j^2-)=\tau_j^2/(2\ln2\,\sigma_j^2(\rho_j^2+\tau_j^2))$.
For the rate it would be $1/(2\ln2\,\sigma_j^2)$.

These components are two-dimensional blocks of Theorem~\ref{thm:decouple},
each solved by Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:parallel}
(a) $\RW(D)$ is the union, over $D_j\ge0$ with $\sum_jD_j\le D$, of the
Minkowski sums of the scalar regions of the components, attained by
product channels.
(b) $L(D)=\min\sum_jL_j(D_j)$ over such allocations, with a unique
minimizer $D_j=D_j(\vartheta^\star)$, where $D_j(\vartheta)=\sigma_j^2$ for
$\vartheta\le\kappa_j$ and otherwise solves $-L_j'(D_j)=\vartheta$, and
$\vartheta^\star$ is the unique level with $\sum_jD_j(\vartheta^\star)=D$.
(c) As $D$ decreases, the leakage-optimal description begins to describe
component $j$ in decreasing order of $\sigma_j^2(1+\rho_j^2/\tau_j^2)$,
while the rate-optimal description proceeds in decreasing order of
$\sigma_j^2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) By Theorem~\ref{thm:op} it suffices to identify the single-letter
union. For an admissible channel with $D_j=\E(Y_j-\hat Y_j)^2$, each
induced channel $p(\hat y_j\mid t_j)$ satisfies $\hat Y_j-T_j-S_j$, and
since the pairs $(T_j,S_j)$ are independent the chain rule gives
$I(T;\hY)\ge\sum_jI(T_j;\hat Y_j)$ and
$I(T;\hY\mid S)=\sum_j[I(T_j;\hY,S,T^{j-1})-I(T_j;S_j)]\ge\sum_jI(T_j;\hat Y_j\mid S_j)$.
A product of component channels attains the sum with equality.
(b) Each $L_j$ is convex and nonincreasing, strictly convex on
$(0,\sigma_j^2)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:slope}, with left derivative
$-\kappa_j$ at $\sigma_j^2$. An allocation is optimal if and only if some
$\vartheta\ge0$ lies in $-\partial L_j(D_j)$ for every $j$ with
$\vartheta(\sum_jD_j-D)=0$. Since $D<\sum_j\sigma_j^2$, $\vartheta>0$. No $D_j$
is $0$ because $-L_j'\to\infty$ there. The sum $\sum_jD_j(\vartheta)$ decreases
continuously and strictly from $\sum_j\sigma_j^2$ at $\min_j\kappa_j$ to
$0$, so $\vartheta^\star$ is unique. (c) $D_j(\vartheta)<\sigma_j^2$ exactly when
$\vartheta>\kappa_j$, and $\vartheta^\star$ increases as $D$ decreases.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:disjoint}
If $m=2$, $\sigma_1^2>\sigma_2^2$, and
$\sigma_1^2(1+\rho_1^2/\tau_1^2)<\sigma_2^2(1+\rho_2^2/\tau_2^2)$, then for every
$D\in[\max\{2\sigma_2^2,\sigma_1^2+D_2(\kappa_1)\},\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)$ the
rate-optimal description describes component $1$ alone and the
leakage-optimal description describes component $2$ alone.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Reverse water-filling describes component $1$ alone exactly for
$2\sigma_2^2\le D<\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:parallel}(b)
describes component $2$ alone exactly when $\kappa_2<\vartheta^\star\le\kappa_1$,
that is, for $\sigma_1^2+D_2(\kappa_1)\le D<\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Decoder Side Information}
\label{sec:decsi}

Suppose the decoder also observes $C=H_CT+N_C$, with
$N_C\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{N_C})$, $\Sigma_{N_C}\succ0$, independent of $T$,
$U$, and all coding randomness, and reconstructs $\hY^n=g_n(M,C^n)$. The
encoder does not see $C$. Rate, distortion, and leakage
$L_n=\tfrac1nH(M\mid S^n)$ are as in Section~\ref{sec:model}. Write
$J_S:=J$ for the holder's information, $J_C:=H_C^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{N_C}^{-1}H_C$,
and $\Sigma_{T\mid C}=(\Sigma_T^{-1}+J_C)^{-1}$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:decregion}
Let $\mathcal R_C(D)$ be the set of pairs $(R,L)$ for which there are
random variables $V_0,V$ with values in Euclidean spaces,
$V_0-V-T-(C,S)$, and $I(T;V)<\infty$, and a
measurable $\hat y$ with
$\E[(Y-\hat y(V,C))^{\mathsf T}W(Y-\hat y(V,C))]\le D$, such that
\[
R\ge I(T;V\mid C),\qquad
L\ge I(T;V\mid C)+I(V_0;C)-I(V_0;S).
\]
A pair $(R,L)$ is achievable at distortion $D$ if and only if
$(R+\epsilon,L+\epsilon)\in\mathcal R_C(D+\epsilon)$ for every $\epsilon>0$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:decsi}. The converse single-letterizes
directly for the continuous source, and the achievability reduces to the
finite-alphabet theorem of Villard and Piantanida
\cite[Theorem~3]{villard2013} by quantization, along the lines of
Appendix~\ref{app:coding}. Since $V_0-V-T-(C,S)$, the leakage bound can also
be written $I(T;V)-I(V;C\mid V_0)-I(V_0;S)$. With $C$ constant the best
choice is $V_0=V$, because $I(V_0;S)\le I(V;S)$, and the bound becomes
$I(T;V\mid S)$, the region of Theorem~\ref{thm:op}. In general the two
auxiliaries are what makes the region hard to evaluate. Xu, Chen, Chen,
and Jin \cite{xu2024} characterize the region under a matrix distortion on
the whole source, and in the preprint version \cite{xuchen2021}, which is
the version we checked, the characterization is stated for full-rank side
informations. Under a weighted distortion we do not settle whether
Gaussian auxiliaries are optimal. The two
degraded cases are explicit.

For Gaussian linear observations the information matrices order the
side informations. If $J_S\preceq J_C$, the sufficient statistic
$J_CT+\nu_C$, $\nu_C\sim\mathcal N(0,J_C)$, of $C$ is mapped by $A=J_SJ_C^{+}$ to
$J_ST+A\nu_C$. Here $AJ_C=J_S$, because $J_S\preceq J_C$ puts the null space
of $J_C$ inside that of $J_S$. Also
$AJ_CA^{\mathsf T}=J_S^{1/2}(J_S^{1/2}J_C^{+}J_S^{1/2})J_S^{1/2}\preceq J_S$,
because $J_C^{+1/2}J_SJ_C^{+1/2}\preceq J_C^{+1/2}J_CJ_C^{+1/2}\preceq I$
gives $\|X\|\le1$ for $X=J_S^{1/2}J_C^{+1/2}$, so that
$XX^{\mathsf T}=J_S^{1/2}J_C^{+}J_S^{1/2}\preceq I$. So adding independent noise of
covariance $J_S-AJ_CA^{\mathsf T}$ reproduces the law, given $T$, of the
sufficient statistic $J_ST+\nu_S$ of $S$, and drawing $S'$ from the
conditional law of $S$ given that statistic, which does not depend on
$T$, gives a channel from $C$ to $S'$ with $(T,S')\sim(T,S)$ and
$T-C-S'$. For any $V_0$ with $V_0-T-(C,S)$, the mutual information
$I(V_0;S)$ depends only on the law of $(V_0,T)$ and the channel from $T$
to $S$, so $I(V_0;S)=I(V_0;S')\le I(V_0;C)$ by data processing. Applied to
the conditional laws given $V_0$, the same argument gives
$I(V;S\mid V_0)\le I(V;C\mid V_0)$ when $V_0-V-T-(C,S)$. The symmetric
statements hold when $J_C\preceq J_S$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mmsecomp}
Let $V-T-(C,S)$ with $J_C\preceq J_S$, and let
$M_C=\E[\Cov(T\mid V,C)]$ and $M_S=\E[\Cov(T\mid V,S)]$ be the minimum
mean-square error covariances, with $M_C\succ0$. Then
$M_S\preceq\bigl(M_C^{-1}+J_S-J_C\bigr)^{-1}$, with equality when $(V,T)$ is
jointly Gaussian.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $J_+=J_S-J_C\succeq0$. The conditional law of $T$ given $(V,S)$
depends on $S$ only through its sufficient statistic $J_ST+\nu_S$,
$\nu_S\sim\mathcal N(0,J_S)$, which has the law, given $T$, of the sum
$c+o$ of the sufficient statistics $c=J_CT+\nu_C$ and $o=J_+T+\nu_+$ of two
independent observations $C'$ and $O$ of $T$ with information matrices
$J_C$ and $J_+$. Take $C'=H_CT+N_C'$ with $N_C'\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{N_C})$
independent of $(V,T,U)$ and of the noise of $O$ below. Since $c+o$ is sufficient for $T$ in $(C',O)$ and
$V-T-(C',O)$, the error covariance $M_S$ equals that of estimating $T$
from $(V,C',O)$, and $(T,V,C')$ has the law of $(T,V,C)$. Write
$J_+=B^{\mathsf T}B$ and take $O=BT+n$ with $n\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$
independent of everything else, which has sufficient statistic
$B^{\mathsf T}O$ of the required law. Let $w=(V,C')$, let $C_w$ be the
conditional covariance of $T$ given $w$, so that $\E[C_w]=M_C$, and fix
the gain $L=M_CB^{\mathsf T}(BM_CB^{\mathsf T}+I)^{-1}$. The affine estimate
$\E[T\mid w]+L(O-B\,\E[T\mid w])$ has conditional error covariance
$(I-LB)C_w(I-LB)^{\mathsf T}+LL^{\mathsf T}$ given $w$, which is affine in
$C_w$. Its mean is therefore
$(I-LB)M_C(I-LB)^{\mathsf T}+LL^{\mathsf T}
=M_C-M_CB^{\mathsf T}(BM_CB^{\mathsf T}+I)^{-1}BM_C=(M_C^{-1}+J_+)^{-1}$,
and since $M_S$ is the minimum mean-square error from $(w,O)$,
$M_S\preceq(M_C^{-1}+J_+)^{-1}$. When $(V,T)$ is jointly Gaussian, $C_w=M_C$
for every $w$ and the conditional mean of $T$ given $(w,O)$ is this affine
estimate, so equality holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:decsi}
Let $0<D<\tr(Q\Sigma_{T\mid C})$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $J_S\preceq J_C$, the achievable pairs at distortion $D$ are
the quadrant above $(R_C(D),R_C(D))$, where
$R_C(D)=\min\{\tfrac12\log(\det\Sigma_{T\mid C}/\det M):0\prec M\preceq\Sigma_{T\mid C},\ \tr(QM)\le D\}$
is reverse water-filling on the eigenvalues of
$\Sigma_{T\mid C}^{1/2}Q\Sigma_{T\mid C}^{1/2}$. The leakage-optimal and
rate-optimal descriptions coincide, and the holder learns as much as the
rate.
\item[(b)] If $J_C\preceq J_S$, the achievable pairs at distortion $D$ are
the region of Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior} for the source
$\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{T\mid C})$ with holder information $J_S-J_C$ and the
same weight $Q$. Its coordinate is the decoder's error covariance
$M=\E[\Cov(T\mid V,C)]$. The results of
Sections~\ref{sec:convex}--\ref{sec:directions} are statements about that
program, so they hold with $\Sigma_T$ replaced by $\Sigma_{T\mid C}$, $J$
by $J_S-J_C$ (written as $H'^{\mathsf T}H'$ with unit noise where an
observation map is needed), and $D_{\max}$ by $\tr(Q\Sigma_{T\mid C})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $V$ be any description with $I(T;V)<\infty$ and write
$M_C=\E[\Cov(T\mid V,C)]$, which is positive definite because a singular
$M_C$ reveals a linear functional of $T$ without noise and makes
$I(T;V\mid C)$ infinite. Since $T$ has a conditional density given $C$
and $I(T;V\mid C)<\infty$, it has a conditional density given $(V,C)$
almost surely. Since $h(T\mid C)=\tfrac12\log\det(2\pi e\Sigma_{T\mid C})$
and, by the Gaussian maximum-entropy bound and the concavity of
$\log\det$, $h(T\mid V,C)\le\tfrac12\log\det(2\pi eM_C)$, we have
$I(T;V\mid C)\ge\tfrac12\log(\det\Sigma_{T\mid C}/\det M_C)$, and likewise
$I(T;V\mid S)\ge\tfrac12\log(\det\Sigma_{T\mid S}/\det M_S)$. The distortion
of any reconstruction from $(V,C)$ is at least $\tr(QM_C)$.

(a) By Theorem~\ref{thm:decregion}, an achievable $(R,L)$ has
$(R+\epsilon,L+\epsilon)\in\mathcal R_C(D+\epsilon)$ for every $\epsilon>0$.
For any feasible $(V_0,V)$, $I(V_0;C)\ge I(V_0;S)$ because $J_S\preceq J_C$,
so both $R+\epsilon$ and $L+\epsilon$ are at least
$I(T;V\mid C)\ge R_C(D+\epsilon)$. The function $R_C$ is continuous on
$(0,\tr(Q\Sigma_{T\mid C}))$, so $R,L\ge R_C(D)$. Conversely, $V_0$ constant
and a jointly Gaussian $V$ with $\Cov(T\mid V,C)$ equal to the minimizer
$M$ of $R_C(D)$, with $\hat y=\E[Y\mid V,C]$, put $(R_C(D),R_C(D))$ in $\mathcal R_C(D)$, and
the region is increasing in $D$. The minimizer exists because the
objective grows without bound as $\det M\to0$. Let $\theta\in(0,1]$ make
$\theta\Sigma_{T\mid C}$ feasible, and take $\delta>0$ so small that the objective
exceeds its value at $\theta\Sigma_{T\mid C}$ wherever $\det M<\delta$. The
minimum may then be taken over the compact feasible set with
$\det M\ge\delta$, which is nonempty and on which the objective is continuous. Such a $V$ exists because
$M^{-1}-\Sigma_{T\mid C}^{-1}\succeq0$.

(b) For feasible $(V_0,V)$, $J_C\preceq J_S$ gives
$I(V;S\mid V_0)\ge I(V;C\mid V_0)$, so
$I(V_0;C)-I(V_0;S)\ge I(V;C)-I(V;S)$ and the leakage bound is at least
$I(T;V\mid C)+I(V;C)-I(V;S)=I(T;V\mid S)$, with equality for $V_0=V$. By the
bounds above and Lemma~\ref{lem:mmsecomp},
$R+\epsilon\ge\tfrac12\log(\det\Sigma_{T\mid C}/\det M_C)$,
$L+\epsilon\ge\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_{T\mid S}+\tfrac12\log\det(M_C^{-1}+J_S-J_C)$,
and $\tr(QM_C)\le D+\epsilon$, with $0\prec M_C\preceq\Sigma_{T\mid C}$. Since
$(\Sigma_{T\mid C}^{-1}+J_S-J_C)^{-1}=\Kc$, these are the coordinates
\eqref{eq:posterior} of the source $\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{T\mid C})$ with
holder information $J_S-J_C$ at $\Sez=M_C$, at distortion $D+\epsilon$. The
compactness argument in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:op} removes
$\epsilon$. Conversely, $V_0=V$ jointly Gaussian with $\Cov(T\mid V,C)=M$
for any $0\prec M\preceq\Sigma_{T\mid C}$ attains all three coordinates with
equality, by the equality case of Lemma~\ref{lem:mmsecomp}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:decregion} makes them achievable.

The substitution in (b) is exact for three reasons. First, the program
of Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior} depends on the holder only through
$J$, and so does every expression in
Sections~\ref{sec:convex}--\ref{sec:directions} that uses $H$ and
$\Sigma_U$, because
$\det(HXH^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U)=\det\Sigma_U\det(I+JX)$ for every $X$.
Any factorization $J_S-J_C=H'^{\mathsf T}H'$ with unit noise therefore
gives the same program. Second, a jointly Gaussian description
$V=a^{\mathsf T}T+N$ with $N\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ independent of
$(T,C,S)$ has $\Cov(T\mid V,C)=(\Sigma_{T\mid C}^{-1}+aa^{\mathsf T}/\sigma^2)^{-1}$,
the error covariance it would have for the source
$\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_{T\mid C})$. So statements about linear Gaussian
descriptions carry over with $\E[Y\mid\hY]$ replaced by $\E[Y\mid V,C]$.
Third, every constant and hypothesis of those sections is read on the
substituted parameters. In particular $\Sigma_Y$ becomes
$F\Sigma_{T\mid C}F^{\mathsf T}$, the decoder's remaining uncertainty about
$Y$, and a hypothesis such as a holder that observes only the context
becomes a condition on $J_S-J_C$.
\end{proof}

So a decoder that knows more than the holder removes the tradeoff, and
a holder that knows more than the decoder leaves exactly the problem of
the preceding sections for the source as the decoder already knows it,
with the holder's excess information in place of its total. When
neither dominates, the auxiliary $V_0$ can matter, and the region is
beyond the methods of this paper.

\section{Instances}
\label{sec:examples}

\emph{Scalar.} At $(\rho^2,\tau^2,D)=(0.75,0.5,0.3)$ the rate-optimal
channel pays $0.0349$ bits of excess leakage and the leakage-optimal
channel $0.0400$ bits of excess rate. Over the box
$\rho^2\in[0.05,0.95]$, $\tau^2\in[\tfrac14,4]$, $D\in[0.05,0.95]$ the
rate excess of the leakage-optimal channel stays below $0.114$ bits and
the leakage excess of the rate-optimal channel below $0.077$ bits, and at
$(\rho^2,\tau^2,D)=(0.5,10^{-3},0.5)$ the rate excess reaches $1.537$ bits.
Two sources with the same law of $(Y,S)$ up to scaling of $S$,
$(\rho^2,\tau^2)=(\tfrac35,\tfrac15)$ and $(\tfrac34,\tfrac12)$, have
$L=1.1880$ and $1.2105$ bits at $D=0.1$. The leakage is not determined by
the law of $(Y,S)$, because the encoder observes the context.

\emph{Coupled.} For the coupled instance of Section~\ref{sec:model},
the certificate of Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim}(b) holds for $D\ge1.08530$,
out of $D_{\max}=1.4$. The rate-optimal description reduces the error along the
first axis for every $D\ge0.8$. The leakage-optimal description reduces
it along $47.48^\circ$, $46.76^\circ$, $45.07^\circ$, and $41.15^\circ$ at
$D=1.38$, $1.3$, $1.2$, and $1.1$. Four sources with the same $u$ and the same law of
$(Y,S)$ up to scaling, $(|\Cov(Y,V)|^2,\tau^2)=(0.33,0.1)$, $(0.36,0.2)$,
$(0.39,0.3)$, $(0.42,0.4)$, read along $42.83^\circ$, $37.61^\circ$,
$33.75^\circ$, and $30.84^\circ$ at $D=1.3$, each inside the range of its certificate.
The direction, too, is not
determined by the law of $(Y,S)$.

\emph{Independent components.} With
$(\sigma_1^2,\rho_1^2,\tau_1^2)=(1,0,1)$ and
$(\sigma_2^2,\rho_2^2,\tau_2^2)=(0.6,0.9,0.05)$,
$\kappa_1=0.7213$, $\kappa_2=0.0633$, and the two descriptions describe
disjoint components for every $D\in[1.2002,1.6)$. At $D=1.4$ the
rate-optimal description pays $R=L=0.1610$ bits on component $1$ and the
leakage-optimal description pays $L=0.0197$ and $R=0.3272$ bits on
component $2$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

A description held by two parties has a cost for each, and the
encoder's observations decide how the costs trade off. For Gaussian
sources without decoder side information the region is now explicit in
structure. It is a convex program in the error covariance, it has a closed form in a
one-dimensional case, and its directions turn with the budget exactly
when the two parties' geometries are not aligned. Several problems remain
open. With side information at the decoder, the region is proved for
the continuous source and the degraded cases are settled here. When
neither side information dominates, the second auxiliary can matter.
Under a matrix distortion on the whole source, Xu, Chen, Chen, and Jin
\cite{xu2024} characterize the region. Whether their two-precision
program, in the form of the preprint \cite{xuchen2021}, under a
weighted distortion and a rank-deficient holder, has the convexity,
pencil, and turning structure of the case without decoder side
information is open. Explicit solutions are now available in four
settings. They give the whole frontier in commuting geometry at every budget, the
minimum leakage in arbitrary geometry in the one-dimensional case at
large budgets, the whole frontier in arbitrary geometry at small
budgets when the decoder reproduces the whole source, and the whole
frontier for a scalar described variable with any context and any
holder, up to one scalar root. In every geometry and at every budget
the frontier is the fixed point of reverse water-filling on a tilted
weight. For a scalar holder this is two scalar equations, and for a
holder of any rank the fixed point is the limit of a monotone iteration
of water-filling steps. A closed form for a holder of higher rank and a
described variable of higher dimension, or for the boundaries between these cases,
is not known. The general case with side information at both receivers,
where the second auxiliary of Villard and Piantanida can matter, under
a trace-type distortion and a rank-deficient holder, is where the main
open question lies. Stationary
sources and finite blocklengths are not treated.

\section{The Coding Theorem}

\subsection{Finite alphabets}

Let $(X,S)$ be a discrete memoryless pair with finite alphabets and let
$d$ be a distortion with values in $[0,d_{\max}]$. Codes, rate,
distortion, and leakage $H(M\mid S^n)/n$ are as in Section~\ref{sec:model}.

\begin{theorem}[{\cite[Theorem~3]{villard2013}, without decoder side information}]
\label{thm:dmc}
The closure of the achievable set at distortion $D$ is
$\bigcup_{p(\hat x\mid x):\,\E d(X,\hX)\le D}\{(R,L):R\ge I(X;\hX),\ L\ge I(X;\hX\mid S)\}$,
and the union is convex and closed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Villard and Piantanida prove the region in the equivocation form
$\Upsilon\le H(X\mid V)+0-I(X;S\mid U)$ for the equivocation $\Upsilon$, $U-V-X-S$, with no decoder side
information. Taking $U=V$ is optimal, because
$I(X;S\mid U)=I(V;S\mid U)+I(X;S\mid V)\ge I(X;S\mid V)$, and
$\Upsilon=H(X\mid S)-L$ for a deterministic encoder, which gives the
displayed form with $V$ in place of $\hX$, and a reproduction that is a
function of $V$ only lowers both coordinates. For the Gaussian extension
we use the following finite-blocklength form of the achievability,
obtained by covering at rate $I(X;\hX)+2\epsilon$, a random binning of the
codeword index into $2^{n(I(X;\hX\mid S)+3\epsilon)}$ bins used only as a
proof device, Fano's inequality for a hypothetical decoder holding the
bin index and $S^n$, and selection of one deterministic code. For every
channel with finite reproduction alphabet and every $\epsilon>0$ there
are $\epsilon_n\to0$ and, for all large $n$, a deterministic code with
$|\mathcal M_n|\le2^{n(I(X;\hX)+2\epsilon)}$, distortion at most
$\E d(X,\hX)+\epsilon+2\epsilon_nd_{\max}$, and
$H(M\mid S^n)\le n(I(X;\hX\mid S)+3\epsilon)+1+2\epsilon_nn(I(X;\hX)+2\epsilon)$.
Convexity of both coordinates in the channel, for the conditional one by
the decomposition $I(X;\hX\mid S)=\sum_sp(s)I_{p(x\mid s)}(q)$, makes the
union convex without time sharing.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Gaussian source}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:op}]
\emph{Converse.} For any code, $\log|\mathcal M_n|\ge I(T^n;\hY^n)\ge
\sum_iI(T_i;\hY_i)$ and $H(M\mid S^n)\ge I(T^n;\hY^n\mid S^n)
=\sum_i[I(T_i;\hY^n,T^{i-1},S^n)-I(T_i;S_i)]\ge\sum_iI(T_i;\hY_i\mid S_i)$,
by the chain rule and the independence of the pairs $(T_i,S_i)$. Every
term is finite, being at most $I(T_i;S_i)+\log|\mathcal M_n|$ with
$I(T_i;S_i)=\tfrac12\log\det(H\Sigma_TH^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_U)/\det\Sigma_U$.
The mixture of the per-letter channels satisfies the Markov chain, has
distortion $D_n$, and by convexity of both coordinates in the channel
(for general alphabets the conditional coordinate is an integral over
$s$ of relative entropies between measures that depend affinely on the
channel, and relative entropy is jointly convex in its two arguments
\cite{csiszar1967,wyner1978})
lies below $(R_n,L_n)$, so $(R_n,L_n)$ is in the union at $D_n$.

\emph{Closedness.} Let $(R_j,L_j)\to(R,L)$ lie in the union at levels
$D_j$ with $\limsup D_j\le D$, with $\Sigma_j\in\mathcal P(D_j)$ below them
by Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior}. The $\Sigma_j$ lie in the compact
set $0\preceq\Sigma\preceq\Sigma_T$ and $\det\Sigma_j\ge\det\Sigma_T\,2^{-2R_j}$
is bounded away from zero, so a subsequence converges to some
$\Sigma^\star\succ0$ in $\mathcal P(D)$. Since $r$ and $\ell$ are continuous on
positive definite matrices, $(R,L)$ lies above
$(r(\Sigma^\star),\ell(\Sigma^\star))$.

\emph{Achievability.} Fix an admissible channel with $I(T;\hY)<\infty$.
Then $\E\|\hY\|^2<\infty$ because its distortion is finite and $W\succ0$. Let $q_k$ be the saturating dyadic quantizer of
level $k$ applied coordinatewise. Its bounded cells
$[j2^{-k},(j+1)2^{-k})$, $-4^k\le j<4^k$, map to their left endpoints and
the two tail cells to $\pm2^k$. The partitions are nested and generate
the Borel $\sigma$-field.

\emph{Step A.} The $\sigma$-fields of $S_\eta:=q_\eta(S)$ increase to
$\sigma(S)$, so $I(\hY;S_\eta)\uparrow I(\hY;S)$ \cite[Ch.~2]{pinsker1964}.
Fix $\eta$ with $I(\hY;S_\eta)\ge I(\hY;S)-\epsilon$. Since $\hY-T-S_\eta$,
$I(T;\hY\mid S_\eta)=I(T;\hY)-I(\hY;S_\eta)\le I(T;\hY\mid S)+\epsilon$.

\emph{Step B.} With $T_k=q_k(T)$ and $\hY_k=q_k(\hY)$, the surrogate
source $(T_k,S_\eta)$ is discrete memoryless, the surrogate channel is
$p(\hy\mid t_k)=\Pr(\hY_k=\hy\mid T_k=t_k)$, and the surrogate distortion
$d_k(t_k,\hy)=\E[(Y-\hy)^{\mathsf T}W(Y-\hy)\mid T_k=t_k]$ is bounded for
each $k$, every cell having positive probability since
$\Sigma_T\succ0$. The finite-blocklength achievability of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dmc} gives a code of rate at most
$I(T_k;\hY_k)+2\epsilon\le I(T;\hY)+2\epsilon$. Its true distortion equals
its surrogate distortion, because given $T_k^n$ the law of $Y_i$ depends
on $T_{k,i}$ alone. The surrogate distortion of the channel converges to
$\E[(Y-\hY)^{\mathsf T}W(Y-\hY)]\le D$, because $\E[Y\mid T_k]\to Y$ in
$L^2$ by martingale convergence and $\hY_k\to\hY$ in $L^2$. The surrogate
law of $(\hY_k,S_\eta)$ converges weakly to the true law of
$(\hY,S_\eta)$, by the bounded-convergence and martingale argument
applied to test functions bounded on $\R^m\times q_\eta(\R^r)$ and
continuous in the first argument, and the Markov chain $\hY-T-S$. Mutual
information is lower semicontinuous under weak convergence
\cite{posner1975}, so
$I_k(T_k;\hY_k\mid S_\eta)=I(T_k;\hY_k)-I_k(\hY_k;S_\eta)\le I(T;\hY\mid S)+2\epsilon$
for large $k$, and the code has $H(M\mid S_\eta^n)\le n(I(T;\hY\mid S)+5\epsilon+o(1))$.

\emph{Step C.} $S_\eta^n$ is a function of $S^n$, so
$H(M\mid S^n)\le H(M\mid S_\eta^n)$. A diagonal sequence
$\epsilon\downarrow0$ achieves the corner
$(I(T;\hY),I(T;\hY\mid S))$ at distortion $D$, and with the converse,
closedness, and Proposition~\ref{prop:posterior} the region is as stated.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Side information at the decoder}
\label{app:decsi}

\emph{Finite alphabets.} Let $(X,C,E)$ be a discrete memoryless source
with finite alphabets, the encoder seeing $X^n$, the decoder $(M,C^n)$,
and the holder $(M,E^n)$, and let the distortion take values in
$[0,d_{\max}]$. Villard and Piantanida \cite[Theorem~3]{villard2013} prove,
for deterministic encoders, that the closure of the achievable
rate--distortion--equivocation triples is the closure of the set of
$(R,D,\Upsilon)$ with $R\ge I(X;V\mid C)$, $D\ge\E d(X,\hat x(V,C))$, and
$\Upsilon\le H(X\mid V,C)+I(X;C\mid V_0)-I(X;E\mid V_0)$ for finite
auxiliaries with $V_0-V-X-(C,E)$, where the equivocation is
$\tfrac1nH(X^n\mid M,E^n)$. Their reproduction alphabet is the source
alphabet. A finite reproduction alphabet $\mathcal Y$ is covered by
enlarging the source alphabet to the disjoint union
$\mathcal X\sqcup\mathcal Y$, with the
added letters of probability zero. For $x\in\mathcal X$ and $b\in\mathcal X$ set
$d(x,b)=\max_{x',y}d(x',y)$, and let $d$ be bounded but otherwise arbitrary on
the added source letters, so that replacing any such output by a fixed
$y_0\in\mathcal Y$ does not increase the distortion. For a deterministic
encoder the leakage is $\tfrac1nH(M\mid E^n)=H(X\mid E)-\tfrac1nH(X^n\mid M,E^n)$,
and since $V_0-X-(C,E)$,
\[
H(X\mid E)-H(X\mid V,C)-I(X;C\mid V_0)+I(X;E\mid V_0)
=I(X;V\mid C)+I(V_0;C)-I(V_0;E).
\]
So for every point
$(I(X;V\mid C),\E d(X,\hat x(V,C)),I(X;V\mid C)+I(V_0;C)-I(V_0;E))$ of
finite auxiliaries and every $\epsilon>0$, some deterministic code has
rate, distortion, and leakage each within $\epsilon$ of that point or
below it.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:decregion}]
\emph{Converse.} Fix a code, so $M=f_n(T^n)$ and $\hY^n=g_n(M,C^n)$. For
$i=1,\dots,n$ let
\[
V_{0,i}=(M,C^{i-1},S_{i+1}^n),\qquad V_i=(M,C^{i-1},C_{i+1}^n,S_{i+1}^n).
\]
Then $V_{0,i}$ is a function of $V_i$, and $V_i-T_i-(C_i,S_i)$ because,
given $T_i$, the variable $V_i$ is a function of $T_i$ and of
$(T^n,C^n,S^n)$ with the $i$-th letter removed, which is independent of
$(T_i,C_i,S_i)$. The reconstruction $\hY_i$ is a function of $(V_i,C_i)$.
Every mutual information below is finite, being at most $H(M)$ or
$I(T_i;C_i)$ or $I(T_i;S_i)$, so the chain rule and the Csiszár sum
identity apply to these continuous variables. For the rate,
\[
nR_n\ge H(M)\ge I(T^n;M\mid C^n)=\sum_iI(T_i;M,C^{n}_{\setminus i},T_{i+1}^n\mid C_i)
\ge\sum_iI(T_i;V_i\mid C_i),
\]
where the equality uses the chain rule in reverse order and the
independence of $T_i$ from $(C^n_{\setminus i},T_{i+1}^n)$ given $C_i$,
and the inequality uses data processing, since $S_{i+1}^n$ is obtained
from $T_{i+1}^n$ through noise independent of everything else. For the
leakage, $H(M\mid T^n,S^n)=0$ and $M-T^n-(C^n,S^n)$ give
\[
nL_n=I(M;T^n\mid S^n)=I(M;T^n\mid C^n)+I(M;C^n)-I(M;S^n),
\]
and the Csiszár sum identity gives
$I(M;C^n)-I(M;S^n)=\sum_i[I(M;C_i\mid C^{i-1},S_{i+1}^n)-I(M;S_i\mid C^{i-1},S_{i+1}^n)]$.
Since $(C^{i-1},S_{i+1}^n)$ is independent of $(C_i,S_i)$, each term equals
$I(V_{0,i};C_i)-I(V_{0,i};S_i)$, so
$nL_n\ge\sum_i[I(T_i;V_i\mid C_i)+I(V_{0,i};C_i)-I(V_{0,i};S_i)]$. With $\tau$
uniform on $\{1,\dots,n\}$ and independent of everything, set
$V_0=(V_{0,\tau},\tau)$, $V=(V_\tau,\tau)$, and $(T,C,S)=(T_\tau,C_\tau,S_\tau)$, which has the
source law. Then $V_0-V-T-(C,S)$, $I(T;V)\le H(M)<\infty$,
$I(T;V\mid C)$, $I(V_0;C)$, and $I(V_0;S)$ are the averages of their
per-letter versions, and the reconstruction $\hat y(V,C)=\hY_\tau$ has
distortion $D_n$. So $(R_n,L_n)\in\mathcal R_C(D_n)$ for every code. If
$(R,L)$ is achievable at $D$, then for every $\epsilon>0$ and all large $n$
some code has $R_n\le R+\epsilon$, $L_n\le L+\epsilon$, $D_n\le D+\epsilon$,
and since $\mathcal R_C$ is increasing in $(R,L)$ and in $D$,
$(R+\epsilon,L+\epsilon)\in\mathcal R_C(D+\epsilon)$.

\emph{Achievability.} It suffices to show that for every feasible
$(V_0,V,\hat y)$ and $\epsilon>0$ there are codes, for all large $n$, with
rate at most $I(T;V\mid C)+\epsilon$, distortion at most $D+\epsilon$, and
leakage at most $I(T;V\mid C)+I(V_0;C)-I(V_0;S)+\epsilon$. A diagonal
sequence over $\epsilon$ then achieves any $(R,L)$ with
$(R+\epsilon,L+\epsilon)\in\mathcal R_C(D+\epsilon)$ for all $\epsilon$. Replace
$\hat y$ by $\E[Y\mid V,C]$, which does not increase the distortion.
The auxiliaries $V_0$ and $V$ take values in Euclidean spaces, as in the
definition of $\mathcal R_C(D)$, and the auxiliaries built in the converse
do too, because $M$ and $\tau$ take finitely many values and, after
zero-padding $V_i$ and $V_{0,i}$ to a common dimension, which stays injective
once $\tau$ is included, each lies in a fixed Euclidean space. Let $q_j$
denote the saturating dyadic quantizers of Appendix~\ref{app:coding}.

\emph{Step A (auxiliaries and side informations).} Let $U_j=q_j(V_0)$,
$V'_j=(U_j,q_j(V))$, $C_\kappa=q_\kappa(C)$, and $S_\eta=q_\eta(S)$. Then
$U_j$ is a function of $V'_j$ and $U_j-V'_j-T-(C_\kappa,S_\eta)$. Along the
nested partitions, $I(T;V'_j)\uparrow I(T;V_0,V)=I(T;V)$,
$I(V'_j;C_\kappa)\uparrow I(V;C)$, $I(U_j;C_\kappa)\uparrow I(V_0;C)$, and
$I(U_j;S_\eta)\uparrow I(V_0;S)$ \cite[Ch.~2]{pinsker1964}, all limits
finite. Since $V-T-C$, $I(T;V\mid C)=I(T;V)-I(V;C)$, so
$I(T;V'_j\mid C_\kappa)\to I(T;V\mid C)$ and the leakage expression with
$(U_j,V'_j,C_\kappa,S_\eta)$ converges to the one with $(V_0,V,C,S)$. With
$\hat y_{j,\kappa}=\E[Y\mid V'_j,C_\kappa]$, martingale convergence gives
$\hat y_{j,\kappa}\to\E[Y\mid V_0,V,C]=\E[Y\mid V,C]$ in $L^2$, because
$V_0-(V,C)-T$, so the distortion converges as well. Fix $j,\kappa,\eta$
with all three quantities within $\epsilon$ of their limits. The values
of $\hat y_{j,\kappa}$ form a finite set $\mathcal Y$ with
$\max_{y\in\mathcal Y}\|y\|_W=:b<\infty$.

\emph{Step B (the surrogate source).} Let $T_k=q_k(T)$ and let the
surrogate discrete memoryless source be $(X,C,E)=(T_k,C_\kappa,S_\eta)$ with
its true joint law. A code that uses only $T_k^n$ at the encoder and
$(M,C_\kappa^n)$ at the decoder is a code for the Gaussian problem whose
rate and leakage $H(M\mid S_\eta^n)$ equal its surrogate rate and leakage,
because the pair $(T_k^n,S_\eta^n)$ has the same law in both. Give the
surrogate the distortion
$d_k(t,y)=\E[(Y-y)^{\mathsf T}W(Y-y)\mid T_k=t]$ and the auxiliaries the
channel $p_k(u,v'\mid t)=\Pr(U_j=u,V'_j=v'\mid T_k=t)$, which keeps
$U_j$ a function of $V'_j$ and so the chain $U-V'-X-(C,E)$. The true
distortion of a code differs from its surrogate distortion only because
the decoder's output depends on $C_\kappa^n$. With
$m(t)=\E[Y\mid T_k=t]$ and $m(t,c)=\E[Y\mid T_k=t,C_\kappa=c]$, the conditional
expected distortion of an output $y$ given $(T_{k,i},C_{\kappa,i})=(t,c)$ is
$\|y-m(t,c)\|_W^2+v(t,c)$ against $\|y-m(t)\|_W^2+v(t)$ for $d_k$, and
averaging over $c$ given $t$ cancels every term except
$-2\E[y_i^{\mathsf T}W(m(T_k,C_\kappa)-m(T_k))]$. By Cauchy--Schwarz the
difference per letter is at most
$2b\,\E[\|m(T_k,C_\kappa)-m(T_k)\|_W^2]^{1/2}$, which tends to $0$ as
$k\to\infty$ because both conditional means converge to $Y$ in $L^2$, and
the bound holds for every code whose outputs lie in $\mathcal Y$.

\emph{Step C (convergence of the surrogate quantities).} The surrogate
joint law of $(U_j,V'_j,C_\kappa,S_\eta)$ is
$\E[\mathbf 1\{C_\kappa=c,S_\eta=s\}\Pr(U_j=u,V'_j=v'\mid T_k)]$, which converges
as $k\to\infty$ to $\E[\mathbf 1\{C_\kappa=c,S_\eta=s\}\Pr(U_j=u,V'_j=v'\mid T)]$,
the true joint law, by martingale convergence and $(V_0,V)-T-(C,S)$. All
four alphabets are finite, so the surrogate values of $I(V';C)$,
$I(U;C)$, and $I(U;E)$ converge to the true ones, while the surrogate
$I(X;V')=I(T_k;V'_j)\le I(T;V'_j)$. The surrogate distortion of
$\hat x(v',c)=\hat y_{j,\kappa}(v',c)$ is
$\E[\sum_{v'}\Pr(V'_j=v'\mid T_k)\,(\|\hat y_{j,\kappa}(v',C_\kappa)-m(T_k)\|_W^2+v(T_k))]$,
which converges to $\E\|Y-\hat y_{j,\kappa}(V'_j,C_\kappa)\|_W^2$ because
$m(T_k)\to Y$ in $L^2$, $\E v(T_k)\to0$, and the outputs are bounded. Fix
$k$ with the surrogate rate, leakage, and distortion within $\epsilon$ of
their values in Step~A and the distortion mismatch of Step~B at most
$\epsilon$.

\emph{Step D (codes).} By the finite-alphabet theorem there is a
blocklength $n_0$ and a deterministic code of that length with rate at
most $I(T;V\mid C)+4\epsilon$, surrogate distortion at most $D+4\epsilon$,
outputs in $\mathcal Y$, and $\tfrac1{n_0}H(M\mid S_\eta^{n_0})$ at most the
leakage expression plus $4\epsilon$. As a code for the Gaussian problem
its distortion is at most $D+5\epsilon$ by Step~B, and its leakage
satisfies $H(M\mid S^{n_0})\le H(M\mid S_\eta^{n_0})$ because $S_\eta^{n_0}$
is a function of $S^{n_0}$. For a general blocklength $n=kn_0+r$ with
$0\le r<n_0$, apply this code to each of the $k$ blocks and reproduce the
last $r$ letters by zero. The message is the tuple of block messages, so
the rate does not increase. The pairs of block message and block of
$S^n$ are independent across blocks, so the leakage is the sum of the
block leakages divided by $n$ and does not exceed that of one block. The
distortion is at most $(kn_0(D+5\epsilon)+rD_{\max})/n$, whose limsup is
at most $D+5\epsilon$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Gaussian Exhaustion Lemma}
\label{app:exhaust}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gauss}]
\emph{The divergence bound.} Conditional mutual information is the
expected divergence of the conditional law of $T$ given $(\hY,S)$ from
the conditional law given $S$ \cite{wyner1978,pinsker1964}. Fix a
realization, let $P$ be the conditional law with mean $\mu$ and
covariance $C$, $P_G=\mathcal N(\mu,C)$, and
$Q_S=\mathcal N(\mu_S,\Kc)$. If $P$ has no density the divergence is
infinite. Otherwise $\log(dP_G/dQ_S)$ is quadratic in $t$ and $P$, $P_G$
share two moments, so $D(P\|Q_S)=D(P\|P_G)+D(P_G\|Q_S)\ge D(P_G\|Q_S)$.
Averaging $D(P_G\|Q_S)$, the trace term plus the mean term,
$\E[\tr(\Kc^{-1}C)+(\mu-\mu_S)^{\mathsf T}\Kc^{-1}(\mu-\mu_S)]$, averages to
$p$ by the law of total covariance, and by concavity of $\ln\det$ and Jensen,
$\E[-\ln\det C]\ge-\ln\det\E C\ge-\ln\det\Se$, since the conditional mean
has error covariance no larger than any linear estimator. Hence
$I(T;\hY\mid S)\ge\tfrac12\log(\det\Kc/\det\Se)$, and the same argument
without $S$ gives $I(T;\hY)\ge\tfrac12\log(\det\Sigma_T/\det\Sez)$.

\emph{The determinant identity.} With $\hY=AT+N'$, $\Cov(N',T)=0$, and
$\Cov(N',S)=0$, the change of variables $(T,S,\hY)\mapsto(T,S,N')$ is
block triangular with unit diagonal, so
$\det\Cov(T,S,\hY)=\det\Sigma_S\det\Kc\det\Sigma_N$. The Schur factorization
in the other order gives $\det\Cov(\hY,S)\det\Se$, and
$\Cov(\hY)-\Cov(\hY,S)\Sigma_S^{-1}\Cov(S,\hY)=A\Kc A^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_N\succ0$.
So $\det\Se=\det\Kc\det\Sigma_N/\det(A\Kc A^{\mathsf T}+\Sigma_N)$, finite and
positive.

\emph{Equality.} For the jointly Gaussian channel $AT+N$ the conditional
law of $T$ given $(\hY,S)$ is Gaussian with the linear estimate as mean
and $\Se$ as covariance, so every inequality is an equality. Its second
moments match the given channel's.
\end{proof}

\section{Practical Quantizers}
\label{sec:quant}

The theorems concern long blocks and Gaussian test channels. A practical
description is often a fixed-rate scalar quantizer applied to one
projection of the data. We ask two questions of such a quantizer. Does
the projection it reads change its leakage at equal rate and under the
same distortion budget? And does the leakage-aware projection turn with the budget,
as Theorem~\ref{thm:turning} predicts for the optimum?

\textbf{Setup.} Take the coupled instance of Section~\ref{sec:model},
so that $Q=F^{\mathsf T}F$ and $D_{\max}=\tr\Sigma_Y=1.4$. For a direction
$b$ let $Z=b^{\mathsf T}T/(b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb)^{1/2}$, which is standard
normal, and let $M=q(Z)$ for a scalar quantizer $q$ with $K$ levels and
thresholds at the midpoints between levels. The decoder reproduces
$\hY=\E[Y\mid M]$. Write $p_i$ for the probability of cell $i$, $m_i$ for
$\E[Z\mid M=i]$, and $\gamma=\sum_ip_im_i^2$. Let
$c=\E[YZ]=F\Sigma_Tb/(b^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Tb)^{1/2}$ and let $\rho$ be the
correlation of $Z$ with the scalar $S$. Because $Y-cZ$ is independent of
$Z$, the distortion is
\[
D(b)=\tr\Sigma_Y-\gamma\,\|c\|^2 .
\]
The entropy $H(M)=-\sum_ip_i\log p_i$ does not depend on $b$, so every
comparison below is at equal rate, whether the index is sent at
$\log K$ bits or entropy coded. Given $S$, the variable $Z$ is normal
with variance $1-\rho^2$ and a mean proportional to $\rho$, so the
leakage $H(M\mid S)$ depends on $b$ only through $\rho$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:quant}
For every quantizer $q$, the leakage $H(q(Z)\mid S)$ is a nonincreasing
function of $|\rho|$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\bar S$ be $S$ scaled to unit variance. The law of $(Z,\bar S)$ is
fixed by $\rho$, and replacing $\bar S$ by $-\bar S$ flips the sign of
$\rho$ without changing $H(M\mid\bar S)$, so the leakage is a function of
$|\rho|$. For $0\le\rho<\rho'\le1$, let $(Z,\bar S')$ have correlation
$\rho'$ and let
$\bar S''=(\rho/\rho')\bar S'+(1-\rho^2/\rho'^2)^{1/2}N$ with $N$ standard
normal and independent. Then $(Z,\bar S'')$ has correlation $\rho$ and
$Z-\bar S'-\bar S''$ is a Markov chain, so
$H(M\mid\bar S'')\ge H(M\mid\bar S',\bar S'')=H(M\mid\bar S')$.
\end{proof}

For every quantizer used here the leakage is strictly decreasing in
$|\rho|$, which we checked at 200 values of $|\rho|$ in $[0,0.995]$. The
leakage-aware projection for a given quantizer therefore maximizes
$|\rho|$ subject to $\gamma\|c\|^2\ge\tr\Sigma_Y-D_0$. We place each
budget relative to the principal-axis quantizer, which reads $Y_1$, has
$c=(1,0)$, and has distortion $D_R=\tr\Sigma_Y-\gamma$. Setting
$D_0=D_R+f(\tr\Sigma_Y-D_R)$ with $0<f<1$, the constraint becomes
$\|c\|^2\ge1-f$, and the quantizer has dropped out. At a given $f$ the
leakage-aware projection is the same for every quantizer, and only its
leakage depends on the quantizer. For $f\ge0.64$ it is the context
itself. The choice $Z=V$ has $c=\Cov(Y,V)=0.6u$ with $\|c\|^2=0.36$, and
$V$ maximizes $|\rho|$ over all projections because $S=V+U$.

\textbf{Computation.} We use the Lloyd--Max quantizers for the standard
normal law \cite{lloyd1982,max1960} at $k=2$, $3$, and $4$ bits, and
$f=0.15$, $0.4$, and $0.7$. The rate and the distortion are closed-form
expressions in the cell probabilities and conditional means, which are
evaluated through the normal distribution function. The leakage is an
exact one-dimensional integral over $S$ of the conditional entropy of the
cell index. We evaluate it by adaptive quadrature, split at the points
where $S$ crosses a cell edge. Gauss--Hermite rules with $160$ to $1280$
nodes agree with it to six decimals. The leakage-aware projection is the
exact maximizer of $|\rho|$ over the feasible directions, found from a
one-parameter generalized eigenproblem, and a search over $2\times10^6$
random directions confirms it. Its costs are then evaluated by the same
formulas. Table~\ref{tab:quant} lists the results.

\textbf{Results.} At equal rate and equal budget, the leakage-aware
quantizer leaks $12.9\%$ to $72.1\%$ less than the principal-axis
quantizer (Table~\ref{tab:quant} and Fig.~\ref{fig:quant}(a)). The gain
grows as the budget loosens and is largest at 2 bits, where at $f=0.7$
the quantizer leaks $0.5067$ bits instead of $1.8147$. The principal-axis
quantizer reduces the error along the first axis at every budget. The
leakage-aware quantizer reduces it along $16.53^\circ$, $31.95^\circ$, and
$50.00^\circ$ at the three values of $f$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:quant}(b)). The
reduction above makes the direction the same for every quantizer, because
the budget fixes $|c_{YZ}|^2$ at the same fraction of its maximum for
every codebook. The direction turns with the budget toward the context
direction $u$ at $50^\circ$. At $f=0.7$ the budget does not bind, and the
quantizer reads the context $V$ itself.

The Gaussian optimum of Theorem~\ref{thm:onedim} turns the same way but
less, through $3.7^\circ$ to $6.8^\circ$, $15.0^\circ$ to $20.0^\circ$, and
$41.3^\circ$ to $42.9^\circ$ at the same budgets. A Gaussian description
at budget $D_0$ chooses its noise variance as well as its direction, so
it can spend part of the slack on coarser resolution. A fixed-rate
quantizer has a fixed $\gamma$. It can spend the slack only on direction,
and it turns further. Its leakage also stays well above the floor. Every
budget at $f=0.7$ lies in the certified range $D\ge1.08530$ of
Section~\ref{sec:examples}, where $L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D_0)=L(D_0)$. Because
$\hY$ is an admissible channel and
$H(M\mid S)=I(T;M\mid S)\ge I(T;\hY\mid S)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:op} makes
$L(D_0)$ a lower bound on the leakage of the quantizer. The 2-bit
quantizer leaks $0.5067$ bits there, against a floor of $0.0636$ bits.
At $f=0.15$ and $0.4$ the budgets lie below the certified range, and
$L_{\mathrm{1d}}(D_0)$ is the best value among one-dimensional Gaussian
descriptions rather than a bound.
