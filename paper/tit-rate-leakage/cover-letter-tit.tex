To the Editor-in-Chief and Associate Editor\\
IEEE Transactions on Information Theory

Dear Editor:

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Rate, Leakage, and Distortion
for Gaussian Sources: Water-Filling on a Tilted Weight,'' for publication
in the IEEE Transactions on Information Theory. I am the sole author. The
review manuscript is in single-column format.

An encoder observes a jointly Gaussian vector and describes part of it to
a decoder under a weighted quadratic distortion, while a second party
holds a noisy linear observation of the source and never decodes. The
description then has two costs, its rate and its conditional leakage to
the second party. The single-letter region is known from secure source
coding (Villard and Piantanida [1]), and Ekrem and Ulukus [2] and Xu, Chen,
Chen, and Jin [3] treat vector Gaussian sources under a matrix distortion
on the whole source, a setting in which their bounds already imply that
one description minimizes both costs (Proposition~4). The manuscript
determines the structure of the region under a weighted distortion on
part of the source, where the two costs trade off.

The main results are in three groups. First, both costs are convex in the
error covariance, and every frontier point is the fixed point of reverse
water-filling on the decoder's weight tilted by the second party's
information (Theorem~7), computable by a monotone iteration
(Proposition~11). Second, explicit solutions. When the optimal
description is one-dimensional, which an explicit certificate decides,
the minimum leakage is a closed-form function of the largest eigenvalue
of a matrix pencil in which the budget enters (Theorem~16). For a scalar
second-party observation the frontier reduces to two scalar equations,
and for a scalar described variable to one scalar root (Corollary~9 and
Theorem~18). Third, directions. The best one-dimensional description
turns with the budget unless the two parties' geometries share it
(Theorem~20), a description with a fixed direction pays an explicit
excess leakage (Theorem~25), and the rate-optimal and leakage-optimal
descriptions differ exactly under a stated misalignment condition
(Theorem~22). With side information at the decoder, the manuscript
proves the region for the continuous source (Theorem~29), settles the two
degraded cases (Theorem~31), and brackets the general case between a
Gaussian inner bound and two outer bounds that are tight in both
degraded cases (Proposition~32). An appendix shows the same effects in
fixed-rate scalar quantizers.

Several earlier documents are relevant, and they are distinct from one
another. First, this Transactions declined a different manuscript of
mine, IT-26-0996, on 27 July 2026 without review, on scope and
presentation. That manuscript was a broad synthesis. The present submission is a
focused source-coding paper, shares no theorem with IT-26-0996, and uses
standard source-coding terminology throughout.

Second, the manuscript cites two archived manuscripts by the author, as
disclosed in its Related Work section. Reference [21]
(\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21776291}) is a preliminary manuscript containing the
discrete region theorem, which coincides with [1, Theorem~3] and is
credited there, and the scalar Gaussian corner. Reference [22]
(\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22678341}) is a manuscript on a scalar described
variable and a scalar context, which was prepared for this Transactions
but not submitted. Its closed form appears here as Corollary~17, credited
to it, and its other Gaussian results are special cases of the present
theorems. The present manuscript supersedes the Gaussian results of [22],
which will not be submitted separately. Its binary rate--leakage frontier
is not part of the present manuscript. A companion draft on vector
described variables is likewise superseded and will not be submitted.

The present manuscript's sources, verification scripts, and recorded
outputs are archived at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23127802} and at a pinned
commit of the public repository named in its Appendix. The identities
and instances are checked numerically and symbolically, and the scalar
steps, the linear-algebra step of the rank bound, and the convexity of
both costs in the error covariance are machine-checked in Lean~4, as
described in the Appendix. The check scripts, their records, and the
Lean modules are submitted as supplementary material. Also submitted, as
optional supplementary reading, is a short note that relates the results
to a broader programme on observer-dependent descriptions and reports a
registered empirical campaign. Its measurements use a classifier
cross-entropy proxy for leakage and are reported as evidence about the
predictive usefulness of the Gaussian model, not as measurements of
conditional entropy. The manuscript does not depend on it.

The use of AI tools in preparing the manuscript is disclosed in its
Acknowledgment, as IEEE policy requires. This manuscript is not under
review elsewhere, and I have no conflicts of interest to declare.
