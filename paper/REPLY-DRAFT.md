Subject: Re: Manuscript IT-26-0996

Dear Dr. Tan,

Thank you for the decision, and particularly for the specific technical
comments — a desk rejection with that level of detail is more useful than most
full reviews.

The criticisms are correct and I am not writing to contest them. Definition 1
overloads $X$ for three distinct objects (the alphabet, the random vector, and
a set of vectors in the running example), takes an expectation before any
probability space is introduced — the measure-theoretic setting does not appear
until the coding appendix — and does not state the smoothness of $D_Y$ that the
second-order expansion it precedes actually requires. The abstract also
introduces private vocabulary for an object that already has a name: the
distortion is a composite (indirect/remote) distortion measure in the sense of
Dobrushin–Tsybakov and Wolf–Ziv, and it should have been written that way from
the first line.

On scope I take your point. Reading the submission again, the
information-theoretic content — the achievability and converse for the
composite distortion, a single-letter theorem for the true nonlinear
divergence, the complete two-stage rate region with successive refinability
characterised by nesting of the optimal error covariances, and the rate penalty
for a misidentified observer — sits in the last three sections and the
appendices, while the empirical material leads. That was the wrong order for
your readership.

One question, if you have a moment for it. Would a restructured manuscript —
the coding theorems as the body, standard notation and a measure-theoretic
setup from the outset, the empirical program removed to a separate paper — be
within scope for the Transactions? Or would you point that work toward JSAIT
instead? I would rather ask now than resubmit into the same mismatch.

Either way, thank you for the time you and the reviewers spent on it.
