\title{A Landauer Test for Consumer Complementarity:\\
Rate and Reset-Work Floors for Serving Two Read Operators}
\author{Andrew H. Bond}
\date{Working draft v0.4 --- August 2026}
\begin{document}\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Two consumers read the same Gaussian source through different rank-one read
operators. We ask what a description that serves both must cost, in bits
materialized and in ideal Landauer work at reset, beyond what the more
expensive consumer costs alone --- the \emph{complementarity tax}. The rate
side, restricted to the read plane, is classical and we claim no novelty for
it: the product bound $D_A D_B \ge \kappa\,2^{-2R}$, with
$\kappa=(u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x u)(v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x v)(1-\rho_{AB}^2)$
and $\rho_{AB}$ the source correlation of the two reads, is the Gaussian
evaluation of Gray's conditional rate--distortion lower bound
\cite{gray1973}, and it is \emph{exactly} the joint rate--distortion
function precisely when $\mathrm{diag}(D_A,D_B)\preceq B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x
B$, $B=[u\;v]$ --- the low-distortion branch of the bivariate
individual-distortion problem \cite{xiaoluo2005,lapidothtinguely2010}, in
the semidefinite-condition form of the recent vector literature
\cite{stylianou2021,chen2026}. We repackage these facts consumer-relatively
--- two rank-one reads of a $d$-dimensional source, the pair's geometry
entering only through the incompatibility invariant
$\kappa=\det(B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_xB)$ --- and prove what our prior-art
sweeps did not find. First, the complementarity tax itself: on the
incompatibility-dominated regime it has a closed form, and no prior
isolation of the excess of simultaneous service over the more expensive
single consumer surfaced under any name. Second, and principally, the
thermodynamic half: the reset-work coordinate of the consumer-relative
Landauer region obeys the same product bound with $\Sigma_x$ replaced by
$\Sigma_{X\mid S}$, so the work floor is discounted from the rate floor by
exactly $I(u^{\mathsf T}X, v^{\mathsf T}X; S)$, the side information the
reset context holds about the read plane.
Erasure pays for consumer incompatibility only to the extent that the reset
context does not already resolve it. A worked two-consumer instance gives
exact tax values and an exact rate-tax/work-tax gap that grows to
$I(X;S)$-type quantities as distortion vanishes. These predictions have
been confirmed: a preregistered falsification harness passed every gate
(GO-P-2026-055), and the tax has been \emph{measured} on materialized
codebook records on two independent source families --- Gaussian and binary,
each with its own derived floor --- with mismatched reset contexts saving
nothing and the joint eraser allocating its budget to the record its
context cannot resolve (GO-P-2026-058/059, all gates passed).
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
$X\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_x)$ on $\mathbb R^d$, $\Sigma_x\succ0$, i.i.d.\
copies. Consumers $A$ and $B$ have read directions $u,v\in\mathbb R^d$,
linearly independent, with read operators $P_A=uu^{\mathsf T}$,
$P_B=vv^{\mathsf T}$ and distortions
$d_A(x,\hat x)=\bigl(u^{\mathsf T}(x-\hat x)\bigr)^2$,
$d_B(x,\hat x)=\bigl(v^{\mathsf T}(x-\hat x)\bigr)^2$ --- the rank-one case of
the consumer-relative quadratic distortion $d_{\mathcal C}(x,\hat x)=
(x-\hat x)^{\mathsf T}P_{\mathcal C}(x-\hat x)$ of Observation Theory
\cite{bond2026observation}. Write
\[
B:=[\,u\ \ v\,]\in\mathbb R^{d\times2},\qquad
Y:=B^{\mathsf T}X=\begin{pmatrix}u^{\mathsf T}X\\ v^{\mathsf T}X\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \Sigma_Y:=B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x B\succ0 .
\]
$Y$ is what the two consumers, jointly, can ever be judged on. Its covariance
carries the incompatibility of the pair: with
$\sigma_u^2=u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x u$, $\sigma_v^2=v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x v$,
and read correlation
$\rho_{AB}=u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_x v/(\sigma_u\sigma_v)$,
\begin{equation}
\kappa:=\det\Sigma_Y
=\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2\bigl(1-\rho_{AB}^2\bigr)\ >0,
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
the squared area of the parallelogram spanned by $\Sigma_x^{1/2}u$ and
$\Sigma_x^{1/2}v$ (strictly positive under the standing hypotheses
$\Sigma_x\succ0$, $u,v$ linearly independent). For $\Sigma_x=\sigma^2 I$ and
unit $u,v$ at angle $\theta$, $\kappa=\sigma^4\sin^2\theta$. As the
consumers align, $\kappa\to0$;
consumers whose reads are uncorrelated under the source have maximal
$\kappa$.

For the thermodynamic coordinate we adopt the model of the consumer-relative
Landauer paper \cite{bondlandauerV}: descriptions are stored in a memory
whose later reset may consult retained side information $S$, jointly
Gaussian with $X$, with the Markov structure $\hX-X-S$ (side information is
never available to the consumers). By the multi-consumer region
(\emph{Shared observation for several consumers}, \cite{bondlandauerV}), a
shared memory serving reproductions
% [VI-10 sharpening] That theorem is proved in the source for finite
% alphabets and bounded distortion; its Gaussian/quadratic use here is the
% standard abstract-alphabet extension, inherited exactly as the source's own
% Gaussian sections inherit it, and flagged rather than re-proved. Every
% inequality proved in the present note is alphabet-agnostic.
$(\hX_A,\hX_B)$ at distortions $(D_A,D_B)$ has achievable rate--work pairs
characterized by
\begin{equation}
R\ \ge\ I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B),
\qquad
L\ \ge\ I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B\mid S),
\label{eq:coords}
\end{equation}
over joint test channels meeting both distortion constraints; the ideal
isothermal reset work is $W^\star=\kb T\ln2\cdot L$ with $L$ in bits. All
logarithms are base 2. Define the conditional analogues
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{X\mid S}:=\E\bigl[\Cov(X\mid S)\bigr],\qquad
\kappa_S:=\det\bigl(B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{X\mid S}B\bigr)
=\det\Sigma_{Y\mid S}.
\label{eq:kappaS}
\end{equation}
Since $(X,S)$ is jointly Gaussian, $\Sigma_{X\mid S}$ is the (deterministic)
conditional covariance and $\Sigma_{Y\mid S}=B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{X\mid S}B$.

\begin{definition}[Joint and single-consumer optima]\label{def:optima}
For $0<D_A\le\sigma_u^2$, $0<D_B\le\sigma_v^2$,
\begin{align*}
R_{AB}(D_A,D_B)&:=\min\bigl\{I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B):\E d_A(X,\hX_A)\le D_A,\
\E d_B(X,\hX_B)\le D_B\bigr\},\\
L_{AB}(D_A,D_B\mid S)&:=\min\bigl\{I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B\mid S):\text{same
constraints},\ (\hX_A,\hX_B)-X-S\bigr\},
\end{align*}
and $R_A(D_A),L_A(D_A\mid S)$ (resp.\ $B$) the same programs with only one
constraint. The single-consumer rate is classical rank-one reverse
water-filling (\emph{Isothermal read-operator work law},
\cite{bondlandauerV}):
$R_A(D_A)=\tfrac12\log(\sigma_u^2/D_A)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Complementarity tax]\label{def:tax}
\[
\mathrm{CT}_R(D_A,D_B):=R_{AB}(D_A,D_B)-\max\bigl(R_A(D_A),R_B(D_B)\bigr),
\quad
\mathrm{CT}_W(D_A,D_B\mid S):=L_{AB}-\max\bigl(L_A,L_B\bigr).
\]
Both are $\ge0$ trivially (a joint code contains a code for each consumer).
The content of this note is \emph{how large} they must be, and how retained
side information separates them.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Common reconstruction is without loss for these programs]
\label{rem:common}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:cm} below, replacing $(\hX_A,\hX_B)$ by the single
reproduction $\hX=\E[X\mid\hX_A,\hX_B]$ weakly improves both distortions
while $I(X;\hX)\le I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B)$ (and conditionally on $S$ likewise,
since $\hX$ is a function of the pair); the improvement step uses the
equality clause of Lemma~\ref{lem:cm}. Hence $R_{AB}$ equals the
simultaneous-service value $R(D_1,D_2)$ of
\cite[\emph{Simultaneous service}, \S7]{bondtwoobserver}: the max-det
program
$\max\{\log\det\Sigma:\ \Sigma\preceq\Sigma_x,\ \tr(P_A\Sigma)\le D_A,\
\tr(P_B\Sigma)\le D_B\}$. Everything exact below is therefore checkable
against an SDP.
\end{remark}

\section{Three lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[Conditional mean serves every observer
{\cite[Lem.~1]{bondtwoobserver}}]\label{lem:cm}
For any $P\succeq0$, any random variable $V$ jointly distributed with $X$,
and any estimator $g(V)$,
$\E[(X-g(V))^{\mathsf T}P(X-g(V))]\ \ge\
\E[(X-\E[X\mid V])^{\mathsf T}P(X-\E[X\mid V])]\ =\
\tr\bigl(P\,\Sigma(V)\bigr)$, where
$\Sigma(V):=\E[\Cov(X\mid V)]$; the conditional mean attains the bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Plane information bound]\label{lem:plane}
Let $V$ be any random variable jointly distributed with $X$, and
$Y=B^{\mathsf T}X$ as above. Then
\[
I(X;V)\ \ge\ I(Y;V)\ \ge\
\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_Y-\tfrac12\log\det\bigl(B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(V)B\bigr).
\]
If moreover $(X,S)$ is jointly Gaussian --- no Markov structure between $V$
and $S$ is required ---
\[
I(X;V\mid S)\ \ge\ I(Y;V\mid S)\ \ge\
\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_{Y\mid S}
-\tfrac12\log\det\bigl(\E[\Cov(Y\mid V,S)]\bigr),
\]
and $\E[\Cov(Y\mid V,S)]\preceq \E[\Cov(Y\mid V)]=B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(V)B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$Y$ is a deterministic function of $X$, so
$I(X;V)=I(Y;V)+I(X;V\mid Y)\ge I(Y;V)$, and identically with all mutual
informations conditioned on $S$. For the entropy bound,
$I(Y;V)=h(Y)-h(Y\mid V)$ with $h(Y)=\tfrac12\log\bigl[(2\pi
e)^2\det\Sigma_Y\bigr]$ exactly ($Y$ Gaussian). For each $v$,
$h(Y\mid V=v)\le\tfrac12\log\bigl[(2\pi e)^2\det\Cov(Y\mid V=v)\bigr]$
(Gaussian entropy maximization); averaging over $V$ and applying Jensen to
the concave map $\Sigma\mapsto\log\det\Sigma$ on the positive semidefinite
cone gives
$h(Y\mid V)\le\tfrac12\log\bigl[(2\pi e)^2\det\E[\Cov(Y\mid V)]\bigr]$.
Finally $\E[\Cov(Y\mid V)]=B^{\mathsf T}\E[\Cov(X\mid V)]B$. The conditional
case is identical with $(V,S)$ in place of $V$ and
$h(Y\mid S)=\tfrac12\log[(2\pi e)^2\det\Sigma_{Y\mid S}]$ exact because
$(Y,S)$ is jointly Gaussian with constant conditional covariance. The
ordering $\E[\Cov(Y\mid V,S)]\preceq\E[\Cov(Y\mid V)]$ is refined
conditioning (law of total covariance), and $\det$ is monotone on the
positive semidefinite order.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Read-frame Hadamard]\label{lem:hadamard}
Let $\Sigma_W\in\mathbb S^2_{+}$ have $(\Sigma_W)_{11}\le D_A$,
$(\Sigma_W)_{22}\le D_B$. Then $\det\Sigma_W\le D_A D_B$, with equality iff
$\Sigma_W=\mathrm{diag}(D_A,D_B)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\det\Sigma_W=(\Sigma_W)_{11}(\Sigma_W)_{22}-(\Sigma_W)_{12}^2
\le(\Sigma_W)_{11}(\Sigma_W)_{22}\le D_AD_B$; equality forces
$(\Sigma_W)_{12}=0$ and both diagonal entries at their caps.
\end{proof}

\section{The rate relation}
The results of this section are, in the reduced coordinates $Y=B^{\mathsf
T}X$, known: Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} is the Gaussian evaluation of Gray's
conditional rate--distortion lower bound \cite{gray1973} (equivalently, the
vector Shannon lower bound), and Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} is the
low-distortion branch of the joint rate--distortion function of a bivariate
Gaussian under individual square-error criteria, solved by Xiao and Luo
\cite[Thm.~6]{xiaoluo2005}, re-derived with explicit regime boundaries by
Lapidoth and Tinguely \cite[Thm.~III.1]{lapidothtinguely2010}, and stated as
a semidefinite condition in the vector case
\cite{stylianou2021,chen2026}. We state and prove them in the
consumer-relative form --- the source is $d$-dimensional and only the two
rank-one functionals are judged, with the reduction carried by
Lemma~\ref{lem:plane} and the geometry by $\kappa$ --- both for completeness
and because the work-side results of Section~4, which we did not find in
prior literature, reuse every step. See Section~7 for the full delineation.

\begin{theorem}[Consumer complementarity relation; classical after
reduction]\label{thm:rate}
Let $(\hX_A,\hX_B)$ be jointly distributed with $X$ and satisfy
$\E d_A(X,\hX_A)\le D_A$, $\E d_B(X,\hX_B)\le D_B$. Then
\begin{equation}
I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B)\ \ge\ \tfrac12\log\frac{\kappa}{D_AD_B},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
D_A\,D_B\ \ge\ \kappa\;2^{-2I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B)} .
\label{eq:ratebound}
\end{equation}
In particular $R_{AB}(D_A,D_B)\ge\tfrac12\log^+\!\bigl(\kappa/(D_AD_B)\bigr)$
and, in normalized precisions,
$\dfrac{D_A}{\sigma_u^2}\cdot\dfrac{D_B}{\sigma_v^2}\ \ge\
\bigl(1-\rho_{AB}^2\bigr)\,2^{-2R}$ at any achievable rate $R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $V=(\hX_A,\hX_B)$ and $\Sigma_W:=B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(V)B$. The estimator
$g(V)=\hX_A$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:cm} with $P=P_A$ give
$(\Sigma_W)_{11}=u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(V)u\le\E d_A\le D_A$; likewise
$(\Sigma_W)_{22}\le D_B$. Lemma~\ref{lem:plane} gives
$I(X;V)\ge\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_Y-\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_W$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:hadamard} gives $\det\Sigma_W\le D_AD_B$. Combine with
$\det\Sigma_Y=\kappa$.
\end{proof}

The bound is not merely an envelope; on a named regime it is the exact
answer --- in the reduced coordinates, the known low-distortion branch
\cite{xiaoluo2005,lapidothtinguely2010,stylianou2021}.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness on the incompatibility-dominated regime;
transport of \cite{xiaoluo2005,lapidothtinguely2010,stylianou2021}]
\label{prop:exact}
Let $\Delta:=\mathrm{diag}(D_A,D_B)$ with $0<D_A\le\sigma_u^2$,
$0<D_B\le\sigma_v^2$. Then
\[
R_{AB}(D_A,D_B)=\tfrac12\log\frac{\kappa}{D_AD_B}
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\Delta\ \preceq\ \Sigma_Y .
\]
For $\Sigma_x=\sigma^2I$, unit reads at angle $\theta\in(0,\pi)$, and
$D_A=D_B=D$, the condition reads $D\le\sigma^2(1-\lvert\cos\theta\rvert)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$, achievability.) Suppose $\Delta\preceq\Sigma_Y$. Take the
backward channel on the read plane: $\hY$ jointly Gaussian with
$\Cov(\hY)=\Sigma_Y-\Delta\succeq0$ and $Y=\hY+Z$,
$Z\sim\mathcal N(0,\Delta)$ independent of $\hY$, generated from $Y$ by a
test channel (so $\hY-Y-X$ is Markov). Set the common reproduction
$\hX:=B(B^{\mathsf T}B)^{-1}\hY$, so that $B^{\mathsf T}\hX=\hY$ exactly.
Then $u^{\mathsf T}(X-\hX)=e_1^{\mathsf T}(Y-\hY)=Z_1$, so
$\E d_A=\Delta_{11}=D_A$, and likewise $\E d_B=D_B$. The rate is
$I(X;\hX)\le I(X;\hY)=I(Y;\hY)=h(Y)-h(Z)
=\tfrac12\log\bigl(\det\Sigma_Y/\det\Delta\bigr)
=\tfrac12\log\bigl(\kappa/(D_AD_B)\bigr)$, using the Markov chain and the
fact that $Y-\hY=Z$ is Gaussian and independent of $\hY$. With
Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} this pins $R_{AB}$ at the stated value.

($\Rightarrow$, necessity.) The minimum defining $R_{AB}$ is attained: by
Remark~\ref{rem:common} it equals the max-det program's value, whose optimum
exists (compact feasible set, upper semicontinuous objective) and is realized
by the Gaussian backward channel. So let an admissible $V=(\hX_A,\hX_B)$
attain
$I(X;V)=\tfrac12\log(\kappa/(D_AD_B))$; then every inequality in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} is tight; in particular Lemma~\ref{lem:hadamard} is
tight, so $B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(V)B=\Delta$. But
$\E[\Cov(Y\mid V)]=\Sigma_Y-\Cov\bigl(\E[Y\mid V]\bigr)\preceq\Sigma_Y$
always, hence $\Delta\preceq\Sigma_Y$. For the isotropic instance,
$\Sigma_Y=\sigma^2\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\cos\theta\\
\cos\theta&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ has eigenvalues
$\sigma^2(1\pm\cos\theta)$, so
$\Sigma_Y-DI\succeq0$ iff $D\le\sigma^2(1-\lvert\cos\theta\rvert)$
--- symmetric in $\theta\mapsto\pi-\theta$, as it must be since $v\mapsto-v$
changes neither consumer.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Outside the regime, $R_{AB}$ is still exactly computable as the
simultaneous-service max-det program (Remark~\ref{rem:common});
\eqref{eq:ratebound} remains a valid, now strict, floor. The VI-10
verification nets found the floor active and exact at
$\theta\in\{30^\circ,\dots,90^\circ\}$, $D=0.1$, $\sigma=1$; inactive at
$\theta=15^\circ$ where $D>\sigma^2(1-\cos\theta)$; strictly loose at the
obtuse probe $\theta=120^\circ$, $D=0.7$ (gap $+0.061$ bits), which is what
forced the $\lvert\cos\theta\rvert$ in the statement; and switching exactly
at a bracket around the boundary $\theta^\star=\arccos(1-D/\sigma^2)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Complementarity tax, closed form]\label{cor:taxrate}
On the regime $\Delta\preceq\Sigma_Y$, with $M\in\{A,B\}$ the consumer of
larger single-rate and $m$ the other,
\[
\mathrm{CT}_R(D_A,D_B)
=\tfrac12\log\frac{\kappa}{\sigma_M^2\,D_m}
=\tfrac12\log\frac{\sigma_m^2\bigl(1-\rho_{AB}^2\bigr)}{D_m}
\quad(\text{exact}),
\]
which is positive iff $D_m<\sigma_m^2(1-\rho_{AB}^2)$. For the isotropic
equal-distortion instance,
$\mathrm{CT}_R=\tfrac12\log\bigl(\sigma^2\sin^2\theta/D\bigr)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Subtract $\max_j R_j=\tfrac12\log(\sigma_M^2/D_M)$ from
Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} and use
$\kappa=\sigma_u^2\sigma_v^2(1-\rho_{AB}^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Recovery of the orthogonal-observers extreme]
At $\theta=\pi/2$, $\Sigma_x=\sigma^2I_2$:
$\mathrm{CT}_R=\tfrac12\log(\sigma^2/D)=R_m$, i.e.\
$R_{AB}=R_A+R_B$ --- zero reuse. This is the \emph{value} of the
orthogonal-observers example of \cite[\S6]{bondtwoobserver} recovered from
the product bound (that example is stated for successive refinement, where
the minimal two-stage total is likewise $R_1(D_1)+R_2(D_2)$; the
simultaneous-service optimum coincides there, $\Sigma^{\mathrm s}=
\mathrm{diag}(D_1,D_2)$). The present corollary interpolates the extreme
continuously in $\theta$.
\end{remark}

\section{The work relation and the side-information discount}
\begin{theorem}[Complementarity relation at reset]\label{thm:work}
Let $(X,S)$ be jointly Gaussian, let $(\hX_A,\hX_B)-X-S$ be Markov, and let
the pair satisfy the two distortion constraints. Then
\begin{equation}
I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B\mid S)\ \ge\ \tfrac12\log\frac{\kappa_S}{D_AD_B},
\qquad
D_A\,D_B\ \ge\ \kappa_S\;2^{-2I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B\mid S)} .
\label{eq:workbound}
\end{equation}
Hence $L_{AB}(D_A,D_B\mid S)\ge\tfrac12\log^+\!\bigl(\kappa_S/(D_AD_B)\bigr)$
and the ideal reset work of any admissible shared memory obeys
$W^\star\ \ge\ \kb T\ln2\cdot\tfrac12\log^+\!\bigl(\kappa_S/(D_AD_B)\bigr)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Identical to Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} via the conditional half of
Lemma~\ref{lem:plane}: with $V=(\hX_A,\hX_B)$,
$I(X;V\mid S)\ge\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma_{Y\mid S}
-\tfrac12\log\det\E[\Cov(Y\mid V,S)]$, and
$\det\E[\Cov(Y\mid V,S)]\le\det\bigl(B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(V)B\bigr)\le
D_AD_B$ by the ordering in Lemma~\ref{lem:plane} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:hadamard}. Note the distortion constraints bound the
\emph{unconditional} error covariance --- the consumers never see $S$ ---
while the erasure mechanism does; this asymmetry is exactly the model of
\cite{bondlandauerV}. The Markov hypothesis is not used in the derivation
--- inequality \eqref{eq:workbound} holds for arbitrary joints of
$(X,\hX_A,\hX_B,S)$ with $(X,S)$ jointly Gaussian; it scopes the model in
which $I(X;\hX_A,\hX_B\mid S)$ \emph{is} the operational reset-work
coordinate \eqref{eq:coords}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The discount is the plane side information]
\label{cor:discount}
The rate floor and the work floor of
\eqref{eq:ratebound},\,\eqref{eq:workbound} differ by
\[
\tfrac12\log\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_S}
=\tfrac12\log\frac{\det\Sigma_Y}{\det\Sigma_{Y\mid S}}
=I(Y;S)
=I\bigl(u^{\mathsf T}X,\,v^{\mathsf T}X;\;S\bigr),
\]
exactly, for jointly Gaussian $(X,S)$. Consequently: (i) $\kappa_S\le\kappa$
always --- erasure never pays more incompatibility than materialization;
(ii) if $S$ determines some direction in $\mathrm{span}\{u,v\}$ (e.g.\ $S$
retains one consumer's read exactly), then $\kappa_S=0$ and the work floor
is vacuous: the reset context has already resolved the read-plane
incompatibility; (iii) if $S$ is independent of $Y$ (a mismatched or
shuffled reset context), $\kappa_S=\kappa$ and the two floors coincide ---
a useless context discounts nothing.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$I(Y;S)=h(Y)-h(Y\mid S)$ with both entropies Gaussian-exact. (i) is
$\Sigma_{Y\mid S}\preceq\Sigma_Y$ and monotonicity of $\det$; (ii)--(iii)
are the degenerate and independent cases of the same identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Tightness of the work floor]\label{rem:worktight}
Unlike Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}, the floor \eqref{eq:workbound} is generally
not attained at $D>0$: the consumer cannot condition on $S$, so its test
channel leaves residual correlation with $S$ that the erasure integral
counts. This is the same $D>0$ gap exhibited by the scalar Gaussian corner
(\emph{Scalar Gaussian corner}, \cite{bondlandauerV}), whose work coordinate
exceeds
$\tfrac12\log(\sigma^2_{X\mid S}/D)$ by exactly the discount shortfall
$\tfrac12\log\bigl[(\sigma^2_{X\mid S}+(\sigma^2-\sigma^2_{X\mid
S})D/\sigma^2)/\sigma^2_{X\mid S}\bigr]$, vanishing as $D\to0$. The worked
instance below inherits this: the work floor is asymptotically tight as
$D\to0$, and the \emph{exact} finite-$D$ work tax is computed there by
per-coordinate reduction. Characterizing the exact two-consumer
rate--work--distortion region with side information (the $S$-analogue of
the simultaneous-service SDP) is open and flagged for the sequel.
\end{remark}

\section{Worked instance: the GO-10 targets}\label{sec:instance}
Let $\Sigma_x=I_2$, $u=e_1$, $v=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$, equal distortions
$D_A=D_B=D$, and reset side information $S=u^{\mathsf T}X+N$,
$N\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau^2)$ independent of $(X,\hX_A,\hX_B)$. Write
$s^2:=\Var(X_1\mid S)=\tau^2/(1+\tau^2)$.

\paragraph{Rate side ($\theta$-sweep).} $\kappa=\sin^2\theta$. For
$D\le1-\lvert\cos\theta\rvert$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:exact}; all sweep
angles below are acute),
\[
R_{AB}=\tfrac12\log\frac{\sin^2\theta}{D^2},
\qquad
\mathrm{CT}_R=\tfrac12\log\frac{\sin^2\theta}{D}\quad(\text{exact}).
\]
At $D=0.1$: $\mathrm{CT}_R=0.661,\ 1.161,\ 1.453,\ 1.611,\ 1.661$ bits at
$\theta=30^\circ,45^\circ,60^\circ,75^\circ,90^\circ$; at $\theta=15^\circ$
the regime condition fails ($1-\cos15^\circ\approx0.034<0.1$) and the exact
value is the SDP's ($\mathrm{CT}_R\approx0.122$ bits), strictly above the
now-inactive floor. Aligned consumers, $\theta\to0$: tax $\to0$.

\paragraph{Work side ($\theta=90^\circ$, $\tau$-sweep).} Here the two source
coordinates are independent, $S$ is correlated with $X_1$ only, and the
distortion functionals separate, so both joint programs split
per-coordinate (converse: condition coordinate-wise and apply the scalar
corner converse of \cite{bondlandauerV} to
$(X_1,u^{\mathsf T}\hX_A,S)$, which is Markov-compatible because $S$ depends
on $X$ only through $X_1$; achievability: independent per-coordinate reverse
channels). With all quantities in bits:
\[
L_A=\tfrac12\log\frac{s^2+(1-s^2)D}{D},\qquad
L_B=\tfrac12\log\frac1D,\qquad
L_{AB}=L_A+L_B,
\]
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CT}_W=L_A
=\tfrac12\log\frac{s^2+(1-s^2)D}{D},
\qquad
\mathrm{CT}_R-\mathrm{CT}_W
=\tfrac12\log\frac{1}{s^2+(1-s^2)D}
\ \nearrow\ I(X_1;S)\ \text{as }D\to0 .
\label{eq:taxgap}
\end{equation}
The tax gap \eqref{eq:taxgap} is exact at every $D$, monotone in the
staleness/quality of $S$, equals $0$ when $S$ is useless ($s^2=1$), and
approaches the full retained information $I(X_1;S)=\tfrac12\log(1/s^2)$ as
$D\to0$ --- the two-consumer face of the Paper~V separation between what a
consumer must be sent and what a reset must destroy. Representative exact
values ($s^2=0.2$): $\mathrm{CT}_R-\mathrm{CT}_W=0.661,\ 1.029,\ 1.147$
bits at $D=0.25,\ 0.05,\ 0.005$, against $I(X_1;S)=1.161$.

\paragraph{Controls.} (i) Mismatched reset context (an independent copy
$S'$): $\kappa_{S'}=\kappa$, discount $=0$, $\mathrm{CT}_W=\mathrm{CT}_R$
--- Corollary~\ref{cor:discount}(iii). (ii) $S$ aligned with the read plane
but orthogonal to both reads is impossible for $d=2$; for $d\ge3$, $S$
correlated only with $\ker B^{\mathsf T}$ directions discounts nothing ---
same control, geometric version.

\section{Interpretation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rate} has the shape, though not the substance, of an
uncertainty relation: the product of two achievable precisions is floored by
$2^{-2R}$ times an incompatibility functional of the two observables ---
here $1-\rho_{AB}^2$, the fraction of either read's variance that the other
read cannot linearly explain. The resource is description rate rather than
$\hbar$, and the obstruction is statistical (the source cannot be
simultaneously well-resolved along non-collinear directions with too few
bits) rather than operator noncommutativity. We therefore call it a
\emph{complementarity relation} and claim no quantum content. The analogy
has a precise nearest neighbor: Hall's information exclusion principle
\cite{hall1995} bounds $I_X+I_P\le\log_2(\Delta X\,\Delta P/\hbar)$ ---
rearranged, a product of spreads floored by a scale times an exponential of
information gained --- and the memory-assisted entropic uncertainty
relations \cite{berta2010}, in which side information relaxes the floor
through a conditional entropy, parallel the discount $\kappa\to\kappa_S$
below. But every relation in that literature is a sum of entropies or
accessible informations against quantum overlap constants; our sweeps found
no rate-constrained exclusion relation, no rate--distortion form, and no
product-of-distortions floor there. The Fisher-information route to
classical uncertainty (Stam; Dembo--Cover--Thomas) involves no coding rate,
and the one classical compression ``uncertainty principle'' we found
\cite{safaryan2021} concerns a single compressed vector with no
incompatibility functional. The substance of \eqref{eq:ratebound} is,
rather, classical source coding (Section~7); what the uncertainty shape adds
is the reading of $\kappa$ as an incompatibility functional and the
observation that the same shape survives at reset with a side-information
discount --- which is where the prior literature ends.
Theorem~\ref{thm:work} is the thermodynamic half: at reset, the same product
bound holds against the conditional incompatibility $\kappa_S$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:discount} prices the difference at exactly the side
information the reset context holds about the read plane. Materialization
pays for consumer incompatibility against the prior; erasure pays only for
the part the reset context has not already resolved.

\section{Delineation}\label{sec:delineation}
The prior-art sweeps flagged in earlier drafts were executed on 2026-08-03
(four independent searches; record at
\texttt{paper/complementarity-tax-NOVELTY.md}). Their findings are folded
in below; ``no prior found'' always means no prior statement found under the
recorded queries, not provable novelty.

\textbf{Gaussian coding under individual fidelity criteria (the governing
prior line).} Restricted to the read plane, Section~3 is not new.
Inequality \eqref{eq:ratebound} is Gray's conditional rate--distortion lower
bound \cite{gray1973}, $R_{X_1X_2}(D_1,D_2)\ge
R_{X_1}(D_1)+R_{X_2}(D_2)-I(X_1;X_2)$, evaluated for a bivariate Gaussian
(equivalently, the vector Shannon lower bound). The exactness
formula-plus-regime of Proposition~\ref{prop:exact} is the low-distortion
branch of the joint rate--distortion function under individual square-error
criteria: closed form by Xiao and Luo \cite[Thm.~6]{xiaoluo2005} (their
scalar regime condition $\rho^2\le(1-D_1)(1-D_2)$ is
$\Delta\preceq\Sigma_Y$ written in scalars), explicit three-regime display
by Lapidoth and Tinguely \cite[Thm.~III.1]{lapidothtinguely2010},
matrix-ordering (``semidefinite condition'') statement, max-det program,
and multivariate extension by Stylianou, Charalambous, and Charalambous
\cite{stylianou2021}, with the package treated as known and the
condition-violated regime analyzed by Chen et al.\ \cite{chen2026}; see
also \cite{nayak2008} for the restatement with the backward-channel
tightness structure. What we did not find stated in that line: the
$d$-dimensional two-rank-one-read formulation with
$\kappa=\det(B^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_xB)$ as the invariant (the reduction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:plane} is folklore for a single projection); the tax
$\mathrm{CT}_R=R_{AB}-\max(R_A,R_B)$ under any name --- the nearest
quantity, the rate penalty of universal representations \cite{nguyen2025},
prices one-at-a-time universality rather than simultaneous constraints and
vanishes in its Gaussian setting; and anything thermodynamic.

\textbf{Two-observer theorem \cite{bondtwoobserver}.} The
simultaneous-service proposition there (\S7) characterizes $R_{AB}$ as a
max-det SDP for arbitrary PSD read operators; the orthogonal-observers
example (\S6) computes the extreme value; neither contains the closed-form
floor or the regime characterization, which --- as established above ---
were already in \cite{xiaoluo2005,lapidothtinguely2010,stylianou2021}.
Relative to both, the contributions of this note are the tax quantity and
its closed form (Corollary~\ref{cor:taxrate}) and the entire work side
(Section~4). \textbf{Consumer-relative Landauer \cite{bondlandauerV}.} The
multi-consumer theorem supplies the coordinates \eqref{eq:coords}; the
coordinated-reset corollary (conditional total correlation) prices
resetting \emph{several existing records} jointly versus separately --- the
GO-9 face. The tax is a different comparison: one record serving two
consumers versus the more expensive consumer alone. Both reduce to
conditional-information accounting, but neither implies the other.
\textbf{Common reconstruction (Steinberg).} Supplies the work-only endpoint
semantics in \cite{bondlandauerV}; not a two-consumer statement.
\textbf{Multiple descriptions (El Gamal--Cover).} Distinct problem: there,
two \emph{channels} may fail independently and every subset of descriptions
must decode; here one shared memory serves two \emph{distortion
functionals}. No degraded-message structure is imposed.

\textbf{Thermodynamics of erasure with side information.} The conditional
coordinate belongs to the conditional-erasure tradition in which retained
context discounts Landauer work: classically Sagawa--Ueda, quantumly del
Rio et al.\ \cite{delrio2011} and, most sharply, Berta, Brand\~ao, Majenz,
and Wilde \cite{bbmw2018}, where conditional mutual information exactly
prices decoupling given a retained system. All price the erasure of one
record against one knowledge context; the sweep found no two-consumer or
two-observable erasure floor, no product bound of the form
\eqref{eq:workbound}, and no discount identity of the form
Corollary~\ref{cor:discount}. At the level of floors, the discount is a
two-consumer Gaussian instance of the gap between rate--distortion and
conditional rate--distortion functions \cite{gray1973}; its content here is
the operational asymmetry --- the reset mechanism holds $S$, the consumers
never see it --- which turns Gray's coding gap into a statement about what
erasure, and only erasure, gets to discount. Finally, despite the name,
Theorem~\ref{thm:work} is \emph{not} a thermodynamic uncertainty relation
in the Barato--Seifert sense \cite{baratoseifert2015}: TURs bound
fluctuations of time-integrated currents by entropy production in
nonequilibrium steady states; our relation bounds a product of mean-square
distortions by conditional information at an ideal isothermal reset. One
manual follow-up remains open: Kastner and Schlatter (Mathematics 12:206,
2024) pair ``complementarity'' with ``erasure cost'' in a quantum
foundational register; the accessible material indicates a qualitative
argument with no quantitative floor, but the full text resisted retrieval
and should be read before submission.
