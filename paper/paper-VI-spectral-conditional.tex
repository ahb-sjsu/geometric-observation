\begin{abstract}
Gray's conditional rate--distortion function --- side information at
both encoder and decoder --- has been known in scalar and
finite-vector Gaussian forms since 1972, yet its spectral form for
jointly stationary Gaussian pair processes has, to the best of a
four-sweep, three-library-check search, never been written down. We
supply it: at the work endpoint, $L(D)$ is the equal-slope allocation
of per-frequency quadratic-root values over the conditional spectrum,
recovering classical Kolmogorov--Pinsker reverse water-filling as the
uninformative-context limit; the weighted rate--work version carries
per-frequency two-water-level profiles under a single distortion
price. En route we prove that the staleness of the conditioning
context is an \emph{access-width} phenomenon, not a delay phenomenon
--- pure delay is information-free under path access (an exact
finite-$n$ recoding identity), while time-local access pays a closed
staleness tax whose substitution rule depends on the encoder's epoch
of access --- and that every observation access class enters through
one conditional variance, making the ideal reset work of a stored
record against an aging reference an exact Kalman-family curve
$W(\Delta)$. \todo{compress to 150 words for the venue.}
\end{abstract}
