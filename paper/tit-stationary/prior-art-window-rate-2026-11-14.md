# Prior art: finite-block (window) rate-distortion gap R_N(D) - R(D) for stationary Gaussian sources

Search date: 2026-11-14. Read-only literature search; no repository files touched.
Access labels used below: **[PRIMARY]** = I read the source text itself; **[ABSTRACT]** = abstract or bibliographic record only; **[SECONDARY]** = another paper's description of it; **[RECOLLECTION]** = from memory, unverified.

## Headline finding

**The draft's rate-only bound (ii) coincides with the classical Wyner-Ziv (1971) memory bound applied to N-blocks.**

Gray, *Source Coding Theory* (1990), Sec. 4.7.1, eqs. (4.7.3)-(4.7.4) [PRIMARY] proves, for a stationary source with a density and an additive distortion measure:

- R_k(D) >= R_1(D) - ( h(p_1) - (1/k) h(p_k) )   (4.7.3)
- R(D) >= R_1(D) - ( h(X_0) - hbar(X) )           (4.7.4)

with the remark that the subtracted term "depends only on the source and not on the distortion measure", and the attribution: "These bounds are originally due to Wyner and Ziv [83], but the proof is closer to Berger's [5]."

Apply (4.7.4) to the stationary process of consecutive N-blocks (super-letters in R^N, or R^{mN} for a vector source). Its first-order RDF is N R_N(D), its marginal entropy is h(X^N), its entropy rate is N hbar. Dividing by N:

  R_N(D) - R(D) <= (1/N) h(X^N) - hbar   for every D.

For a Gaussian source, (1/N) h(X^N) - hbar = (1/2N) [ ln det T_N(Sigma) - N (1/2pi) int ln det Sigma ] = s_N / (2N) nats = s_N / (2 N ln 2) bits. This is exactly the draft's bound, at every distortion level, with the same constant. Equality at small distortion also follows classically (the Shannon lower bound is tight for both R_N and R when D <= min Sigma, scalar case).

Caveats on this identification:
- The super-letter step is my own one-line derivation from (4.7.4). I did not find a source that writes the N-block form "R_N(D) - Delta_N <= R(D) <= R_N(D)" explicitly. I could not open Wyner-Ziv 1971 or Berger 1971 to check whether they state it. [RECOLLECTION, unverified: I believe the N-block form appears in one of them.]
- Gray's proof in Sec. 4.7.1 is written for scalar letters with a pdf; Sec. 4.7 says the variational results extend "in a straightforward manner to finite-dimensional vectors". The draft's weighted quadratic distortion on vector letters is an additive single-letter distortion on super-letters, so it should be covered, but that is my reading, not a quoted statement.
- The draft's proof route (feasible Toeplitz error covariance T_N(Delta*)) is different: constructive upper bound on R_N, versus a variational lower bound on R. I found no prior use of that construction. The method may be new; the rate-only inequality is not.

## (b) Verdict on the draft's claims

**Claim (i), R_N(D) >= R(D) for every N by subadditivity of N R_N(D): KNOWN.** Textbook fact. Gray, *Entropy and Information Theory*, Lemma 10.6.2 gives lim = inf for stationary sources on standard alphabets (so Gaussian sources are covered), stated for the distortion-rate function. Gallager 1968 Th. 9.8.1 is the usual citation for the rate form (confirmed only through Naiss-Permuter's description). The remark that convergence need not be monotone in N: I found no prior statement either way.

**Claim (ii), R_N(D) - R(D) <= s_N/(2 N ln 2) bits at every D: KNOWN IN SUBSTANCE (rate-only case).**
- The inequality is the Wyner-Ziv 1971 bound applied to N-blocks (see headline). The N = 1 Gaussian case is written out explicitly in Gibson 2017 eq. (24). The all-N form is immediate from Gray 1990 eq. (4.7.4) but I did not see it printed.
- "Equality for a scalar source at D <= min Sigma": follows from tightness of the Shannon lower bound on both sides; classical, though I did not find it phrased as a statement about R_N - R.
- "s_N nondecreasing, equals twice the block-past mutual information, bounded under a strong-Szego condition": standard. h(X^N) - N hbar = I(X^N; past) is elementary; the limit is the past-future information (Li-Xie 1996, Li 2006; Ibragimov's strong Szego theorem as surveyed by Bingham 2012). Note that the strong Szego condition in Bingham is stated on the cepstrum (sum n L_n^2 < inf, L = Fourier coefficients of log w), not on the covariances R_k as in the draft's "sum k \|R_k\|^2 < inf". The two should be equivalent for a spectrum bounded away from zero and infinity, but the draft should say which form it uses and cite accordingly. I did not find a matrix-valued (block Toeplitz) strong Szego statement in what I read.
- "Hence the gap is O(1/N) for Lipschitz spectra": partially known. O(1/n) at every distortion is proved for scalar AR(1) (Tian-Kostina 2019) and scalar finite-order AR (Tasci-Kostina 2026), with existential constants and by eigenvalue-distribution arguments. I found no O(1/N) statement for general (non-AR) spectra or for vector sources, and no explicit constant, other than what the Wyner-Ziv bound gives.
- What I did not find anywhere: the explicit sentence "R_N(D) - R(D) <= (block-past mutual information)/N for stationary Gaussian sources", the Toeplitz-error-covariance construction, or anything for the two-cost (rate plus conditional leakage) problem.

Recommendation: the draft should cite Wyner-Ziv 1971 (and Gray 1990 Sec. 4.7.1, or Berger 1971) for the rate-only bound and present its own contribution as (a) a constructive proof and (b) the extension to the two-cost problem. It should also cite Tasci-Kostina 2026 and Tian-Kostina 2019 for O(1/n) in the AR case. Someone with library access should open Wyner-Ziv 1971 (in particular its "Remark 4") and Berger 1971 Ch. 4 before submission to see whether the N-block form and the Gaussian evaluation are printed there.

**Finite-block convergence for Wyner-Ziv / conditional / secrecy Gaussian problems with memory: NOT FOUND.** Only asymptotic formulas via the Toeplitz distribution theorem (Gao-Soleymani 2010 at abstract level; Chen 2012 Appendix A). Nothing on rate-distortion-equivocation with memory at finite block length.

## (c) Best citable source for R_N(D) >= R(D)

1. Seen directly: R. M. Gray, *Entropy and Information Theory*, Sec. 10.6, Lemma 10.6.2 (numbering of the corrected first edition, online version of June 26, 2023, https://ee.stanford.edu/~gray/it.pdf). It is stated as D(R) = inf_N (1/N) D_N(R). The Springer 2nd edition (2011) numbers things differently; I did not check its numbering.
2. For the rate form: R. G. Gallager, *Information Theory and Reliable Communication*, Wiley, 1968, Theorem 9.8.1 with Lemma 4A.2. Location confirmed only through Naiss and Permuter 2013 (arXiv:1106.0895, Appendix A), which I read. Gallager's theorem is for discrete sources, so for a Gaussian source Gray's lemma is the safer citation.
